# The films' formal register

The standard that the films' owner made final on 20 November 2026 (「照此定稿」, 19:42:52 CST), as approved. The
register check (`filmkit.register`) takes its forms from these rules and the review round gives them to the
reviewers; neither restates them.

## Scope

Every text a viewer of a film hears or reads, in both editions: the narration, the captions and transcript, the
words in the picture, the title card and cover, the credits, the player page, and the release's description,
synopsis and comment.

## The order

1. **信** first, unchanged: every statement keeps its source's scope, subject, modality and qualifiers, and each
   concept keeps its approved rendering (`content/concepts.toml`). No register repair may narrow or widen a claim.
2. **达**: a first-time listener understands each sentence at one hearing, at the narration's pace: a clear
   subject, familiar collocations, no double negation, no abstract noun standing for a mechanism.
3. **雅**: formal standard written language (规范书面语), concise and exact. Plain does not mean colloquial: a
   sentence is plain when it is clear, not when it sounds like conversation.

## Rules of 雅 (formal register)

- **R1. The film does not address its audience.** No 你、你们、您、大家、咱们; in English no "you" or "your"
  addressing the viewer, the generic "you" included. Name the actual subject (调用智能体的人, 提交者, 验收人, the
  team that uses ETHOS) or use an impersonal construction.
- **R2. No inclusive first person.** 我们 / "we" only where it stands for a party the film has named (the data
  department in its incident story), and the named party is preferred; never a "we" that takes in the viewer or
  humanity at large (「越来越多的 Agent 在替我们改代码」).
- **R3. No colloquial words, particles or forms.** Chinese: 弄、搞、拿准/拿不准、跑 (for running a program: 运行)、
  看一眼、现查、其实、说白了、一下、吧、呢、啊、呀; spoken fillers and slang. English: no contractions in narration or
  text ("does not", not "doesn't"), no phrasal slang, no fillers.
- **R4. Questions only as the subject's own questions.** A question states, once, a question the film then
  answers; no rhetorical question whose answer is implied, and no string of questions in one thought where one
  statement carries the point.
- **R5. Complete, standard sentences.** No fragments for effect, no exclamations, no dashes, no ellipsis, no
  staged contrast, no one-line closer, no forced triad (the existing machine-tell rules stay).
- **R6. Terms and collocations of the field.** The established term of the domain (data, finance, software
  engineering), not an everyday or internet idiom (报表, 止损 for containment), and not computing slang.
