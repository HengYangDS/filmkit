# filmkit

filmkit rebuilds a narrated bilingual explainer film, a Chinese and an English edition, from the film's tracked sources. It narrates each edition's script with a local speech model, assembles one soundtrack per edition at a single program level, times the captions, renders the picture with HyperFrames once with its captions and once without, packages a UHD master and a 1080p viewing film of each with FFmpeg, and verifies every film against the inputs it binds. The build is offline after one supply step.

The engine carries no film's content. Each film's repository holds its scripts, casts, pronunciation contract, pages, review policy and picture composition, and the engine reads them from the film it builds. The [DDWG introduction films](https://github.com/HengYangDS/data-department-work-guidelines-film) are built with it.

## Requirements

- An Apple-silicon Mac: the speech models run on MLX, and the 1080p films are encoded with VideoToolbox.
- [Mise](https://mise.jdx.dev), which installs the film's locked Node, CPython and uv.
- FFmpeg with VideoToolbox, ExifTool and rsync 3, from Homebrew: `brew install ffmpeg exiftool rsync`.
- Network access for the supply step only. It installs the browser build that the renderer pins and the three speech models; `HF_ENDPOINT` names a Hugging Face mirror where the hub is unreachable.

## A film's layout

A film is the folder that holds `content/film.toml`. The engine builds the film that `FILMKIT_ROOT` names or, when it is unset, the nearest film at or above the working directory, and it finds the film's npm packages in the nearest `node_modules` at or above the film.

| Path                                                        | What it holds                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `content/film.toml`                                         | The declaration: the film's file prefix, editions, fonts, player colours and review policy  |
| `<sources>/film-script.json`                                | An edition's script, in the sources folder that the declaration names for it                |
| `<sources>/voice-reference.json`                            | The edition's cast: the narrator's reference recording, its text, the seeds and any retakes |
| `<sources>/narration.md`                                    | The edition's narration: its segment texts joined by blank lines                            |
| `picture/index.html`, `picture/film.js`, `picture/film.css` | The picture composition: the renderer document's template, its script and its style         |
| `content/release.json`                                      | The published release that `filmkit.delivery.reproduction` compares a rebuild with          |
| `build/`                                                    | Everything the engine writes; keep it out of version control                                |

Every other file is named by the declaration, a script or a cast: the entry page, the source manifest, the concept contract, the covers, the fonts, the pronunciation contract, the reviewer prompt, the listening-plan readings and the reference recordings.

Each script declares the edition's title, author, language, duration, frame rate, credits duration, chapters, release metadata, pause policy and pronunciation contract, and its segments, each split into utterance groups that end at sentence punctuation. Both editions declare the same duration, author and chapter count, at 60 fps. The engine refuses a declaration or a script that differs from this contract before any work, naming each failing field. [`examples/sample-film`](examples/sample-film) is a complete film to follow.

Every text a viewer reads outside the picture sets quotation marks as the captions do: the captions, subtitles and transcript, the transcript's title and headings, the player's transcript and chapters, the chapter metadata and the films' title, comment, description and synopsis tags set a straight double quote as an opening or closing typographic one and an apostrophe as a right single quote. Each delivered film tags its picture, narration and chapter tracks with the edition's language; packaging writes the chapter track's language in place, since FFmpeg tags that track English, and refuses a film any of whose tracks carries another.

A new film names its covers before they exist. It narrates and stages its picture without them; `filmkit.tools player-check cover <stage> <cover> <painting>` paints each edition's cover from the staged picture, reports a painting with no tracked cover as uncompared and fails, and the painting is then tracked as the cover. Packaging refuses an edition whose cover is missing, naming its field.

## Concepts and readings

A film can make its words checkable before anything is narrated. Narration runs both checks first, including a dry run, and refuses on either, naming every failure.

- **Concepts.** The concept contract that `concepts` names is a TOML file of `[[concept]]` entries. Each has an `id`, the source `term`, its `essence` in one sentence, a `source` with the `path` of a document the source manifest pins and a `quote` from it, and a `zh` and an `en` table with the edition's `rendering`, the other `forms` the narration may use and the counterexamples to `avoid`. A segment lists the ids it states in `concepts`, the same in both editions, and must say each by its rendering or a form. No counterexample may appear in the narration, the titles, the caption terms, the release's synopsis, comment and description, the summary each edition's player shows, or the text files below `picture/`. With `FILM_SOURCE_CHECKOUT` naming a clone of the source repository, every quotation must be in its document at the pinned commit. A Latin-script word keeps its base form in Chinese, so where the English counterpart of a Chinese thought uses such a word only in its plural, the Chinese clause that holds it must hold a number word (们, 些, 多, 几, 各, 每, 众, 两, 俩, 双, 它们, 他们, 一个个, or a numeral other than a lone 一 before its classifier and the word); a `[number]` table adds `words` and the generic uses it keeps, as `exceptions` of a `thought`, a `term` and a `reason`. `python -m filmkit.concepts` runs the check alone.
- **Readings.** A pronunciation contract of `schema_version` 2 must declare, as `contextual-mandarin-word` targets, the reading of every Chinese character on the engine's watch list wherever the narration says it. A character that reads differently in ordinary words needs a declared word of two or more characters around each occurrence; one that reads differently only inside known words may be declared alone. Each declared reading must be a dictionary reading per character, or after a word's first character that reading in the neutral tone; where the dictionary reads an occurrence otherwise in context, the word records why in `dictionary_disagreement`. Declarations the narration no longer says are refused. Every Latin word in the Chinese narration and every English word with two or more capitals needs a declared spoken form, and every language's normalization must give each `[[narration.terms]]` entry its declared `spoken` form: one spelling for the voice in every edition. `python -m filmkit.narration.readings` runs the check alone and writes each edition's occurrence plan to `build/evidence/readings/`, with the text around every occurrence, which the reviewer prompt names beside the contract. With `--propose` it instead writes `build/evidence/readings/zh-proposals.json`: a proposed word and the dictionary's reading for each occurrence nothing declares yet, for the author to correct and declare.

A review round's prompts name the concept contract and the occurrence plans. For a film that declares a contract, `filmkit.review.round prompts` refuses to write them unless the reviewer prompt cites `{concepts}` and `{readings}` and the review's dimensions include `concept_fidelity`, and names each omission.

A film that declares neither, or keeps a version 1 pronunciation contract, narrates as before.

## How the narration flows

Selection, assembly and the audition measure how each take and each soundtrack flows (`filmkit.narration.flow`). The heard pause at a word boundary is the longest run of 10 ms frames between the middles of its two aligned words whose level lies 24 dB or more below the speech median. A boundary whose text holds no pause mark (a quotation mark is none) joins its words inside a phrase and may hold at most `switch_limit_s` where Chinese characters and Latin script meet, where Chinese speech synthesis hesitates, and `unmarked_limit_s` elsewhere: 0.2 s and 0.4 s unless `[narration.flow]` declares others. Selection and assembly refuse every other boundary above its limit, naming its thought, its two words and the pause, and each declared exception that no boundary of its edition matches; the audition proposes the most fluent qualifying take. Between adjacent thoughts of a segment the measurement records the steps of speaking rate, median pitch and level for listening and never refuses one, since a question raises the pitch on purpose.

Each occurrence of a `[[narration.terms]]` term is measured by its first vowel: the median first and second formants of its voiced frames up to 32% of the way into its aligned word, from up to one aligner frame (80 ms) before the word's aligned start but never before the previous word's aligned end, compared with those of the reference occurrence a person heard correct. The pinned aligner places boundaries on 80 ms frames and may leave the first vowel of a word that begins with one unassigned after the word before it, as in “The Eethos mark”. An occurrence whose F1 exceeds 1.5 times the reference's or whose F2 falls below 0.85 times is said unlike it, as an open vowel raises F1 and lowers F2. Selection and assembly refuse it by name with both ratios, and the audition prefers a take whose occurrences pass. Because the narrator's own reference sets the standard, no vowel target needs a calibration per narrator, and because the declaration lives in `film.toml`, adding it changes no recording's fingerprint.

`filmkit.review.measurement` records each edition's boundaries by their marks, its continuity steps and its terms' vowels, and the listening plan names for human listening the unmarked pauses from 0.15 s up to their limits, those kept by an exception, the largest continuity steps and every term occurrence with its vowel.

## The picture gate

`filmkit.produce check-picture --language zh|en` audits an edition's current picture before it renders: the frame audit over every frame of its timeline at one thirtieth of a second, the sync audit of its labels against their words, and the motion audit at 30 frames per second. Besides layout, legibility and collisions, the frame audit follows the lit picture through all speech and reports each stretch of 2 s or more in which it stands more than 12% off the content centre or covers less than a tenth of the content band. The command writes `build/evidence/editions/<edition>/picture-gates.json`, which binds the composition, the timeline, the stylesheet, the declared fonts and the engine version and lists every gated finding, and refuses while one remains. `render-picture` refuses an edition without a passing record for its current composition, timeline, stylesheet, fonts and engine version, so a film's picture task runs `check-picture` before each render.

The headless audits measure text with the film's own fonts. `check-picture` reads each `@font-face` rule of `picture/film.css` whose source is one of the film's `picture.fonts`, records the advance width of every character each face maps in `build/runtime/work/picture-gates/text-metrics.json`, and names that table to the frame and motion audits in `FILMKIT_TEXT_METRICS`; `python -m filmkit.tools` names it to them too when the caller names none. A text measures the sum of its characters' advances at its size plus its letter spacing, as a canvas measures it without kerning. A family the stylesheet does not declare and a character its face does not map keep the estimate, and every text's height keeps the estimate.

In every frame, spoken or not, the frame audit also measures balance as the reviews since DDWG round 29 do: the box of every paint brighter than grey 60 at full size within rows 120–919, and its horizontal offset from x 960. The gate refuses `off_centre_builds`, each run of 1.5 s or more in which that box stands more than 90 px off. It lists `off_centre_settled` in the gate record without refusing it: each run of 2 s or more in which the box stands more than 50 px off while its edges keep still, unless the picture then grows from it. Whether such a state is a finished composition or a build-up waiting for its next part, only the stage's intent tells.

The frame audit also measures how text lines up, from the exact anchors, sizes and baselines of each voiced frame's settled names and marks and the widths of the film's fonts, and reports each defect that lasts 1 s or more per scene with its span, texts and offsets. The gate refuses all seven of these measures: `ragged_rows`, names of one size side by side on baselines more than 3.5 px apart; `ragged_columns`, the lines of a block or the items of a column off their shared edge, or a left-aligned block off the axis of the mark it stands under or over; `crammed_lines`, stacked lines with less than 7 px between their inks; `off_card_centre`, a name alone in a small card more than 4 px off its centre or middle; `uneven_pitch`, like marks at uneven pitch over names at equal gaps; `touching_marks`, a name within 3 px of a mark's stroke; and `mixed_case`, the names of one set (the `set` a composition registers with a name) or of one row of one size, anywhere across the frame, that begin in both cases. A name counts once by its whole text, and a name that opens with a quotation mark, begins with a digit or a letter without case, or whose first word holds a later capital, as an acronym does, is not judged.

The gate reads the sync audit's continuity too. It refuses each seat, a mark that stands in speech for more than 0.6 s before a name is legible at it, and each run of a thought's speech longer than 1 s with no legible name. It lists in the record, without refusing them, the thoughts that keep the previous picture for more than 1 s, since a deliberate index may stay while the first new name arrives. The seat check judges a dashed closed outline on its own even where a link joins it to a larger mark, and counts a frame as named by what it holds only when that is a text or a named mark, or when it holds a mark and a name stands within reach; a pictogram in a frame names nothing. The sync audit also compares each caption cue of three or more words with the labels: the gate refuses a cue whose words one label legible during the cue holds exactly, in order, and records a cue at least 80% of whose words labels drawn during it repeat, and a cue that is itself one quotation, since the narration then reads out what the picture shows. A composition registers a name's whole text, set and role with each text it paints, as `whole`, `set` and `role` in `window.__filmLayout`, for these measures to read.

The frame audit also traces every text of every frame, whatever its role and whether or not a scene draws it, with its contrast and opacity (`TEXTS`, read by `filmkit.picture.text_trace`), and the gate reads two more measures from that trace. The frames before the first word must show every word of the title the edition's script declares, at 3:1 or more, for at least 1 s: a text counts for the title words it repeats as one run, so the title may stand in parts and on several lines, and a card whose wording departs from the declared title does not count. And no text at rest may stand below 4.5:1: the gate refuses each run of 0.7 s or more in which a text that holds a letter or a digit shows above 4% opacity at a WCAG contrast below 4.5:1 against the ground, its colour composited over the ground at its opacity. A shorter run is a text entering or leaving the picture. A colour other than `#rrggbb` counts as the film's ink, and contrast is measured against the ground, not against a panel behind the text.

The frame audit also reads every label a frame paints on more than one line, as the film paints it (`wrapped_labels`), and judges it at the first frame in which each of its lines stands at 0.9 opacity or more. Within the lines of one size, the gate refuses a line other than the last that spans less than `line_share` of the widest of them, a last line under `last_share`, an English line other than the last that ends on a lead word (an article, preposition, conjunction, determiner, possessive, wh-word or auxiliary, or one of the film's `lead_words`), a last line holding one word of a name of three or more, and a Chinese line after the first that opens on closing punctuation. A name over its smaller gloss is two blocks, and the same name standing in two places is two labels. A film keeps a break by declaring its `edition`, its painted `lines` and a `reason` among the `exceptions` of `[picture.wrapping]`; the gate record lists each kept break and refuses an exception that no frame of its edition paints.

### Motion

The motion audit follows every mark a scene draws at ink level 25 or more as a viewer sees it move, at 30 frames per second, with speeds in 1080p pixels per second. A mark is its scene, kind, words and line width with its screen box. A mark whose box stands unchanged continues the mark that stood there. The other marks of each group of like marks continue the previous frame's by the assignment of least total miss, a mark's miss being how far its edges stand from where its own motion puts them. A pair is one mark when it moved whole, at nearly the same size (2 px or 5%) with both edges of each axis shifting together, or when it grew, shrank or was redrawn, in place or while it moved, overlapping by half the smaller box once the shift both edges share is taken out; that shift stays within 4,500 px/s, while its edges may grow faster, as a stroke drawn on does. A pair that holds one end of one of its drawn pieces exactly where it stood grows or turns from that end and does not travel, however its box flips. A mark's drawn pieces also show its motion: an open piece is where it is drawn from, its first point, and a closed piece is at its centre, and the shift that more than half of a pair's pieces, taken in their order, share to within 2 px or a fifth is the shift its pieces share. A mark rests only where its box and its pieces both show it at rest, so a link drawn on from a start that glides, whose box stops shifting as it turns a corner, or a row that grows a piece at its end while it glides, neither halts nor starts. Where a piece turns a corner and its box flips too far for the overlap, the pair is one mark when each piece, moved by the shift its pieces share, meets the piece drawn in its place. Of marks that stand alike, as two outlines crossfading on one gliding box, the one nearest the mark's ink continues it. A followed mark changes its velocity within one frame by no more than 1,200 px/s or its own speed, a mark under 6 px across grows into or shrinks from only a mark that is small across, so a flow's dot entering and leaving by size is no race, and a text whose words change in place, as a counter's, continues.

A frame is a stage cut for a scene when 35% or more of the scene's marks at ink level 60 or more, counted or weighed by their ink, vanish or appear in it, or when the composition declares a cut of the scene in it, in a global `stageCuts` map of each scene to the times of its cuts: the one place where marks may change at once. A mark that appears or vanishes is judged by the pieces it brings or takes, its drawn subpaths that no like mark of the other frame holds in place, so a path that gains a stroke, a slab that joins its stack or a line drawn on from a point is no pop. Outside stage cuts the gate refuses four kinds of motion:

| Finding         | What a viewer sees                                                                                                       |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `motion_halts`  | A mark whose speed falls from 60 px/s or more to under 1.5 px/s within one frame                                         |
| `motion_starts` | A mark followed for two frames or more whose speed rises from under 1.5 px/s to 60 px/s or more within one frame         |
| `motion_races`  | A mark of 6 px or more across moving at 900 px/s or more                                                                 |
| `motion_pops`   | A mark at ink level 60 or more that appears or vanishes within one frame, bringing or taking pieces 12 px or more across |

A halt after a step at under half the step before it, and a start before a step of more than twice its own, is an eased end sampled at 30 fps and is not refused. The gate names each finding once per run of frames of one kind, scene and drawing line, with its time, the paints and words, its speed or ink and the line of `picture/film.js` that drew it: the innermost line of the scene's own code on the call stack, a scene's body or a module the bundle places below `scenes/`, rather than the shared helper that painted it, with the module where the bundle names one. A film keeps a finding by declaring its `edition`, `scene`, `source` (that line, `film.js:<line>`, or the module, which other edits leave in place), `kind` (`halt`, `start`, `race` or `pop`) and `reason` in `[[picture.motion.exceptions]]`; the gate record lists each kept finding and refuses an exception that matches no finding of its edition. The motion report's `followed` section holds every event, the findings and, for each scene, the fastest glide (a text or a mark more than 16 px across) and flow (a smaller mark) with their times. The gate applies to every film from 0.12.0.

## Before a render

`filmkit.produce preflight --language zh|en` reports every stale or missing input of an edition's render at once, each by the owner that would refuse it later, and refuses while one remains: a stage of either picture that is not its current UHD picture, a tracked cover other than the one the current stage paints (the `player-check cover` comparison, its painting below `build/runtime/work/preflight/<edition>/`), an earlier `film-package.json`, which packaging would refuse to extend once the render replaces its picture, a draft still scan that is missing, failing or bound to another timeline or composition, a failing concept check, and a picture gate record that has not passed on the current inputs. A film scans its 10 fps draft with `python -m filmkit.picture.still_scan <draft> --language zh|en`, which judges it against the edition's timeline and writes `build/evidence/editions/<edition>/draft-stills.json`, bound to the draft, the timeline and the composition.

## Captions on and off

Each edition has two pictures. The captioned picture draws the timeline's captions. The uncaptioned picture is staged from the same composition and timeline with `captions` set to `[]`, so a composition whose caption painter is the only reader of the captions needs no change. `stage-picture`, `render-picture` and `validate-picture` take `--uncaptioned` for it: its stage is `build/runtime/work/final-delivery-<edition>-uhd-uncaptioned/`, its picture `<slug>-final-picture-uncaptioned.mp4` and its receipt `render/receipt-uncaptioned.json`, rendered with the same HyperFrames settings, and each stage record and receipt names its `picture`. A record written before 0.13.0 names none and stays the captioned picture's.

`filmkit.produce compare-pictures --language zh|en` proves that the two differ only by the captions, and packaging refuses an edition without its passing record, `build/evidence/editions/<edition>/caption-equivalence.json`:

- **The composition.** `filmkit.tools caption-equivalence` runs the composition headlessly on the timeline and on the timeline without captions, at every caption's start, middle and end and every 0.5 s. Each uncaptioned frame's paints must be the captioned frame's in order, once its caption paints are set aside: texts painted outside every scene whose letters and digits are a cue's display text, the cue that started last or the next one, and that the frame's layout names with the `caption` role. Any other difference refuses the edition, so a film that paints anything else from its captions is refused. The tool reports the band of rows the caption paints cover.
- **The encoded pictures.** Both rendered pictures are decoded at every 0.5 s and at every caption's start, middle and end frame and compared outside the caption band, widened by 24 rows of the 1080-row frame on each side and out to 16-pixel blocks. In each frame no 64-pixel tile of the luma, or 32-pixel tile of the chroma, may differ by more than 4 levels on average, and the PSNR of the compared samples must be at least 50 dB. On the sample film the encoder's noise reached 1.5 levels and 60.2 dB, and a picture missing one label outside the captions 17 to 25 levels.

The record binds the composition, timeline, engine and both pictures' stage documents, files and receipts, and holds each level's measures and findings.

## The player

The engine renders each edition's player page. A film declares only the `summary` the page shows under the film and the colours of `[player]`, and a film whose sources still hold a `player.html` is refused when the declaration loads and at packaging, naming those declarations. In the edition's language, the page shows the title, the author, language, running time and frame rate, links to the other edition's player and to the entry page, the film, a button for each version and the captions control, a button for each chapter, the summary, the transcript and the files list.

The page opens on the viewing film with its captions. Each version has a film whose picture draws the captions and one without them. The captions control is a button, pressed while the captions show, whose words say whether they do, `字幕：开` or `字幕：关` and `Captions: on` or `Captions: off`; its accessible name is `字幕` or `Captions` beside the pressed state. It switches the film to the other one of the same version, and a version button switches to its version's film with the captions as they are; both keep the time and whether the film plays. The page shows the captions no other way. It embeds the edition's WebVTT cues as a hidden metadata track: a `captions` track would make the browser's own controls offer a captions menu, which would double the drawn captions. The cues are embedded as a data URL because Chrome refuses a page opened from disk the file beside it. While the film plays, the active cue marks the transcript's current paragraph.

The transcript, in the player and in the Markdown file, gives each thought a paragraph. A chapter heading carries the chapter's start and a section heading the start of its voice, as `m:ss`, or `h:mm:ss` past an hour; in the player each time is a button that plays the film from there.

The files list names each delivered file by kind, version, format and size, in decimal units, and says of each film whether its picture draws the captions, as `影片 · 超高清 4K · 画面内含字幕 · MP4 · 263 MB` and `影片 · 超高清 4K · 无字幕 · MP4 · 241 MB`. A browser shows the films and covers as a viewer expects, so they are links. It would show every other file as raw text, so the Markdown transcript, the chapter files and the SRT and WebVTT captions are downloads, labelled by kind and purpose, as `讲稿（Markdown，供下载） · 9 KB`. They are data URLs of the files' bytes, since a browser ignores `download` on a file beside a page opened from disk. The transcript in the page stays the place to read.

`filmkit.delivery.documents_folder` reads the pages back before rsync changes anything: `player-check players` opens the entry page and both players in a real browser from `build/artifacts/film`, and delivery refuses a page that fails, naming the page and its problems. A dry run reads no player. The receipt, `build/evidence/delivery/player-check.json`, records in `pages` the `verdict` and `problems` of each page beside what was read, and the delivery readback names it with its SHA-256. Each page's links must lead to delivery members, sibling pages or places in the page, and the entry page must lead to both players. On each player:

- the track embeds the delivered WebVTT, and its cues equal the SRT's in number, times and text;
- the page opens on the viewing film with its captions control pressed; the control turns the captions off on the paused viewing film and on again on the playing UHD film, and a version button keeps them off, each switch reaching the other film from the same time and keeping the film paused or playing, and the control pressed as the captions show;
- the track stays hidden on every film, whichever of the page's controls is clicked, and the browser's own captions control stays hidden;
- on each version a cue marks the paragraph that holds it, a switch keeps the time, and all four films play at their size and duration and decode at three seek points;
- a chapter button and a transcript time play from their time, and the language links lead to the other edition's player and the entry page;
- each listed file carries its member's size, no link opens raw text, and each download saves the delivered bytes and leaves the page where it is.

## Review rounds

`filmkit.review.round` prepares and records an independent review round. `prompts --round <n>` fills the film's reviewer prompt for each edition; with `--previous <dir>`, `{previous_review}` cites that round's review and findings and asks the reviewer to verify each repair, and without it the round is the film's first production review and `{previous_review}` says that no earlier one exists. A template that cites `{previous}` or `{findings}` itself still works with `--previous` and is refused without it. `{clarity}` states the declared clarity floors.

A round after the first needs `--dispositions <file>`, a JSON file `{"round": <n>, "findings": {<finding id>: <disposition>}}` that gives every finding of the previous round's reviews one disposition: `fixed` with the `evidence` that its reverification holds, `declared` with the `design` that keeps it, or `deferred` with its `owner` and `reason`. The prompts refuse a file that misses a finding, names one the reviews do not hold or gives an entry other fields, and a template that does not cite `{dispositions}`, which states the edition's dispositions in these three groups and asks the reviewer to re-verify each fixed finding by its own reverification line.

A film that declares `[review.levels]` is reviewed as its viewer judges it, level by level: 信, true to the source, to the other edition and to the words; 达, understood on first hearing and first sight; and 雅, natural, restrained and finished. Its template must cite `{levels}`, which states each level with what it asks and, under it, each of its dimensions with what that asks, and `{sentences}`, which names `sentences-<edition>.json` in the round's directory: every sentence of every thought, split at 。？！ in Chinese and . ? ! in English with each quotation kept whole, with its id `<segment>.<thought>.<sentence>`, its text and its start and end in the timeline. Each review then gives every level a verdict, `{"verdict": "pass" | "fail", "findings": [<ids>]}` under `levels`, names each finding's `level`, and gives every sentence a verdict under `sentences`, `"ok"` or the ids of the findings that concern it. `record` and the verifier refuse a review that misses a level or a sentence, names an unknown sentence or a finding it does not hold, leaves a finding out of its level's findings, or whose level fails, or passes while a dimension it holds stands below `dimension_minimum` or a material finding remains in it: a level cannot be passed by averaging. A film without levels keeps its prompts, records and verification.

`produce assemble` and `produce refresh-captions` rewrite an edition's build in place, so both refuse while a review round's prompts name the edition's current films by their hashes and that round has not set them aside, naming the round and the `set-aside` command that frees the films.

`set-aside` moves the previous build's films, package record and rendered pictures of each edition into `build/artifacts/film/superseded/<label>/<edition>/` with a manifest of their hashes, and reads each back, before the next render and package. A reviewed round's packages, labelled `round-<n>`, are bound by its reviews. An unreviewed build, such as a preview the owner watched, is set aside with `--unreviewed --consumer <dir>`, which requires `<dir>/<edition>/` to hold its films byte-identical and records that folder as their consumer, under `--round <n>` or a `--label` of its own. `--language` limits a set-aside to one edition, and `--without-picture` leaves the picture in place for the editions it names, or for both when it names none.

`filmkit.review.measurement` also records each edition's motion as `picture_motion`, from the motion report its picture gate judged: the counts of halts, starts, races and pops outside stage cuts, the findings and how many of them the film declares, the fastest glide and flow of each scene and of the edition with their times, and every finding with its time, scene, speed or ink and drawing line, so reviewers judge motion from measurement rather than from contact sheets. It refuses a gate record that is not bound to the current composition and timeline, and a motion report other than the one the record binds.

`filmkit.review.measurement` records, among the measurements a round's reviewers read, each edition's voice clarity: the median cepstral peak prominence over the loud frames of its soundtrack and of each segment's voice, computed as the reviewers state it (16 kHz mono, 1024-sample frames every 256 samples, frames within 18 dB of the 95th-percentile energy, periodic Hann window, quefrency bins 40–266, the highest level minus the least-squares line at its bin), each value to 0.01 dB beside the edition's floor. A film declares the floors in dB for both editions, as `clarity_minimum_db = { zh = 18.0, en = 17.0 }` in `[review]`.

## Installing the engine in a film

A film pins a release tag in its Python project and locks it:

```toml
[project]
dependencies = ["filmkit"]

[tool.uv.sources]
filmkit = { git = "https://github.com/HengYangDS/filmkit", tag = "v0.13.0" }
```

Its `package.json` declares the npm packages the engine runs, at these versions: `hyperframes` 0.9.9, the renderer, which the engine checks before it stages, renders or runs a tool; `gsap` 3.15.0, which each staged document loads; and `puppeteer-core` 25.16.0 and `@puppeteer/browsers` 3.2.6, which drive and install the browser build. It also overrides `undici` to 7.30.1, because HyperFrames 0.9.9 pins 7.25.0, which known advisories affect.

## Whole endings

The assembly refuses a thought whose take ends inside its last syllable. In-context narration with mlx-audio 0.6.4 can produce such takes: its decode counts a frame as valid only when the frame's first codebook id is above 0, while the reference tokenizer pads with −1 and counts every id above it. The reference's codes are decoded with the take's, so each frame with id 0 in either removes 80 ms from the end of the take, and a reference whose pauses encode to id 0 shortens every take narrated from it. The fix is proposed upstream as [Blaizzy/mlx-audio#1116](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/pull/1116).

Until a release carries it, `filmkit.narration.audition` keeps endings whole. Its targets file names each thought the assembly refused and a phrase the take must contain. An audition narrates other seeds of each target and proposes a take that still ends naturally; a run-on target is narrated on into the next thought and cut in the quiet between, so the trim falls on the words cut away. `apply` binds the proposals into the cast's retakes, and narration then records only the retaken thoughts again. A retake that `apply` binds keeps the SHA-256 of the spoken text its audition narrated, and narration refuses one whose thought now speaks other text, so a changed reading is auditioned again instead of narrated from a seed chosen for other speech.

## Take onsets

A take can open with a codec onset, a low thump of 10 to 60 ms that dies into the take's quiet before its first word. The assembly releases it to digital silence where it would sound out of digital silence. At a thought join, sound of at most 0.1 s between digital silence after the thought's last word and the join's native quiet is released; where a join's digital silence ends its native quiet, the sound it is left into is released when that sound meets heard quiet within 0.1 s and before the next word. The silence leaves into that quiet through a soft fade. A recording that offers its clip no heard quiet before its first word begins the clip in its released onset: the sound before its first 10 ms heard as quiet, when that lasts at most 0.1 s and ends by the first aligned word; any other such recording is refused. Speech, the designed quiet and every timing stay as they are, and each designed thought pause and each clip's `boundary_fades` record `released_onset_samples`. The audition proposes a take that opens its recording only when the assembly would begin its clip in quiet (`clip_opens_quiet`); it records the raw level of the take's first 10 ms (`first_dbfs`, `opens_quiet`) and whether its onset is releasable (`onset_releasable`). The aligner places words in 0.08 s steps, so a take heard in its first 10 ms whose first word aligns at 0 s cannot release its onset and is refused.

## The sample film

`examples/sample-film` is a one-minute bilingual film that the engine's tests load and that builds end to end:

```sh
mise run bootstrap
mise run sample:supply
mise run sample:build
```

The build writes only below `examples/sample-film/build/`. Its text is written for this repository and is no film's source.

Packaging refuses to extend films bound to other inputs. A film sets a reviewed round's films aside with `filmkit.review.round set-aside`, and a build nobody reviewed whose films a consumer folder holds with `set-aside --unreviewed --consumer <dir>`; to rebuild the unreviewed sample after a change, first delete each edition's four films below `build/artifacts/film/<edition>/` and its `build/evidence/editions/<edition>/film-package.json`.
