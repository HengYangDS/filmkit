## Why

On 23 November 2026 the owner said [S1]:

- 14:20 「此外，en 和 zh 应该是不同语种的变体，为什么在相关仓库中，组织结构上不是并列的？这在语义和逻辑上不对」
- 14:21 「本身从基础能力上，从本质上位的角度，就应该是支持多语种，且各语种之间是并列结构。目前仅支持中英，未来有可能扩展」
- 14:22 「组织结构上的问题，需要全面深挖修复」

The structure audit of that day found where the engine and its films break this [S2]. The engine reads each edition's files from the folder the film declares, but it fixes the set of languages itself: a closed constant, nine `Literal["zh", "en"]` types and a second table in Node. Twenty-three branches test one code and hand the other language to everything else, so a third language would not be refused but misplaced; seven places assume exactly two editions; several places take Latin script to mean English. Each language's values and behaviour are spread over about 30 files with no one declaration, and the Han character class alone is defined ten times with six ranges. In every film, and in the sample, the Chinese edition's files sit in `content/` among the files that all editions share, its reference recording beside the shared identity recording, and its cover without a code, while the English edition's files sit in `content/en/`. The scripts, casts and contract restate languages, paths and digests that the engine derives. This Change makes the languages equals in the engine and in the sample's layout, and gives each film one command that names what it must move. The sample's picture still holds both editions' words, and gives the Chinese words to any edition that is not English, until Change B.

At 18:03 and 18:26 the same day the owner asked for the engine to be derived again from its essence, breaking changes allowed [S8]. This Change is that derivation's first release, R1 (0.31.0) [S8, §5.5, §5.7]. Its later releases retire modules this Change would otherwise build into, so what nothing needs before R2b is cut, what a film or the sample needs until then is built as a bridge that names the release deleting it, and the restated facts that need no new mechanism are withdrawn here too [S8, §2.2]. `amendments-r1.md` lists each change with its reason.

## What Changes

- **BREAKING: each edition's files stand in the folder of its code.** An edition's script, cast, pronunciation and readings stand in `content/<code>/`; what every edition shares stays in `content/`. An edition's reference recording stands at `assets/audio/<code>/narration-reference.wav`, the narrator's shared identity recording at `assets/audio/narrator-identity.wav`, and its cover at `assets/cover/<slug>-cover-<code>.png`. The engine derives each place from the code. No language is the unmarked default in any name.
- **BREAKING: the film's `[editions.<code>]` tables are the one source of its languages and their order.** The engine keeps no list of codes; it admits a code for which it holds a language declaration and refuses any other at load. A film may have one edition or more, and everything that covers several editions takes them in the declared order.
- **The engine declares each language once.** One declaration per language, `src/filmkit/languages/<code>.toml`, loaded by one module, holds every value and choice specific to it: its tags, the name its speech models take, its display name and byline, its unit of speech, whether spaces delimit its words, how it marks number, the language it reads each other script in (Chinese reads Latin script as English), its caption phrasing, its reading checks, its stemmer, its lead words, its register rules and its player words. A behaviour that more than one language could share stays once in code, named by its basis, and a declaration chooses it. No code branches on a language's code, name or tag, and no script stands for a language: each piece of text takes the language its words are written in.
- **Script and punctuation classes are defined once** in one data file that the Python modules and the Node audits both read.
- **Editions are N, not a pair.** A player links every other edition's player; the entry page states `lang="mul"` and links every declared edition in the declared order, each link in its own language; a review prompt names the other editions as `{others}` and each edition's files as `{editions}`; the concept check compares each edition with every other; verification and the release record ask for every declared edition, as bridges until R2b's `filmkit verify`; readiness already asks every edition of the set the declaration now gives.
- **BREAKING: what the engine derives is removed, not moved.** A script declares no `language`, at its top or in a segment, no `pronunciation_contract`, no `author` and no `fps`, and its pause policy no `performance_intent`, `energy_curve`, `pacing_profile` or `provider_notes`, which no step reads. A segment declares no `chapter_index` or `chapter_title`, which its `chapter` and the script's `chapters` give. A cast declares no `narration_language`, `language`, `reference_language`, `performance_recording`, `reference_audio`, `reference_text_sha256` or `provider`, and none of the engine's pins, `model`, `revision` or `reference_mode`; its `scope`, the history of its reference recording, moves into its `derivation`, as do any words of the pause-policy prose that record how the voice was made or approved. The film's declaration states `author` once; the frame rate is the engine's. The declaration's `editions.<code>.sources`, `editions.<code>.cover` and `review.readings` are withdrawn. Each edition's `narration.md`, a copy of its script's texts, is no longer kept. The design names what derives each.
- **BREAKING: the loader refuses at once every fact of the declaration and its sources that this release changes**, each in one line that says what to write instead: every withdrawn key of the declaration, a script, a segment, a cast or a pronunciation part; every edition's file at an old place, with its destination; every file this release removes; a missing reference recording or pronunciation part; a cast whose derivation cites the identity recording elsewhere; a review prompt line that cites a former field; and an entry page that breaks the new rule.
- **BREAKING: the pronunciation contract is split by edition.** Each edition's part stands in `content/<code>/pronunciation.json` with its own sources and targets, and its analyst readings in `content/<code>/readings.json`; the schema version, the control boundary, the acceptance criteria and any source that several editions cite stand once in the declaration's `[pronunciation]` table. A part states no language, and the contract tracks no occurrence plan, which the readings check derives. A take binds only its own edition's part.
- **A recording binds its reference recording by its content.** No step opens or compares the path a take recorded, so moving a reference recording keeps every take selectable and auditionable.
- **`filmkit.adoption` gains, as a bridge until R2b retires it, the question `editions`, which fails closed:** each line of a film's tasks, its NOTICE, its test loaders and any other tracked text the loader does not read that names a moved or removed file by its old path. Each finding belongs to one question: the loader's refusals are the `declaration` question's.
- **The sample film moves** to this layout, task by task, its suite passing at each.

Released editions keep their tag's engine and are not rebuilt: the released films pin `v0.22.3` (`0.22.3` in each of the three films' `uv.lock`). A film adopts this release with its December editions, in its own lane, so its first adoption also carries what 0.23 to 0.30 ask (`CHANGELOG.md`), which the release note restates, among it every take narrated anew under 0.23.1's mlx-audio 0.6.6. A pure move keeps the sample's delivered bytes, proved on one engine before any code changes. The release note names every record that changes: every take's fingerprint and file name, each narration receipt, the timelines and so the stage digests, `film-package.json` and the viewing packages, the readiness receipts, the listening plans, `content/release.json`'s source paths, and the entry page's bytes.

Out of scope, each a later release of the derivation (the design's Non-Goals give each its paragraph): picture words by id, one table per edition (Change B, the derivation's R5); the README's form (the documentation goal G-2, R2b); the tracked generated bundle `picture/film.js` (R5); the keying of evidence and caches (R2b); the release record's place (R2b); `produce --language all`, which nothing needs before R2b's targets. This Change corrects only the README's statements that it would make false, and the README grows by nothing.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `film-engine`: a film's editions are the languages it declares, in its order, one or more; the engine declares each language once and branches on none; script and punctuation classes are defined once; each edition's files stand in the folder of its code, and the declaration names no place the engine derives; a script, a segment and a cast state only what the engine does not derive, none of its pins and no prose that no step reads, and the film states its author once; the loader refuses at once every fact of the declaration and its sources that a film must change; no narration copy is kept beside a script; an edition's text holds only the scripts its language reads.
- `sample-film`: the sample lays out its two editions as equals.
- `concept-contract`: a concept carries one rendering for each declared edition; each edition is compared with every other; the number rule reads which languages mark number.
- `film-package`: track tags and player words come from the language's declaration; a player links every other edition; the entry page states `mul` and leads to every edition, which the loader checks.
- `review-rounds`: a round carries the register report of every edition; each prompt names its edition's files and every other edition by fields.
- `spoken-readings`: each edition's pronunciation stands in its folder and binds only its own recordings; the shared provenance stands once in the declaration; a part states nothing the engine derives.
- `picture-gates`: a label's language is the language its words are written in, as the registry reads them in its edition, never its script alone; an English label of a Chinese edition is stemmed and wrapped as English.
- `formal-register`: each place is checked by the rules of the languages its words are in; a listed form is refused where its rule's language applies.
- `engine-distribution`: the bridge question `editions`, for the old paths in the text the loader does not read; one finding for each fact; the `runtime` question keeps only the review prompt's lines.
- `narration-anchors`: word boundaries hold in a language whose words spaces delimit; the report compares each edition with every other.
