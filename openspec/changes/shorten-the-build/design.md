## Context

The engine builds each edition from narration to verified films in one sequence of commands, none of which records how long it took except the render. The films' session assembled the cost of one build of the 25-minute film from logs and file times on 17 November 2026 (18 cores, engine 0.22.3): narration of both editions 37 to 50 min, four UHD renders 61 min (844 to 951 s each), two comparisons 30 min, packaging 22 min, verification 8 min, with the audits and the sound stage about 2 h 55 min. The proposal states the owner's request and the scope.

Two measurements and one review stand behind the decisions, all of 17 November 2026 and all kept in the films' production kit (`fk/r35/`), outside this repository:

- **bench1** (`bench1/result.txt`, one host ticket, 02:03 to 02:25 CST): a 120 s span, 7,200 UHD frames, of a film's captioned and uncaptioned stages, software rasterizer, screenshot capture.
- **The static review** (`static-review.md`): every heavy command's processes, decodes and loops read from the source at `e70a437`, with phase splits taken from the file times of the 16 November build.
- **bench2** (`bench2/result.txt`, one ticket, 03:40 to 03:53 CST), on the same span: 16 workers again, segmented capture, the 1080p film by x264 against today's encoder, and one decode feeding three encodes. Its two renders at once were refused by the renderer's own lock.
- **bench3** (`bench3/result.txt`, one ticket, 04:34 to 04:53 CST), on the same span and on four segments of a film's narration: x264 at four rate factors and three presets for the 1080p film and at three rate factors for the derived UHD picture, an edition's derived passes at once, two renders at once with the renderer's own lock off, and narration in one, two and four processes.

One small measurement of this Change is cited where it is used: a synthetic clip derived at niceness 15 (`fk/r36/evidence/ground-ripple.txt`).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A build's time is read from records, stage by stage, with one command.
- The 25-minute film rebuilds in 90 minutes of compute or less. This Change removes one of an edition's two renders and its every-frame comparison, shortens the render that stays, and takes the 1080p encodes off the hardware encoder; each saving is to be measured on a film's build with the durations command before the budget is called met.
- A film's task list does not change, and a film that needs the native pair can still have it.

**Non-Goals:**

- A one-scene change in 20 minutes: it needs span renders and a segment cache, a later slice.
- Any change to what a frame shows. The captioned picture is rendered as before; only the worker count of its render changes, which changes no frame.
- New review thresholds.

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, and where it applies.

1. **Durations are recorded beside the evidence, never inside a record that another record binds.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review, whose phase splits had to be read from file times ("the sampled pass 219 and 196 s, the every-frame scan 675 and 678 s"), and on how the engine binds records: a stage record binds the assembly record by SHA-256, the film package binds the comparison record, and the readiness receipt binds every record it judged. A duration differs from run to run.
   - **Considered:** (a) the seconds inside each command's own record, as first proposed; (b) one ledger file for a build; (c) one small duration record for each command and edition, beside the edition's evidence.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). With (a), running `check-picture` or `compare-pictures` again on unchanged inputs would change the bytes of a record that a receipt or a package binds, and a harmless repeat would make the next step stale. With (b), two commands of one edition that end together would write one file. Each record of (c) has one writer and binds nothing.
   - **Applies to:** every action of `filmkit.produce` and `narrate`. The render receipt keeps its `elapsed_seconds`. A phase is named by the owner of the work: the comparison's validation, composition report, sampled frames and every-frame scan; each film of a package; each audit of `check-picture`; each step of `narrate`. The verifier's contract is that it writes nothing, so it states each film's seconds in the report it prints, and the reproduction in the report it writes, which no record binds.
2. **The render's worker count comes from the host.**
   - **Rests on:** bench1 and bench2. Warm, 8 workers took 126.2 to 141.2 s and 16 workers 114.1 and 118.0 s; cold, as the first render of a ticket, 184.2 s with 8 and 143.6 s with 16; 12 workers took 125.1 and 127.5 s and the renderer's `auto` 124.8 s. Every picture was identical to the 8-worker picture frame for frame (PSNR infinite). About eight cores were busy whatever the count (CPU seconds over wall seconds 7.7 to 8.1).
   - **Considered:** keeping 8; the renderer's `auto`; a value in the film's declaration; a value from the host.
   - **Chosen because:** the count is a property of the host, not of the film, and it changes no frame, so a declaration would only copy a host's number into every film. Sixteen workers took a tenth to a fifth less time than eight on the one host measured: a setting worth taking, not the main lever. The rule, the logical cores less two, never under 8 nor over 16, gives 16 on the 18-core host and today's 8 on a host of ten cores or fewer; `FILMKIT_RENDER_WORKERS` overrides it for a measurement.
   - **Applies to:** the pictures and the draft. One host is measured; the range between 8 and 16 on other hosts is an interpolation, which is why the receipt records the count.
3. **The renderer's determinism is proven once for each supply, in the sample build.**
   - **Rests on:** what the per-build comparison guards against. Its every-frame scan was added for the one-frame faults of 10 to 12 November, which were traced to GPU rasterization; since 0.20.0 the films draw in the software rasterizer. bench1's six renders of one stage, at four worker counts and two capture paths, were identical frame for frame.
   - **Considered:** keeping two native renders and the every-frame comparison in every film build (30 of 61 render minutes and all 30 comparison minutes for the 25-minute film); dropping the proof; proving it where the renderer, the browser and the capture are pinned, at the engine's release.
   - **Chosen because:** the fault the comparison guards against belongs to the renderer supply, which only an engine release changes, and the sample build runs for every release. There it renders one stage twice and requires every decoded frame identical (`qualify-renderer`), and compares a natively rendered pair at every frame.
   - **Applies to:** films that declare `software` capture. A film of another capture keeps its two renders and the every-frame comparison in every build, without asking (decision 11); an owner who wants the proof for an edition under `software` runs `render-picture --uncaptioned --native` and `compare-pictures`, which then compares at every frame as before.
4. **The uncaptioned picture is the captioned picture with its band painted.**
   - **Rests on:** bench1: derived by one x264 encode (veryfast, CRF 14) in 17.8 s against 141.2 s for the native render of the span, 10.1 MB against 18.9 MB; the band identical to the native uncaptioned render's in all 7,200 frames; above the band 59.0 dB on average and 52.1 dB at the lowest frame from the native render, where the two native renders stand 64.8 and 56.1 dB apart and the engine's floor for a compared frame is 45 dB. VideoToolbox derived the same picture in 97.5 s.
   - **Considered:** (a) the native second render; (b) the renderer's `--variables` batch, which still renders every frame twice; (c) a lossless intermediate from which both pictures are encoded, which changes the captioned picture's path too; (d) painting the band of the captioned picture and encoding once.
   - **Chosen because:** (d) is the only one that removes the second render, and it leaves the captioned picture, the one every review reads, untouched. The paint is the ground as the captioned picture itself encodes it, read from one of its frames in YUV and laid over the band's rows without a colour conversion; the measurements painted by RGB colour, which matched that film's ground and need not match another's. The rate factor is 12: bench3 derived the span at rate factors 14, 12 and 10 in 20.7, 21.0 and 22.8 s, at 59.5, 60.7 and 61.7 dB on average above the band from the native render and 52.1, 53.5 and 52.6 dB at the lowest frame, so 12 is a decibel closer than 14 in the same time. The time of this form of the paint is to be measured in the sample build.
   - **Applies to:** a film whose captions stand on a flat ground that nothing else draws on, which decision 5 proves for each derivation. The derived picture is a second encode of the frames above the band; a film that wants its uncaptioned master as a first encode asks for `--native`.
5. **A derivation proves that the band holds only captions on the ground, in the work it does anyway.**
   - **Rests on:** what the every-frame comparison proved for a native pair, that the pictures differ by the captions alone. For a derived picture the frames above the band are the captioned picture's by construction, so what remains to prove is about the band: that the composition paints nothing but captions there, and that what the paint replaces is the ground.
   - **Considered:** (a) decoding both pictures again after the encode, which is the comparison this Change removes; (b) the composition's report at every half second alone, which a mark shown for a third of a second passes; (c) a headless pass at every thirtieth of a second, the frame audit's own step, with a measure of the captioned band taken in the encode's decode.
   - **Chosen because:** (c) costs no decode. The headless pass refuses a caption paint that leaves the painted rows and any other paint that reaches them, and lists the stretches without a caption. The encode's filter graph measures the captioned band's lowest and highest sample of each plane at every frame, and the derivation refuses unless every frame of those stretches, less two audit steps at each end, stands within 3 levels of the ground. A caption or a mark departs by tens to hundreds of levels; the encoder's ripple in a flat band reached 2 levels below busy synthetic content and none on bench1's film span. Run read-only on the three films' six current editions at niceness 15, the headless pass took 7 to 21 s and passed each, and listed 18.8 to 127.4 s without a caption in 9 to 160 stretches (`fk/r36/films.log`).
   - **Applies to:** every derivation. Not seen by it: a rectangle the composition fills with `fillRect` under a caption, which the headless stand-in does not report; the derived picture paints over it. No film draws one.
6. **A derived pair is compared by its record.**
   - **Rests on:** decision 5: the proofs are made when the picture is derived and recorded in its receipt.
   - **Considered:** a separate command for the derived pair; leaving `compare-pictures` out of a derived build.
   - **Chosen because:** packaging and the verifier ask for one comparison record of every edition, and a film's task list should not depend on how its uncaptioned picture was made. `compare-pictures` runs the composition report as for any pair, requires the derivation's receipt to bind the current captioned picture, composition, timeline and engine with both proofs passed and the same band, and writes the same record with the pair marked derived.
   - **Applies to:** a pair whose uncaptioned receipt says derived. A natively rendered pair is compared at every frame, and `repair-pictures` serves that pair alone.
7. **The 1080p films are encoded by x264 on a forced keyframe grid.** This replaces the draft's decision 16 (encode again after a keyframe refusal).
   - **Rests on:** six refusals of a 1080p film on its keyframes since 13 November, at host loads from 8 to 120, with no cause found. The sixth, on 17 November at 03:34, held the grid to 102.0 s, then set keyframes at 102.85, 104.85, 105.517 and 105.967 s and every 2 s from there, 251 of 303 off the grid; another heavy step of the same session started on the host in that minute, which is one timed coincidence and not a cause, and the refusal of 16 November has none beside it. Each refusal ended a film's build, cost a host ticket's wait and needed a restart by hand: the hardware encoder's cadence is not guaranteed under this host's load. bench1: VideoToolbox is the slower encoder at UHD (74 frames a second) and x264 the faster. x264 places a keyframe exactly every `-g` frames when `-keyint_min` equals it and scene-cut detection is off.
   - **Considered:** a retry by the packager, which keeps a refusal whose cause is unknown; a VideoToolbox option that pins the cadence, of which none is documented; one decode of the captioned picture feeding an edition's three derived encodes.
   - **Chosen because:** a software encoder's keyframes depend on its options alone, and x264 is also the faster encoder here. The criterion for the rate, set before bench3 ran, was the lowest frame not under VideoToolbox's at the smallest time. bench2 and bench3, on the span against its area-scaled source, as seconds, megabytes, and decibels on average and at the lowest frame: VideoToolbox as the engine ran it 23.7 s, 23.9 MB, 63.4 and 51.8 dB; x264 `veryfast` at rate factor 16, 7.8 s, 4.8 MB, 56.4 and 48.7 dB; at 14, 7.4 s, 5.7 MB, 58.2 and 50.3 dB; at 12, 8.1 s, 6.7 MB, 59.8 and 51.8 dB; at 10, 9.5 s, 7.8 MB, 61.4 and 53.3 dB; `faster` at 14, 11.4 s, 60.0 and 51.3 dB; `medium` at 14, 11.9 s, 61.3 and 51.5 dB; every x264 output with all 60 keyframes on the grid. Rate factor 12 meets the criterion by 0.01 dB on this one span, which is no margin; 10 stands 1.5 dB over it for 1.4 s more a span, about 17 s for a 25-minute film, at a third of VideoToolbox's size, and is faster and closer than the slower presets at 14. The settings are named constants: preset `veryfast`, rate factor 10, `-g 120 -keyint_min 120 -sc_threshold 0`, the area scale as before. One decode feeding the three derived encodes took 43.6 s in bench2, slower than the three as separate passes in a row, so each derived film is its own pass that reads the captioned picture; the 1080p uncaptioned film of a derived pair is painted and scaled from it in that pass, one encode from the render and not two.
   - **Applies to:** both 1080p films of an edition, whose derived passes run in a row and never at once: bench3 ran an edition's three derived films as three processes at once in 51.8 s, where the three in a row take about 36 s (20.7, 7.4 and about 8 s), and two editions' six at once in 79.4 s against about 72 s, since each pass already uses every core. The figures are one span of one film; a film whose 1080p film falls under its floor in a rebuild comparison is the evidence that would move the constant.
8. **Independent checks run side by side, and the two editions' pictures render at once on request.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review: `check-picture`'s audits share no file and each is one single-threaded loop (46 + 87 s and 83 + 102 s on 16 November, before the detail audit's 40 s); the verifier decodes eight films in sequence in 482 s. bench1: one render keeps about eight of 18 cores busy. bench2: the renderer runs one render at a time for a user account and refused a second, naming its own switch, `HYPERFRAMES_RENDER_LOCK=off`. bench3, with that switch: the span's captioned and uncaptioned stages rendered at once in 172.3 s with 8 workers each and 176.4 s with 16 each, where the two in sequence take 228 to 236 s at 16 workers and 252 to 282 s at 8, every frame identical to the single renders'.
   - **Considered:** a general worker pool in the engine; side-by-side runs only where the work is read-only or writes files of its own; for the two editions, leaving the caller to start two commands and set the renderer's switch itself, or threads in one process, or one command that starts each edition's own command as a child.
   - **Chosen because:** the three audits and the films of a verification meet that condition, and each keeps its own report, refusal and order. The two editions' pictures share no file either, and two renders at once take a quarter less time than two in sequence and change no frame, so `render-picture --language all` makes both: each edition by a child of the single-edition command, which keeps that command's receipt, duration record and refusal as they are, with the renderer's switch set for exactly those children and recorded in each receipt. A caller who set the switch for a whole shell would lift the lock for renders the engine does not start. Sixteen workers each gained nothing over eight each, so each child keeps the host's count. Two derivations run in a row, as decision 7 states for every derived pass. A child that fails does not stop the other; once both have ended the command fails, naming the edition.
   - **Applies to:** `check-picture` (three audits at once, the whole-turn check after the frame audit), the verifier (up to four films at once) and `render-picture --language all`, which a film's task list may use and need not; the sample film's picture task uses it, one flat list again. Measured on one host of 18 cores.
9. **Small savings that change no result.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review's changes 9, 10 and 11: transcription has no cache and transcribed 30 recordings in 43 to 48 s when 3 were new; both phases of a still scan select the same frames of a draft and decode it twice; the reproduction compares its film pairs in sequence.
   - **Considered:** leaving them, as seconds against minutes.
   - **Chosen because:** each repeats on every iteration of a film's work, and each is exact: a transcript is a function of the recording and the pinned model, the scan's frames are the same frames, and a pair's comparison reads nothing another pair writes.
   - **Applies to:** `narrate`, the still scan and the reproduction. The static review's changes 1, 2 and 8 speed up the every-frame comparison, which now runs on demand; they stay open.
10. **Narration divides its segments among processes of the speech model.**
    - **Rests on:** the static review: synthesis is 558 to 787 s of a 30-segment narration, and every thought is generated from its own request and seed into files named by its own fingerprint. bench3, on four segments of a film's narration (209 s of Chinese speech): one process 99.8 s, two processes 55.3 s, four processes 55.2 s, the longest segment bounding that run at 48.5 s; both languages at once, one process each, 116.0 s against 190.2 s in sequence; 4.6 GB at the peak for each process, 3.8 GB with four. Every take of every run, 24 in all, has the SHA-256 of the take of its fingerprint that the film delivered.
    - **Considered:** (a) a pool of model workers with a protocol between them and the narration; (b) the vendor's batched generation, which changes what is sampled and so the takes; (c) children of the synthesis itself, each given its segments by the `--ids` the command already takes and a receipt of its own, after which the synthesis takes its ordinary path over retained recordings.
    - **Chosen because:** (c) adds no protocol and no second writer of the edition's receipt. A child is the command a person runs for a retake, so its recordings, attempts and refusals are the ones the engine already judges, and a child that fails leaves what an interrupted run always left; the narration then refuses, naming the child's segments, its log and its last line, and the next run reuses what the other children retained. The children's receipts stand in a work folder that no other step reads and that a passing run removes. Segments go to the processes by the length of their spoken text, the longest first to the least loaded, since the longest segment bounds the run. Two processes are the count measured on a whole run, 1.8 times one process's throughput; a film names more in `FILMKIT_NARRATION_PROCESSES`, and 1 keeps the single process. The duration record states the count.
    - **Applies to:** the synthesis whenever more than one segment is to be generated; one segment stays in the narration's own process. Each process loads the model, so two need about 9 GB of memory. Four processes on a narration of thirty segments are an estimate of about 2.5 times, from four takes, to be measured in a film's build.
11. **A picture is derived only under the capture the release qualifies.**
    - **Rests on:** the first build of the sample film with this Change (17 November 2026, at `95f2205`), in which `qualify-renderer` refused. The sample declared no capture, so both renders of its Chinese captioned stage drew in the host's GPU with the renderer's fast capture, 3,600 frames each at 16 workers; 171 frames differ, 3428 to 3599 but for 3432, all in the credits, which begin at frame 3420. Read frame by frame at niceness 15 (`fk/r37/evidence/qualification-refusal.txt`): 31 to 262 of a frame's 8.3 million luma samples differ, by 4 levels at most, in a box of at most 112 by 104 pixels on the credits card's title line; while the card fades in the difference changes from frame to frame, and from frame 3460 to the last it is the same 147 samples in a strip 12 pixels wide. Whether the rasterizer, the capture or the encoder made it was not determined. Under `software` capture every render of a film's span measured that day, ten at four worker counts, decoded alike, and the three films declare `software` since 0.20.0, after the one-frame faults of 10 to 12 November were traced to GPU rasterization.
    - **Considered:** (a) deriving under any capture, as this Change first did, which rests a film's uncaptioned master on one render in a mode just seen to draw one stage two ways; (b) refusing `render-picture --uncaptioned` without `--native` for a film of another capture, which breaks a task list that worked; (c) a tolerance in `qualify-renderer`, the differences being 4 levels, which would pass a mode whose faults in November were of 124 to 219 levels and came from the same rasterizer; (d) deriving for a film that declares `software` capture, and rendering the uncaptioned stage for any other, with no flag.
    - **Chosen because:** (d) states what holds: a derived picture is as good as its one render, and one render is known to repeat only under the capture the engine qualifies at each release. A film of another capture loses nothing it had: its pair is rendered and compared at every frame as before this Change. The receipt of each uncaptioned picture and the comparison record say which way was taken and why, so a reader of either need not know the rule.
    - **Applies to:** `render-picture --uncaptioned` and the validation of a derived picture, which refuses one whose film has since declared another capture. `qualify-renderer` runs under any capture and its record names it. The sample film declares `software`, so each release qualifies the capture the films release with; a release of the engine requires that qualification to pass. No render of the sample's stage under `software` capture had been compared when this was written.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A film's composition may draw in the band's rows. → The derivation refuses before it encodes and names `--native`; nothing is delivered from a refused derivation.
- A renderer supply may stop drawing a stage the same way under `software` capture. → The sample build's `qualify-renderer` refuses, and with it the release.
- The uncaptioned UHD master becomes a second-generation encode. → 59.0 dB from the native render on average on bench1's span, above the engine's own floor by 14 dB; `--native` stays.
- The rate factors rest on one span of one film, and the derivation's own form of the paint is not yet timed at UHD. → Named constants; the sample build and the verifier check every film's keyframes, and a rebuild comparison measures every frame against its floor.
- More workers on a host under load. → The count is capped at 16 and recorded; the override exists.
- Two speech models in memory at once. → About 4.6 GB each at the peak on the film measured; `FILMKIT_NARRATION_PROCESSES=1` keeps one.
- Side-by-side verification reaches the 240 s limits of a packet listing sooner on a loaded host. → Four films at most; the limits refuse and do not corrupt.

## Migration Plan

An adopting film bumps the engine, keeps its tasks and builds: its uncaptioned picture is derived, its comparison record says so, its 1080p films are x264's. It may render both captioned pictures with one `render-picture --language all` in place of two commands. Its narration runs in two processes unless it names another count, with about 4.6 GB of memory for each. It may drop `stage-picture --uncaptioned` only if it never asks for `--native`; `preflight` still asks for both stages. A release rebuilt from an earlier tag keeps that tag's engine and its bytes.

## Open Questions

- Narration in more than two processes on a whole narration, to be measured in a film's build.
- Segmented capture (`HF_SEGMENTED_CAPTURE=true`) changed nothing in bench2 (118.0 s, identical frames, no segment named in the log); whether it serves one-scene changes is untested and belongs to that later slice.
- Later slices: span renders and the segment cache; a verification that does not decode again what the build proved, which changes what the verifier claims and is the owner's decision; the reviewers' measuring tools shipped by the engine.
