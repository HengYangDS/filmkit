## Context

The engine builds each edition from narration to verified films in one sequence of commands, none of which records how long it took except the render. The films' session assembled the cost of one build of the 25-minute film from logs and file times on 17 November 2026 (18 cores, engine 0.22.3): narration of both editions 37 to 50 min, four UHD renders 61 min (844 to 951 s each), two comparisons 30 min, packaging 22 min, verification 8 min, with the audits and the sound stage about 2 h 55 min. The proposal states the owner's request and the scope.

Two measurements and one review stand behind the decisions, all of 17 November 2026 and all kept in the films' production kit (`fk/r35/`), outside this repository:

- **bench1** (`bench1/result.txt`, one host ticket, 02:03 to 02:25 CST): a 120 s span, 7,200 UHD frames, of a film's captioned and uncaptioned stages, software rasterizer, screenshot capture.
- **The static review** (`static-review.md`): every heavy command's processes, decodes and loops read from the source at `e70a437`, with phase splits taken from the file times of the 16 November build.
- **bench2** (`bench2/result.txt`, one ticket, 03:40 to 03:53 CST), on the same span: 16 workers again, segmented capture, the 1080p film by x264 against today's encoder, and one decode feeding three encodes. Its two renders at once were refused by the renderer's own lock.
- **bench3** (`bench3/result.txt`, one ticket, 04:34 to 04:53 CST), on the same span and on four segments of a film's narration: x264 at four rate factors and three presets for the 1080p film and at three rate factors for the derived UHD picture, an edition's derived passes at once, two renders at once with the renderer's own lock off, and narration in one, two and four processes.
- **The film trial** (`fk/r36/film/`, three tickets, 10:51 to 12:42 CST): the essentials film, 10 minutes, at its tag 1.0.2, built from narration to verified films with this Change's engine at `17cac49` and compared with the release's films by the engine's reproduction.
- **bench4** (`bench4/result.txt`, one ticket, 12:59 to 13:19 CST): the English edition of that film, eight segments and 511 s of speech, narrated in 1, 2, 4, 2, 1 and 3 processes back to back through the engine's own path, then both editions at once with one process each; the GPU's utilization read during each arm.
- **bench5** (`bench5/result.txt`, one ticket, 13:47 to 14:13 CST): whole-film renders of that film's captioned stages, 36,000 UHD frames each, back to back: the Chinese stage alone at 8 and at 16 workers, and both stages at once at 8 workers each, with the renderer's own phases.

One small measurement of this Change is cited where it is used: a synthetic clip derived at niceness 15 (`fk/r36/evidence/ground-ripple.txt`).

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A build's time is read from records, stage by stage, with one command.
- The 25-minute film rebuilds in 90 minutes or less, by the time of the build that command prints (decision 12). This Change removes one of an edition's two renders and its every-frame comparison, narrates in several processes, and takes the 1080p encodes off the hardware encoder; each saving is to be measured on a film's build with the durations command before the budget is called met.
- A film's task list does not change, and a film that needs the native pair can still have it.

What the Change gives a build, from the records of 17 November 2026 (the film trial, bench4 and bench5; the engine before it from the same film's rebuild proof and its 1.0.2 build), for the 10-minute film, both editions: narration about 280 s in three processes where it took 379 s; four renders of about 355 s become two, with two derivations of 88 s; two every-frame comparisons of 180 s become two comparisons by record of 3 s; packaging 183 s where it took 545 s; verification 73 s where it took 67 s, no gain measured; the sound stages and audits about two minutes in both. About 26 minutes where the engine before needs about 48. Projected for the 25-minute film, stage by stage from the measurement of 16 November (175 minutes in all): narration in three processes 24 to 33 minutes where it took 37 to 50; two renders 30.5 where four took 61; two derivations about 7, untimed at that length; the comparisons by record under 1 where they took 30; packaging about 7.5 where it took 22; verification 8; sound and audits 10. That is 88 to 96 minutes: the budget of 90 is reached only at the narration's faster end, and not with a margin. The render, 30 of those minutes, is the lever left. This is a projection until that film is built with the engine. The Change's first projection, 66 to 78 minutes, rested on span measurements that whole films did not bear out, and is withdrawn (decisions 2, 8 and 10).

**Non-Goals:**

- A one-scene change in 20 minutes: it needs span renders and a segment cache, a later slice.
- Any change to what a frame shows. The captioned picture is rendered as before; only the worker count of its render changes, which changes no frame.
- New review thresholds.

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, and where it applies.

1. **Durations are recorded beside the evidence, never inside a record that another record binds.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review, whose phase splits had to be read from file times ("the sampled pass 219 and 196 s, the every-frame scan 675 and 678 s"), and on how the engine binds records: a stage record binds the assembly record by SHA-256, the film package binds the comparison record, and the readiness receipt binds every record it judged. A duration differs from run to run.
   - **Considered:** (a) the seconds inside each command's own record, as first proposed; (b) one ledger file for a build; (c) one small duration record for each command and edition, beside the edition's evidence.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). With (a), running `check-picture` or `compare-pictures` again on unchanged inputs would change the bytes of a record that a receipt or a package binds, and a harmless repeat would make the next step stale. With (b), two commands of one edition that end together would write one file. Each record of (c) has one writer and binds nothing.
   - **Applies to:** every action of `filmkit.produce` and `narrate`, and since decision 12 the tools run from the command line, the verifier and the reproduction. The render receipt keeps its `elapsed_seconds`. A phase is named by the owner of the work: the comparison's validation, composition report, sampled frames and every-frame scan; each film of a package; each audit of `check-picture`; each step of `narrate`; the verifier's bindings, media and Spotlight checks. The verifier and the reproduction also state each film's seconds in their reports.
2. **The render's worker count is eight, as before this Change, and can be named for a measurement.**
   - **Rests on:** bench5, whole-film renders of a film's captioned stage, 36,000 UHD frames, back to back in one ticket (17 November 2026, 13:47 to 14:13 CST, a host of 18 cores on which other processes used 3.1 to 3.7 cores throughout): alone with 8 workers 354.9 s, the capture taking 350 s and the encoder keeping pace with it; alone with 16 workers 438.4 s, the capture done in 255 s and the encoder then needing 179 s more. The renderer's workers capture frames and one encoder reads them. bench1 and bench2 had measured a 120 s span: 16 workers took 114.1 and 118.0 s where 8 took 126.2 to 141.2 s, every picture identical frame for frame, and on that reading this Change first took the count from the host, the logical cores less two within 8 to 16, which the film trial then rendered with.
   - **Considered:** (a) that host's count, 16 on the host measured; (b) 8, as the engine passed before, a named constant; (c) 10 or 12, which no whole film has been rendered with; (d) the renderer's `auto`; (e) a value in the film's declaration, which would copy a host's number into every film.
   - **Chosen because:** (b). On a whole film sixteen workers are 24 % slower than eight: they capture a quarter faster and starve the one encoder. The span had said the opposite because a span that starts 300 s into a film pays a start that hides the encoder's lag. Whether a count between the two is faster than eight is not known and is named among the later slices. The count changes no frame, which bench1 did establish, so it stays the engine's setting and not the film's.
   - **Applies to:** the pictures and the draft. `FILMKIT_RENDER_WORKERS` names another count for a measurement, a value that is no count is refused, and the receipt records the count, so a measurement states what it ran with. One host and one film are measured.
3. **The renderer is qualified once for each supply, in the sample build, by the measure of a rendered pair.**
   - **Rests on:** what the per-build comparison guards against. Its every-frame scan was added for the one-frame faults of 10 to 12 November, which were traced to GPU rasterization; since 0.20.0 the films draw in the software rasterizer. bench1's six renders of one span, each alone, at four worker counts and two capture paths, decoded identically, and the qualification first required that: every decoded frame of a second render equal to the first's. Whole films of 17 November 2026 show that two renders of one stage do not always decode alike although the frames given to the encoder are the same. The film trial's Chinese captioned picture, rendered beside the English one, stands 66.2 dB on average and 52.4 dB at the lowest frame from bench5's render of the same stage alone. The trial's reproduction report finds its UHD captioned films differing from the release's in 34,673 of 36,000 frames in Chinese and 710 in English, each above the floor of 45 dB. The ETHOS film's rebuild proof finds six of eight films decoding to the release's frames in all 54,000 and two at 50.1 and 53.0 dB at the lowest frame. An observation and no cause: the renders of that essentials stage which ran alone wrote 273,224,534 bytes each, bench5's two with different SHA-256, their frames not compared, and the two which ran beside another render wrote 273,209,975 and 273,098,026; the renderer's hardware encode appears stable alone and to vary when the encoder is shared, a pattern in five renders. The sample's qualification of 60 seconds passed with 0 of 3,600 frames differing because nothing shared the encoder. What the engine already measures of a rendered pair: on the three films' current comparison records, six pairs under `software` capture read on 17 November, 360,000 frame pairs hold no fault frame and no differing frame.
   - **Considered:** (a) keeping two native renders and the every-frame comparison in every film build (30 of 61 render minutes and all 30 comparison minutes for the 25-minute film), or dropping the proof; (b) proving it at the engine's release, where the renderer, the browser and the capture are pinned, by identical decoded frames, as first built; (c) the same proof by the engine's own measure of a rendered pair: the sampled frames against the tile limit and the PSNR floor, and the scan of every frame for one-frame faults, lasting differences and edge slivers; (d) a software encode inside the renderer, which would make two renders byte-stable and let identity be required again, the open measurement named among the later slices.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). The fault the qualification guards against belongs to the renderer supply, which only an engine release changes, and the sample build runs for every release. What it is for is that the renderer draws a stage without a one-frame fault that only a twin would show; identical frames ask more than that, of the encoder, and a whole film or a busy host would be refused for the encoder's variation. The comparison of a rendered pair is the measure the films' builds passed for every release so far, with its thresholds set on their pictures, so one function owns "two pictures of one stage, as encoded" and its thresholds, and both `compare-pictures` and the qualification call it. Two renders are both captioned, so no row is left out: the caption band is part of what must agree. How many frames decode identically stays in the record as a fact that decides nothing.
   - **Applies to:** `qualify-renderer` under any capture, in the sample build and where a film runs it on its own stage, and `filmkit.picture.equivalence.compare_renders`, which compares two picture files outside an edition. Films that declare `software` capture rest on it; a film of another capture keeps its two renders and the every-frame comparison in every build, without asking (decision 11), and an owner who wants the proof for an edition under `software` runs `render-picture --uncaptioned --native` and `compare-pictures`, which then compares at every frame as before. The pair that `fast` capture drew on 17 November, 171 frames differing by at most 4 levels, passes this measure, and that is right: the measure asks whether a render holds a fault, and no capture but `software` derives, a rule that rests on which capture a release qualifies and on November's faults, not on a refusal of the qualification (decision 11). Not proven: the measure on a real pair of whole-film renders that differ by the encoder, which is to be run on the trial's and the rebuild proof's pictures of one stage.
4. **The uncaptioned picture is the captioned picture with its band painted.**
   - **Rests on:** bench1: derived by one x264 encode (veryfast, CRF 14) in 17.8 s against 141.2 s for the native render of the span, 10.1 MB against 18.9 MB; the band identical to the native uncaptioned render's in all 7,200 frames; above the band 59.0 dB on average and 52.1 dB at the lowest frame from the native render, where the two native renders stand 64.8 and 56.1 dB apart and the engine's floor for a compared frame is 45 dB. VideoToolbox derived the same picture in 97.5 s.
   - **Considered:** (a) the native second render; (b) the renderer's `--variables` batch, which still renders every frame twice; (c) a lossless intermediate from which both pictures are encoded, which changes the captioned picture's path too; (d) painting the band of the captioned picture and encoding once.
   - **Chosen because:** (d) is the only one that removes the second render, and it leaves the captioned picture, the one every review reads, untouched. The paint is the ground as the captioned picture itself encodes it, read from one of its frames in YUV and laid over the band's rows without a colour conversion; the measurements painted by RGB colour, which matched that film's ground and need not match another's. The rate factor is 12: bench3 derived the span at rate factors 14, 12 and 10 in 20.7, 21.0 and 22.8 s, at 59.5, 60.7 and 61.7 dB on average above the band from the native render and 52.1, 53.5 and 52.6 dB at the lowest frame, so 12 is a decibel closer than 14 in the same time. The time of this form of the paint is to be measured in the sample build.
   - **Applies to:** a film whose captions stand on a flat ground that nothing else draws on, which decision 5 proves for each derivation. The derived picture is a second encode of the frames above the band; a film that wants its uncaptioned master as a first encode asks for `--native`.
5. **A derivation proves that the band holds only captions on the ground and no one-frame fault, in the work it does anyway.**
   - **Rests on:** what the every-frame comparison proved for a native pair, that the pictures differ by the captions alone. For a derived picture the frames above the band are the captioned picture's by construction, so what remains to prove is about the band: that the composition paints nothing but captions there, and that what the paint replaces is the ground.
   - **Considered:** (a) decoding both pictures again after the encode, which is the comparison this Change removes; (b) the composition's report at every half second alone, which a mark shown for a third of a second passes; (c) a headless pass at every thirtieth of a second, the frame audit's own step, with a measure of the captioned band taken in the encode's decode.
   - **Chosen because:** (c) costs no decode. The headless pass refuses a caption paint that leaves the painted rows and any other paint that reaches them, and lists the stretches without a caption and the rows the captions reached. The encode's filter graph takes two measures of the captioned picture, each the lowest and highest sample of each plane, and the derivation refuses a departure of more than 3 levels from the ground, naming `--native`: of the whole band at every frame of those stretches, less two audit steps at each end, and of the edge rows at every frame of the film. The edge rows are up to 16 picture rows at each side of the painted rows, inside the margin by which the band is widened, so no caption reaches them. A caption or a mark departs by tens to hundreds of levels; the encoder's ripple in a flat band reached 2 levels below busy synthetic content and none on bench1's film span. Run read-only on the three films' six current editions at niceness 15, the headless pass took 7 to 21 s and passed each, and listed 18.8 to 127.4 s without a caption in 9 to 160 stretches (`fk/r36/films.log`).
   - **Applies to:** every derivation. The edge rows were added after the independent review of this Change (finding 3) derived a clip whose whole ground was another colour on exactly the frames under a caption: the first form of the check, which read only frames without a caption, passed it, and the derived frames held the band 24 levels from the rows above it. The edge rows refuse that clip at its first such frame, which both ground checks name, with the largest departure and its frame beside it, where they first named the frame that departed most (the picture review of `95f2205..17cac49`, finding 4: for a fade the peak and not where it begins), and with it a scene with a ground of its own, a fade over the whole frame and a plate under a caption that is taller than the band. Each side of the painted rows beyond which the picture continues must have edge rows: a derivation with edge rows on one side alone went on, and a plate under a caption that left the painted rows on the unmeasured side stayed in the derived picture where the rest was painted over (finding 5 of that review; contrived, and none of the three films is near it, their captions standing in rows 956 to 984 with 16 edge rows at each side). Painted rows that end with the frame need none on that side, no picture row lying beyond it. Still not proven: a change of the ground confined to the rows a caption covers, at frames that show the caption, since the composition lays its ground by `fillRect`, which no headless tool sees; the derived picture would paint over it. None of the three films draws one: each lays one whole-frame ground in one colour. What a derivation proves has three parts since the band's one-frame scan: the band audit, the ground in the band and its edge rows, and that scan. Before this Change `compare-pictures` judged each picture's caption band at every frame by its own neighbouring frames, a sample more than 90 levels from both while those agree within 30 being a one-frame fault, the kind of November's glyph fragments; a derived pair's comparison decodes nothing, so the captioned picture's band, the text every viewer reads and the band all four films carry, was scanned by nothing. That rule needs no twin: the derivation's decode now hands the captioned picture's band rows of every frame, as the scan of a rendered pair decodes them, to the same rule (`faults.Band`, its two levels not copied), and a one-frame change refuses the derivation before the derived picture is put in place, naming each frame, time and box as the comparison's findings do and the command that renders the captioned picture again, since the fault is that picture's and a native uncaptioned render would leave it in the captioned films. The receipt's `derived` gains `band_scan`, validation requires it passed, and the comparison record of a derived pair carries it. Its cost, measured light on 17 November 2026 (`fk/r39/evidence/band-scan-cost.txt` in the films' kit): on a clip of 360 frames at 640 by 360 the derivation's pass took 0.19 s with the band's output and 0.18 s without it, the derived picture byte for byte the same; the judging alone, on synthetic planes of a UHD band of 3840 by 176 with no decode, took 0.10 ms a frame on one core, about 4 s for 36,000 frames and 9 s for 90,000. The band's rows at UHD, 0.68 MB a frame through a pipe, and their conversion are not timed at that size; the duration record of `render-picture --uncaptioned` names the judging as the phase `band-scan`, which lies inside `encode`. What the twin comparison proved outside the band, the qualification of each supply replaces (decision 3).
6. **A derived pair is compared by its record.**
   - **Rests on:** decision 5: the proofs are made when the picture is derived and recorded in its receipt.
   - **Considered:** a separate command for the derived pair; leaving `compare-pictures` out of a derived build.
   - **Chosen because:** packaging and the verifier ask for one comparison record of every edition, and a film's task list should not depend on how its uncaptioned picture was made. `compare-pictures` runs the composition report as for any pair, requires the derivation's receipt to bind the current captioned picture, composition, timeline and engine with its proofs passed and the same band, and writes the same record with the pair marked derived.
   - **Applies to:** a pair whose uncaptioned receipt says derived. A natively rendered pair is compared at every frame, and `repair-pictures` serves that pair alone.
7. **The 1080p films are encoded by x264 on a forced keyframe grid.** This replaces the draft's decision 16 (encode again after a keyframe refusal).
   - **Rests on:** six refusals of a 1080p film on its keyframes since 13 November, at host loads from 8 to 120, with no cause found. The sixth, on 17 November at 03:34, held the grid to 102.0 s, then set keyframes at 102.85, 104.85, 105.517 and 105.967 s and every 2 s from there, 251 of 303 off the grid; another heavy step of the same session started on the host in that minute, which is one timed coincidence and not a cause, and the refusal of 16 November has none beside it. Each refusal ended a film's build, cost a host ticket's wait and needed a restart by hand: the hardware encoder's cadence is not guaranteed under this host's load. bench1: VideoToolbox is the slower encoder at UHD (74 frames a second) and x264 the faster. x264 places a keyframe exactly every `-g` frames when `-keyint_min` equals it and scene-cut detection is off.
   - **Considered:** a retry by the packager, which keeps a refusal whose cause is unknown; a VideoToolbox option that pins the cadence, of which none is documented; one decode of the captioned picture feeding an edition's three derived encodes.
   - **Chosen because:** a software encoder's keyframes depend on its options alone, and x264 is also the faster encoder here. The criterion for the rate, set before bench3 ran, was the lowest frame not under VideoToolbox's at the smallest time. bench2 and bench3, on the span against its area-scaled source, as seconds, megabytes, and decibels on average and at the lowest frame: VideoToolbox as the engine ran it 23.7 s, 23.9 MB, 63.4 and 51.8 dB; x264 `veryfast` at rate factor 16, 7.8 s, 4.8 MB, 56.4 and 48.7 dB; at 14, 7.4 s, 5.7 MB, 58.2 and 50.3 dB; at 12, 8.1 s, 6.7 MB, 59.8 and 51.8 dB; at 10, 9.5 s, 7.8 MB, 61.4 and 53.3 dB; `faster` at 14, 11.4 s, 60.0 and 51.3 dB; `medium` at 14, 11.9 s, 61.3 and 51.5 dB; every x264 output with all 60 keyframes on the grid. Rate factor 12 meets the criterion by 0.01 dB on this one span, which is no margin; 10 stands 1.5 dB over it for 1.4 s more a span, about 17 s for a 25-minute film, at a third of VideoToolbox's size, and is faster and closer than the slower presets at 14. The settings are named constants: preset `veryfast`, rate factor 10, `-g 120 -keyint_min 120 -sc_threshold 0`, the area scale as before, and since decision 13 no B-frames, which these measurements held. One decode feeding the three derived encodes took 43.6 s in bench2, slower than the three as separate passes in a row, so each derived film is its own pass that reads the captioned picture; the 1080p uncaptioned film of a derived pair is painted and scaled from it in that pass, one encode from the render and not two.
   - **Applies to:** both 1080p films of an edition, whose derived passes run in a row and never at once: bench3 ran an edition's three derived films as three processes at once in 51.8 s, where the three in a row take about 36 s (20.7, 7.4 and about 8 s), and two editions' six at once in 79.4 s against about 72 s, since each pass already uses every core. The figures are one span of one film; a film whose 1080p film falls under its floor in a rebuild comparison is the evidence that would move the constant.
8. **Independent checks run side by side; an edition's pictures render one after the other.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review: `check-picture`'s audits share no file and each is one single-threaded loop (46 + 87 s and 83 + 102 s on 16 November, before the detail audit's 40 s); the verifier decodes eight films in sequence in 482 s. For renders, whole films of 36,000 UHD frames (the film trial and bench5, 17 November 2026): one stage alone at 8 workers 354.9 s; both stages at once, the renderer's own lock off, 739.8 s at 8 workers each and 862 s at 16 each; and a picture rendered beside another decodes 66.2 dB on average and 52.4 dB at the lowest frame from the same stage rendered alone, the renderer's hardware encode not being the same from run to run on a whole film though the frames it is given are. bench3 had rendered a 120 s span's two stages at once in 172.3 s where two in sequence took 228 to 236 s, and on that reading this Change first shipped a command, `render-picture --language all`, that rendered both editions' pictures at once.
   - **Considered:** a general worker pool in the engine; side-by-side runs only where the work is read-only or writes files of its own; for the two editions' renders, one command that starts each edition's own render as a child with the renderer's lock off for those two, which was built, measured on whole films and not taken.
   - **Chosen because:** the three audits and the films of a verification meet that condition, and each keeps its own report, refusal and order. Two renders at once do not pay: no gain at eight workers each (739.8 s against twice 354.9 s), a fifth lost at sixteen each, a different encode, and a second path of process management to keep right, in which the picture review of `95f2205..17cac49` found a renderer left running after an interrupt in 19 of 24 trials. The span had shown a gain because a pair shares the fixed start that each single span render pays. The command, its module and the receipt's `render_lock` field are removed; nothing in the engine sets the renderer's lock.
   - **Applies to:** `check-picture` (three audits at once, the whole-turn check after the frame audit) and the verifier (up to four films at once), measured on one host of 18 cores. The two editions' packages share one file, the entry page `build/artifacts/film/index.html`, which each writes and then binds by SHA-256; nothing packages two editions at once today, and the page is written whole by replacement, as records are, so that a packaging of the other edition can never hash half of it.
9. **Small savings that change no result.**
   - **Rests on:** the static review's changes 9, 10 and 11: transcription has no cache and transcribed 30 recordings in 43 to 48 s when 3 were new; both phases of a still scan select the same frames of a draft and decode it twice; the reproduction compares its film pairs in sequence.
   - **Considered:** leaving them, as seconds against minutes.
   - **Chosen because:** each repeats on every iteration of a film's work, and each is exact: a transcript is a function of the recording, the pinned model's weights and the speech library that runs them, all of which its cache names, the scan's frames are the same frames, and a pair's comparison reads nothing another pair writes.
   - **Applies to:** `narrate`, the still scan and the reproduction. The static review's changes 1, 2 and 8 speed up the every-frame comparison, which a film's build no longer runs; they are named among the later slices. A cached transcript is filed under the recording, the language and the recognizer's identity: its hub repository and revision, its weights' SHA-256 and the versions of the two libraries that run it, `mlx-audio` and `mlx`, the array library that does the arithmetic and that the lock pins apart; a transcript by other weights or another version of either is a miss and is made again. The tokenizer's and the array types' libraries are pinned by the same lock and are not named, and the alignment cache, which this Change does not alter, names its aligner's revision and weights and no library. Every transcription checks the supplied recognizer against its pins before it reads a cache, which reads the 1.6 GB of weights and took 0.6 to 0.9 s in four readings on 17 November 2026; without that check a run that found every transcript cached would never look at the recognizer its cache names. A cached transcript or alignment that cannot be read as one, because the file is no JSON object or lacks a field of its form, is a miss: the recording is transcribed or aligned again, the entry is replaced and one printed line says so, where it ended every run with a traceback until someone deleted the file (the review of `95f2205..17cac49`, narration, finding 9). An entry that reads whole and binds another recording, text, recognizer or aligner than its place names is still refused, since that is no damage to repair. Assumed and not measured: that the recognizer gives one transcript for a recording, run after run.
10. **Narration divides its segments among processes of the speech model.**
    - **Rests on:** the static review: synthesis is 558 to 787 s of a 30-segment narration, and every thought is generated from its own request and seed into files named by its own fingerprint. bench4, on the eight segments of a film's English edition (511 s of speech), the arms back to back so that drift shows: one process 180.7 and 176.1 s, two processes 153.6 and 143.8 s, three 117.2 s, four 126.2 s; both editions at once with one process each, 229.5 s for both. All 64 takes of its eight runs equal the film's own byte for byte. The GPU read 75 % busy with one process and 87 to 88 % with more: one process leaves it a quarter idle, more fill it, and each take slows as they share it. Each process holds about 4.6 GB at its peak (bench3). What did not stand: bench3 had measured two processes at 1.8 times one on four segments (99.8 s against 55.3 s), and its one-process arm ran slow, one take at 47.9 s that the delivered receipt records at 18.8 s; that baseline was measured once, with nothing watching the GPU. And the film trial's English narration in two processes took 243.3 s, where bench4's two-process arms took 153.6 and 143.8 s: a local model server on the host answered 52 requests inside that run, on the GPU the narration shares with work no queue sees.
    - **Considered:** (a) a pool of model workers with a protocol between them and the narration; (b) the vendor's batched generation, which changes what is sampled and so the takes; (c) children of the synthesis itself, each given its segments by the `--ids` the command already takes and a receipt of its own, after which the synthesis takes its ordinary path over retained recordings. For the count: two processes, 17 % less time than one, measured twice; three, 34 % less, measured once; four, 29 % less, measured once; both editions at once with one process each, 229.5 s for both, which is no better than three processes within each edition in a row.
    - **Chosen because:** (c) adds no protocol and no second writer of the edition's receipt. A child is the command a person runs for a retake, so its recordings, attempts and refusals are the ones the engine already judges, and a child that fails leaves what an interrupted run always left; the narration then refuses, naming the child's segments, its log and the last five lines it wrote, since what a library prints as a process ends can stand after the exception, and the next run reuses what the other children retained. The children's receipts and logs stand in a work folder that no other step reads. Every synthesis for a receipt clears that folder when it begins, whether it then divides or narrates in its own process, and leaves its own work there when it ends, pass or fail. The first form cleared it only when it divided and removed it after a pass: a run of one segment after a failed run left the failed run's logs and a child's receipt of segments beside a later run's, and a passing run threw away the only place where each new segment's record line stood, while the narration's own output called every segment it had just generated `reused` (finding 6 of the review of `95f2205..17cac49`). The narration now reports such a segment as `generated`, with the number of the process that made it. Segments go to the processes by the length of their spoken text, the longest first to the least loaded, since the longest segment bounds the run. The count comes from the host: one process for every 16 GB of its memory, at least one and at most three, read once by the standard library, and one where it cannot be read; `FILMKIT_NARRATION_PROCESSES` names another, and 1 keeps the single process. The two languages stay two commands in a row, since both at once gained no more than three processes within one. Three and four processes were each measured once, on one host and one edition; the narration's duration record keeps `narration_processes`, so every build shows what its count gave.
    - **Applies to:** the synthesis whenever more than one segment is to be generated; one segment stays in the narration's own process. Each process loads the model, so three need about 14 GB of memory at their peaks. The narration prints each process's segments, exit status and seconds; the seconds are that process's own, taken when it is found ended, where the first form waited for the processes in their order and gave each one that ended sooner the time of a slower one before it, so that every division looked balanced (finding 4 of that review). The processes' work is filed by the receipt's whole path from the film's root. The first form took the last six parts of the path, which for a receipt outside the root and near the top of the filesystem began with `/` and sent the folder to the filesystem's root (the review of `95f2205..17cac49`, narration, finding 3). The synthesis now refuses a recordings folder or a receipt outside the root before it reads or writes anything. It did not refuse either before: with one process, a recordings folder outside the root got a thought's request and recording written there and then a traceback from the first path taken from the root, and a receipt outside the root was written.

12. **The printed sum is one build's, and the time of a build counts commands that ran together once.**
    - **Rests on:** the independent review of `ecbc2d2..95f2205` (finding 6) and the first sample build's folder, which listed thirteen records and none of the geometry check. The sum left out commands of the build: the geometry check, a browser pass over every second frame, and the cover check, which a film's task list runs through `filmkit.tools`; the verification, 8 minutes of the 175; the reproduction. It took in every command ever run in the folder, since a record is the latest run of its command: a repair or a retake of last week counted beside today's build. And commands of one build may run at the same time, two editions' work started side by side by a caller: their records add up to more than the build took, as the film trial's did, whose two editions' pictures rendered at once (the counted commands ran for 1,882.9 s and their seconds add up to 2,739.7 s).
    - **Considered:** for what is recorded, (a) the verifier's seconds added by hand when a budget is judged, which leaves the figure to the reader's care; (b) records from the verifier, the reproduction and the tools, the verifier's being the first file it ever writes. For which records are a build's, (c) the earliest and latest start printed, so that a stale record shows and the reader subtracts; (d) a build identifier passed to every command, which every task list would have to carry; (e) the records that started at or after the edition's latest whole narration, the first heavy command of a build, or at or after a time the caller names. For the figure, (f) the sum of the commands' seconds alone; (g) beside it the seconds during which at least one counted command was running.

(e) needs nothing of a task list and is right for a build from narration on; a build that keeps its narration names its start with `--since`. (g) is what a budget means: the time a build took, in which two commands that ran at the same time count once.
    - **Applies to:** `python -m filmkit.durations`. Each edition's records are listed in the order they started; a record that started before the build is marked as earlier and left out; the sum's line says since when it counts and how many records it leaves out. The last line gives the time of the build over the selected editions and, beside it, the sum of the counted commands' seconds; the two are equal for commands run in a row. 14. **A command's children end with it.**
    - **Rests on:** the review of `95f2205..17cac49` (narration, finding 2): a synthesis that was sent SIGTERM ended and left both of its children running under process 1, since nothing stopped a child when the wait was cut short or a later child could not be started. A child holds a speech model of about 4.6 GB and the GPU and narrates to the end of its share; a narration started again meanwhile starts two more and is refused at the thought an orphan is in. The films' kits start `narrate` detached and end it by its process id, and a host ticket ends the process it owns at its deadline. The renderer's own stop was a second piece of such code.
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving it to the caller to signal the process group, which a caller that knows one process id does not do; (b) ending the children directly when the wait is cut short, each in the narration's own process group; (c) each child the leader of a session of its own, its whole process group ended, as the engine ends a renderer; (d) a child that watches for its parent and ends itself, which would reach a parent killed outright and adds a second mechanism to every child.
    - **Chosen because:** (c), in one small module, `filmkit.child_processes`, that the narration's processes, `narrate`'s steps and every run of a renderer use. Ending a group ends what the child itself started. A request to end is turned into an exception while children run, so one path serves an interrupt, a request to end and a failure of the command itself, and the command then refuses, naming which it was. From the first request to end until the command has ended its children and exits, every further request is taken and dropped: the handler stays in place for the whole of that time, the stop lies inside the block that takes the requests, and a command that leaves ignores them from its one line to its exit. The first form restored the default action when the exception left the block and took the signals again only when the stop began: a second SIGTERM in the 50 to 230 microseconds between the two ended the command at once, with every child running (the review of `17cac49..4b50c34`, finding 2: 9 of 54 trials for the narration, 4 of 50 for a renderer), and one that arrived during the stop was held and then ended the command by its default action, without its refusal. `narrate` itself sends that second request: asked to end through its process group, it relays the request to its step, which the group's signal has reached already. The command then says in one line what stopped it and exits with 128 and the signal's number, 130, 143 or 129, as a shell reports a command that the signal ended; a refusal exits with 1 as before. Considered for the exit: ending by the signal itself once the children are gone, which gives a caller the same status and no chance of a line after the last child; and exit 1 with the refusal, the first form, which a caller cannot tell from a failed check.
    - **Applies to:** the synthesis while its processes run, which ends their process groups with SIGTERM and kills what has not ended after 30 s; `narrate`, which asks its running step to end and waits up to 60 s for it, so that the synthesis has the time to end its own children, and records the run as not completed. A hang-up (SIGHUP), which a terminal that closes or a session that drops sends to the foreground process group, is taken as a request to end is, unless the process ignores it, as under nohup: before the children led sessions of their own the hang-up reached them with the narration, and with their own sessions it ended the narration alone and left every child narrating under process 1 (the review of `17cac49..4b50c34`, finding 1, three trials of three). Not covered: a command killed outright (SIGKILL) runs none of this, and since each narration process now leads its own session, a signal to the narration's process group no longer reaches the children either, so a caller ends a narration by asking it to end and waiting. Every run of a renderer, for a picture, a draft, a repair or a qualification, goes through the same module: a request to end is an exception while the renderer runs, so SIGTERM takes the path an interrupt takes, the renderer's process group is sent SIGTERM and, after the grace of 10 s, SIGKILL, and the command refuses, naming the request. Before, a render sent SIGTERM ended at once and left its renderer rendering in its own session, and a second interrupt during the grace cut the stop short before SIGKILL (the picture review of `95f2205..17cac49`, findings 3 and 1). Which signal a caller's deadline sends, `ethos host run`'s or `mise`'s, and to which processes, that review could not determine; both SIGINT and SIGTERM are handled for that reason. Only a child that has not been reaped is ever signalled, and it is reaped last: a reaped child's number is no longer its own, and the kernel may have given it to another process since. The narration handed its stop every child of the run, also one that the wait had reaped minutes before; for such a child the stop asked whether a process group of its number existed and, if one did, sent it SIGTERM and after 30 s SIGKILL (the review of `17cac49..4b50c34`, finding 4, the code path confirmed with a number set by hand; the host gave out about 140 process ids a second, so the range comes round in about 12 minutes). A renderer's end is therefore waited for without reaping it, by a kernel queue on macOS, where Python 3.12 has no `waitid`, and what is left of its group is ended after every run, a clean exit included, where before only a failed exit ended its remaining workers: the exit status is known only once the renderer is reaped, and it is reaped only when its group has ended. Considered: reaping the renderer first and watching its group by its number for the time of the stop, which the kernel keeps from any other process only while a member of the group lives; and reading the exit status from the queue's event, which is one more thing that only macOS does. Not run: the `waitid` path of a host without a kernel queue; on such a kernel an ended child that is not reaped may count as a member of its own group, and the wait for a group then runs its full time (the engine's hosts and its CI are macOS). Measured on stand-in children (17 November 2026, `tests/test_ending.py`): of 51 narrations and 34 renders sent SIGTERM twice, 0 to 10,000 microseconds apart, the first form left a child running in 3 and 4 and ended by the signal, without its line, in 19 and 19; this form in none. Two different requests that are both pending when the interpreter next looks are handed to it lowest number first, so an interrupt sent within that moment of a SIGTERM may be the one the command names; its status is then that one's.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A film's composition may draw in the band's rows. → The derivation refuses before it encodes and names `--native`; nothing is delivered from a refused derivation.
- A renderer supply may draw a one-frame fault under `software` capture. → The sample build's `qualify-renderer` compares two renders of one stage at every frame and refuses, and with it the release; a fault rarer than one in the sample's 3,600 frames is not met there, which is why the captioned picture's band is still scanned in every build and a film can ask for the native pair.
- The uncaptioned UHD master becomes a second-generation encode. → 59.0 dB from the native render on average on bench1's span, above the engine's own floor by 14 dB; `--native` stays.
- The rate factors rest on one span of one film, and the derivation's own form of the paint is not yet timed at UHD. → Named constants; the sample build and the verifier check every film's keyframes, and a rebuild comparison measures every frame against its floor.
- A setting of the build decided on a part of a film. → Three of this Change's settings were first decided on a 120 s span or on four segments, and whole films corrected each: the worker count (decision 2), two renders at once (decision 8) and the narration's processes (decision 10). The rule these corrections leave: a setting of the build is decided on the unit the build runs, a whole film, with its baseline repeated in the same ticket. The span measurements, bench1 to bench3, stay valid for what they compared frame for frame, that the worker count changes no frame, the derived picture against the native one and the x264 rates, and not for the wall time of whole films.
- Up to three speech models in memory at once. → About 4.6 GB each at the peak on the film measured; the count is one for every 16 GB of the host's memory, and `FILMKIT_NARRATION_PROCESSES=1` keeps one.
- The narration shares the GPU with work the host's queue does not see. → A model server beside it made two processes slower than one in the film trial; the duration record states the count and the seconds, so a build shows what its count gave, and the count can be named.
- Side-by-side verification reaches the 240 s limits of a packet listing sooner on a loaded host. → Four films at most; the limits refuse and do not corrupt.

## Migration Plan

An adopting film bumps the engine, keeps its tasks and builds: its uncaptioned picture is derived, its comparison record says so, its 1080p films are x264's. Its narration runs in as many processes as its host's memory gives, one for every 16 GB up to three, unless it names another count, with about 4.6 GB of memory for each. It may drop `stage-picture --uncaptioned` only if it never asks for `--native`; `preflight` still asks for both stages. A release rebuilt from an earlier tag keeps that tag's engine and its bytes.

## Open Questions

- Three and four narration processes were measured once each, on one host of 128 GB and one edition of eight segments. A film's build on another host, or of thirty segments, is the evidence that would move the rule.
- The render is now the largest part of a build. Open, each as one ticket with its baseline repeated in it: whole-film renders at 10 and 12 workers against 8; the renderer's own x264 encode, without `--gpu`, against its hardware encode; and its streaming encode with several workers.
- Segmented capture (`HF_SEGMENTED_CAPTURE=true`) changed nothing in bench2 (118.0 s, identical frames, no segment named in the log); whether it serves one-scene changes is untested and belongs to that later slice.
- Later slices: span renders and the segment cache; a faster every-frame comparison for the pairs that still ask for it (parallel time segments, the sampled measures off the scan's decode, band differences in 8 bits), which serves a path now run on demand and in the sample build only; a verification that does not decode again what the build proved, which changes what the verifier claims and is the owner's decision; the reviewers' measuring tools shipped by the engine.
