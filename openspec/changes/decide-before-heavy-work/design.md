- `check-picture` and `check-sound` write a gate record that binds the composition, timeline, stylesheet, fonts and engine by SHA-256, and `render-picture` reads the picture's record before it starts the renderer.
- Two classes of finding already have the form this Change generalizes: a wrapped label that breaks a rule and a motion finding are gated unless one of the film's declared exceptions names them with a reason, and an exception that matches nothing is gated as stale.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A finding that a reviewer would hold against a film is refused or declared before the first heavy step, by the engine, from tracked declarations.
- One owner answers "ready to render", and its answer can be checked again later without repeating its work.
- A film that declares nothing new and holds no such finding builds the same bytes.

**Non-Goals:**

- New thresholds. Each value stays where it is; the study says where it came from.
- Deciding for a film what to accept. The engine asks for a reason and an identity; the judgment is the film's and its reviewers'.
- Scheduling. Whether a ticket waits or runs is the host's.

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, and where it applies.

1. **Recorded findings take the form the wrapping and motion exceptions already have.**
   - **Rests on:** the films' records of 15 November 2026, which held recorded findings as counts in task lists and scripts; one English thought of the ETHOS film that stood 0.66 dB under its clarity floor after thirty-two takes, which its reviewers accepted as declared.
   - **Considered:** (a) counts in a film's task list or script; (b) gating every recorded class outright; (c) declaration by identity with a reason, stale ones refused.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). A count passes when one finding leaves and another arrives, and it lives outside the gate; an outright gate would refuse films that reviewers accepted with those findings declared. A declaration by identity is what a reviewer reads, and a stale one cannot linger.
   - **Applies to:** captions above the reading rate (by the cue's text), thoughts under the clarity floor (by thought), row gaps (by scene and the two names) and whatever class a later gate records. Not to the classes a spec defines as informational: thoughts that hold the previous picture within the limit, and cues mostly but not wholly drawn as labels.
2. **The row gap's floor is a declaration, not a flag.**
   - **Rests on:** all three films pass `--gate-row-gaps` today; one review asks 48 px at 1080p, about 1.63 em of the films' label face; the films probed 1.7 em with a copied check; the ETHOS film's geometry record held four pairs under 1.5 em while its task list did not pass the flag.
   - **Considered:** keeping `--gate-row-gaps` in each film's task list; a declared floor with a default.
   - **Chosen because:** a flag is forgotten once per film; a floor in `content/film.toml` is read by the same check everywhere.
   - **Applies to:** every film, default 1.5 em. Breaking only for a film that holds pairs under 1.5 em undeclared.
3. **`preflight` grows; no second readiness tool.**
   - **Rests on:** this repository's rule of one owner per question of behavior; the films' readiness script, which repeated part of `preflight`, missed a binding it knew (the stage binds the assembly record) and, on 15 November, refused a packaged edition by asking the render's question after the packaging.
   - **Considered:** (a) a new `ready` command beside `preflight`; (b) each film keeping a script; (c) extending `preflight`.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). The questions already have their owner in `preflight`; a second tool repeats them and drifts from them.
   - **Applies to:** the readiness of every heavy step, each question asked for the step it admits: before a render, a film package record that binds the current picture is one the render replaces; after the packaging, the same record is the next step's input. The draft scan becomes a declaration because the films replaced drafts by the every-frame audits of `check-picture`; a film that declares drafts keeps today's behavior.
4. **The receipt binds inputs by digest and is checked without repeating the work.**
   - **Rests on:** the host's admission queue, which admits a ticket when the slot frees and has no room there for minutes of audits; the films' receipts of 15 November, checked by their digests before each ticket.
   - **Considered:** re-running `preflight` before each ticket; a receipt with the SHA-256 of each judged input and a cheap check that they still hold.
   - **Chosen because:** the receipt is what a queue can ask for at admission and what a later reader can verify.
   - **Applies to:** the engine, which writes and checks the receipt; admitting a ticket on it is the host's. Both editions are judged for a render of either, since they share the composition.
5. **A text review is a record, and requiring it is the film's declaration.**
   - **Rests on:** the DDWG film's edition 1.3.1, whose changed thoughts were narrated before an independent recheck passed the text, so that two of them were narrated two and three times.
   - **Considered:** leaving the order to the film's process; a record that `narrate` can require.
   - **Chosen because:** the engine cannot judge a recheck's independence, but it can refuse narration of scripts that no recorded verdict covers.
   - **Applies to:** films that declare it. Opt-in because the sample film and a film in its first draft narrate before any review exists.
6. **The script editor reports the picture's words it leaves behind; the film's own tests remain the proof.**
   - **Rests on:** the DDWG film's staged audits of 15 November, which refused both editions at once ("Missing spoken cue") after narration and a runtime fit, because scene modules still named words the narration no longer said.
   - **Considered:** (a) only the film's picture tests, which paint every scene from the script and fail on a missing cue; (b) a report from the editor listing quoted phrases of the changed segments' scene modules that the old text said and the new one does not.
   - **Chosen because:** both. The tests catch a cue; they do not catch a label that still shows the old wording.
   - **Applies to:** every script edit, as a list for the author and not a gate, because a label may deliberately keep a condensed wording.
7. **A retake is compared on the soundtrack with the previous edition's take.**
   - **Rests on:** two films in which the order of two takes changed between the take and the assembled soundtrack (an English retake read 0.18 dB above the bound take and 0.11 dB below it once assembled); three DDWG English retakes that read 0.4 to 0.7 dB under the takes release 1.3.0 delivered.
   - **Considered:** the picking measure on the take alone; the assembled soundtrack against the previous edition's, judged by the engine's own clarity measure.
   - **Chosen because:** differences under about 0.3 dB between takes lie within the measure, and only the soundtrack is what the viewer hears.
   - **Applies to:** a film that names a previous edition; the margin is the film's to declare, default 0.3 dB. A first edition has none.
8. **Caption breaks choose among allowed breaks by reading rate.**
   - **Rests on:** two films that wrote authored breaks by hand for four cues to bring them under 25 characters per second; one authored break that made a cue equal to a picture label.
   - **Considered:** authored breaks per cue in the script; the breaker weighing the rate itself.
   - **Chosen because:** both, the breaker first; authored breaks stay for the cases a rule cannot see, such as a cue that would equal a label.
   - **Applies to:** every edition's captions.
9. **A line split or cut short within one frame is a pop, and marks outside scenes are named.**
   - **Rests on:** the ETHOS film's review round 20 (zh-R20-02), where a reviewer found within one frame a 55 px gap in 03.2's path, three times, on the rendered master after two renders; the motion audit had passed it because no drawn piece left whole. A headless scan in the films' kit that matches each draw call's solid stroke pieces by length frame to frame found those three places on that build and none on the repair, and over both editions of the ETHOS and DDWG films finds no other outside a stage cut the scene declares.
   - **Considered:** leaving both to reviewers' scans of the rendered masters; a rule on each draw call's summed solid stroke length (it read a stroke drawn off gradually as a loss and listed 24 to 54 places per edition); a rule on the pieces themselves: a piece replaced by shorter ones losing 30 px or more in one frame, with no loss in the frames on either side, outside a declared stage cut.
   - **Chosen because:** the piece rule finds what the viewer sees, a line that opens or shortens at once, and passes what the viewer reads as drawing; it costs one headless pass per edition.
   - **Applies to:** solid strokes of scenes, as an audit finding of `check-picture`. Whether a chapter title should keep the pace limit is the film's decision; the engine's part is to say in the gate record that it is not judged.
10. **The thresholds' sources are written once, here.**
    - **Rests on:** the pace line, the row floor, the level steps between thoughts and the reading rate, each used by three films and each taken from a review record of one of them.
    - **Considered:** each film's design stating them; one study in this Change.
    - **Chosen because:** one place for a source keeps three films from citing three readings of it.
    - **Applies to:** the study, which names each value, the record it came from and its date, and what it does not cover. Its form follows what ETHOS provides for research documents when that is released; until then the facts stand in this design.
11. **A flow on a way that moves is judged at every phase of its clock.**
    - **Rests on:** the ETHOS film on 16 November 2026. Its first build of edition 2.0.1 passed the picture gate with a declared pace limit of 450 px/s. The owner then raised a pause floor of the Chinese voice, which moved every later word, and the staged audit of the same scenes refused one point of 05.2's flow at 456 px/s. A scan of that draw call at 60 fps over 22 phases of the flow's 2.2 s clock finds 464 px/s in both editions at the worst phase and 417 px/s at the phases the earlier timelines gave (the film's local evidence of that day): a point that rounds the bend of a link while the link's upward run shrinks under a rising stage. A flow's points stand where voice time puts them, and the audit judges the one phase the timeline gives.
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving it, since the next staged audit after a change of timing finds it within minutes, as it did; (b) a margin under the limit, reported as a warning; (c) the audit judging such a flow at every phase of its clock.
    - **Chosen because:** (c). With (a) a film that passes can be refused by a change that touches no picture, and the repair then comes after the sound is settled; (b) would not have caught this stretch, which stood 33 px/s under the limit at the phase the timeline gave. A flow on a fixed way moves at one pace at every phase, so only a flow whose way changes while it runs needs the sweep.
    - **Applies to:** flows drawn on a way that a glide carries or reshapes, in the motion audit of `check-picture`. What the sweep costs is not measured yet; it is measured before the Change's specs are written.
12. **A reading recorded for a place in the text moves with the text.**
    - **Rests on:** the DDWG film on 16 November 2026. `content/review/readings.json` records the part of speech of each English heteronym by segment and character offset. A text edit made 14.2 longer by 43 characters and left four keys of segment 14 behind. `filmkit.review.listening_plan` refuses such a key, rightly, but it first runs at review preparation: the refusal came after four renders, two comparisons, packaging and verification, and the repair was a commit of four keys. The readings check that runs with a text edit does not derive the plan. The same class, picture cues that named words an edit had removed, was among the causes of five refused staged audits the day before (decision 6).
    - **Considered:** (a) deriving the plan with every text edit and in the readiness check, as the films' kit does since that morning (four seconds); (b) the script editor carrying each key to the same occurrence of its term in the segment it rewrites, and naming the keys whose occurrence the edit removed or added; (c) keying a reading by thought and occurrence instead of by offset.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) with (a) in `preflight`. The editor holds the old and the new text of every segment it rewrites, so it can move what a person would otherwise move by hand, and `preflight` catches an edit made another way. (c) changes every film's file and still breaks when an edit adds an occurrence before the recorded one.
    - **Applies to:** the English heteronym readings, and any later record that names a place in the text by position.
13. **A stroke that rests part-drawn through a quiet is a finding, read from what the composition publishes.**
    - **Rests on:** the DDWG film's review round 41 of 16 November 2026 (finding Z41-1, minor, level 达 failed on it): a bracket that names all three layers of a diagram stood at three fifths of its way through the quiet after its thought and left with its stage, so the picture showed two layers where the voice had said three. Release 1.3.0 held the same stall. The film advances a drawn stroke in voice time, which rests in a quiet, and let it set off early only within its clause. A scan the film then wrote lists six such strokes in its Chinese edition and five in its English one, and none in the ETHOS film, whose stroke timing already bounds the start by the thought. The motion audit follows marks that move; a stroke that stops half made neither pops, halts at speed nor races.
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving it to each film's tests; (b) the audit inferring it from the painted strokes of consecutive frames, as the film's first scan did, following each stroke by its start and points; (c) the composition publishing, with the layout it already publishes for each frame, the share every drawn stroke has reached, and the audit reading that at the middle of every quiet between two thoughts.
    - **Chosen because:** (c). (b) could not tell a stroke caught mid-draw from the next step of a build that begins on the next words (a rail's further reach, a frame that deepens) without a second sample before the quiet, and still read by shape; the published share is exact and costs one field. (a) is where the films were when a reviewer found it.
    - **Applies to:** `check-picture`, for a composition that publishes its drawn strokes; one that does not is recorded as not judged. Both films publish `draws` (scene, cue, share and the tip's peak pace) since 16 November; the tip's pace lets the same check list a stroke drawn faster than a mark may move.
14. **A film declares what a single thought's clarity is judged against, and the gate and the prompt hold that line.**
    - **Rests on:** the same round (finding EN41-1, material): the reviewer measured every thought by the prompt's procedure and held all 160 to the floor the film declares, 17 dB, which its declaration says the measurement checks "on every segment"; fifteen thoughts read 16.34 to 16.99 dB, each of them declared in the film's design, while the soundtrack read 17.67 dB and every segment 17.30 dB or more. `check-sound` gates the soundtrack and the segments and records the thoughts. Measured that day on sixteen new takes each of fourteen of the fifteen thoughts and on 88 takes of 22 wordings of five of them (their own and seventeen rewordings): the film's thought medians have a median of 17.62 dB and a standard deviation of 0.61 dB; takes of one sentence vary by about 0.3 dB and a rewording moves a sentence's median by half a decibel at most; six thoughts have no take at the floor. A floor set for a soundtrack and held thought by thought therefore fails about one thought in seven, whatever the voice.
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving a thought unjudged by the gate, as now, so that what a reviewer holds is found only in review; (b) the segments' floor for every thought; (c) a declared line for a thought, the floor less a margin the film states, gated by `check-sound`, with the thoughts between that line and the floor listed, and the prompt's text printing both lines.
    - **Chosen because:** (c), as the mechanism. With (a) the film and its reviewer held different lines and learned it after a full build. (b) asks the text to change for a measure, sentence by sentence, with no end: eighteen further thoughts stand within 0.2 dB above the floor. The value of the margin is each film owner's, since the floor expresses the owner's rejection of a voice. The films' owner decided it on 16 November 2026 for the DDWG film and the ETHOS film: the floor holds for the soundtrack and each segment, a single thought is judged against the floor less 1.0 dB, and the thoughts between the two lines are listed as notes; both films' prompts carry that sentence by hand until the engine prints it. After thirteen retakes the DDWG film's list of thoughts under the floor went from fifteen to ten with four new names, which is what the spread predicts.
    - **Applies to:** `review.clarity_minimum_db`, the sound gate and the prompt. The estimate the films' kit uses to choose a take before assembly (a take scaled to the soundtrack's level and measured on the 16 grids, within about 0.15 dB of the assembled figure on five thoughts) is a candidate for `narration.audition`'s ranking.
15. **Names on a rail's two baselines are neighbours, and the row gap holds between them.**
    - **Rests on:** the same round (note Z41-3): where a dense rail sets every other name a row lower, long names reached under one another and stood 17 to 19 px apart at 1080p, under half the gap the geometry check gates between names on one row; the check reads one row. Lowering the second baseline to keep 48 px met the seat check's reach: a name more than 90 px from its mark in its column no longer names it, and the staged audit refused three rails so set.
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving it to films; (b) the geometry check measuring the clear gap between consecutive names of one list that alternate between two baselines, across where they stand side by side and down where one reaches under the other.
    - **Chosen because:** (b), with a finding a film may declare by decision 1: the three rails that keep their row are a judgment between two of the engine's own rules, which is what a declaration with a reason is for.
    - **Applies to:** the row gaps of `geometry-check`, for names that share a list (`set`).
16. **A film refused on its keyframe cadence alone is encoded again, and the refused encode is recorded.**
    - **Rests on:** five refusals of `package-film` with "keyframes": three on 13 November 2026 at a 1-minute load of 80 to 120, one on 14 November at 16 to 28 while a CI guest was stopped and started, one on 16 November at a 5-minute load of about 13, with all three CI guests running when read two minutes later. Each time the hardware encoder set keyframes off the declared grid at places that differ from run to run (on 16 November the 2 s grid held to 1,096 s, then came intervals of 1.48, 1.62, 1.87, 1.77 and 0.58 s within 21 s, 752 keyframes for 750); `-g` caps the interval and forbids no keyframe. The 39 packagings before the first refusal held none. Under the load of 13 November the same film was refused three times in a row; on 14 and 16 November the second encode passed. Each refusal ended a film's build chain and needed a hand: the packager keeps a refused step's work and will not overwrite it.
    - **Considered:** (a) packaging only on a quiet host, which the fifth refusal met; (b) forcing the cadence: `-force_key_frames` on the grid adds keyframes and forbids none, and a software encoder for the renditions that declare an interval changes the delivered films' encoder; (c) the packager encoding a film again, at most twice, when the finished film differs from the contract in its keyframes alone, with each refused encode's keyframes kept in the packaging record.
    - **Chosen because:** (c) first: the fault falls at a different place each time, the contract's check already keeps a film with it from delivery, and a second encode costs about seven minutes where a stopped chain cost a build's place in the host queue. A retry removes the hand, not the cause, and 13 November shows it can fail again. The cause is not known (inferred from the 14 November case: a disturbance of the encoder's session), so (b)'s software encoder is measured on the sample film at admission, as the only choice that would remove it, and replaces the retry if it holds the cadence at an acceptable cost.
    - **Applies to:** `filmkit.delivery.packaging`, for renditions that declare `keyframe_seconds`.
17. **A sentence that states a record's figure is derived from the record or checked against it.**
    - **Rests on:** 16 November 2026, before two films' releases: after three more assemblies of one English soundtrack and one of a Chinese soundtrack, the two Changelog sections held three stale figures (a level step across segments, a segment's clarity, a soundtrack's largest step) and a sentence about a move the build had removed; one round's changes note named three of five changed builds, and another said that no caption reads above 25 characters per second while declaring two cues above it. All were found by reading, none by a check. The kit now reads each figure a section states again from the scripts, the sound gate's record, the assembly record and the level measure, and its release step refuses a section whose sentence does not stand (`section-figures.py`).
    - **Considered:** (a) reading by hand before each release; (b) a light check that the figures a release document states stand in the current records, from sentence templates the film lists; (c) the engine writing the measured lines of a round's changes note itself.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) for the Changelog and (c) for the note: `review.round prepare` holds the records the note's measured lines come from, and a figure kept by hand is right for one assembly.
    - **Applies to:** `filmkit.review.round prepare` and a check on a film's release documents before the release content is written.
18. **A word that starts in the quiet before an inserted pause moves with the voice, and sync is judged against what is heard.**
    - **Rests on:** the ETHOS film's review round 21 (note zh-R21-02). After the film raised the floor of a sentence end inside a thought from 0.28 s to 0.45 s, four sentences start 0.40 to 0.45 s before their voice: at those joins 0.33 to 0.36 s of quiet was inserted into the middle of a native quiet of 0.09 to 0.12 s, and the aligner had put the next word's start in that quiet before its middle. `shifted_items` moves a time only by the shifts at or before it, so the start stayed before the inserted quiet. The four captions appear 0.43 to 0.45 s before they are spoken and one label 0.42 s before its word, beyond the film's sync tolerance of 0.3 s. The sync audit read 0.01 s and 0.19 s for the two labels, because it judges against the aligned word; every other sentence's voice begins within 0.14 s of its aligned start (median 0.04 s). No gate saw it; a reviewer measured it on the delivered soundtrack.
    - **Considered:** (a) aligning the assembled soundtrack again, a second recognition pass for every assembly; (b) moving the start of the item on the right of a join by the inserted amount when it lies in that join's quiet before the insertion; (c) a check alone: the first word of each sentence against the first heard frame after it.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) with (c): the assembly knows the join, its quiet run and the amount, so the repair is exact and costs nothing, and the check catches the same fault from any other cause (an aligner that places a start early). The sync audit then judges a label against the corrected start.
    - **Applies to:** `soundtrack/quiet.py` (`lengthen_inner_quiet`, `shifted_items`), the sound gate (voice onset less the aligned start of a sentence's first word, at most 0.15 s) and the picture's sync audit.
19. **Evidence that a round's changes note cites is bound to the round's composition and timelines.**
    - **Rests on:** the same round: the note cited two scans as checks of the reviewed picture while their files named the previous build's composition, and the English reviewer found it; the scans had not been run again after a later picture change. The sister film's note held the same fault before its review. Run again, each scan read as before, so no film was affected, but the record said what it did not show. The kit now refuses a note whose cited evidence names another composition or timeline in its subject line (`cited-evidence.py`).
    - **Considered:** (a) the author's care; (b) `review.round prompts` reading each evidence file the note names and refusing one whose declared subject is not the round's composition and timelines; (c) the engine running such scans itself, which decisions 9, 11 and 13 already bring inside the gates.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) until (c) is whole: a scan the engine does not own will still be cited, and its subject line is cheap to require and to check.
    - **Applies to:** `filmkit.review.round prompts`; the convention that an evidence file's first line names its subject by SHA-256.
20. **The record derives a review's overall from its scores.**
    - **Rests on:** the DDWG film's review round 42: twelve scores with the mean 99.25, the reviewer wrote 99.3, and `review.round record` refused it: the record compares the stated overall, within 0.05, with the mean already rounded by Python's `round`, which gives 99.2 for this exact tie, so 99.3 stood 0.1 away. The prompt says only "the mean of the dimensions, rounded to one decimal". The reviewer corrected its own record; a delivery waited on a rounding rule nobody had stated.
    - **Considered:** (a) stating the rounding in the prompt; (b) the record computing the overall and a reviewer's stated figure being informative only; (c) accepting either rounding.
    - **Chosen because:** (b): the overall is arithmetic on the scores, and a derived figure cannot disagree with them.
    - **Applies to:** `filmkit.review.round record` and the prompt's sentence on the overall.
21. **A release page carries the release's record; a film is published only where someone fetches it, one film to watch per edition on the current edition's page; what keeps a release's films is its rebuild proof.**
    - **Rests on:** the films' owner, 16 November 2026: 「为啥成片要上传 Github?」, 「如果重建很快，为啥还需要上传？」, 「gitlab 也不用传全部吧？想好了再操作！」, after the direction of 14 November (「按对象的本质定归宿」; for a large rebuildable object, 「有很强的…LFS 的需求么？」; use the forges 「用好、用满，但又不乱用、不滥用」). Until that day every release sent all eight films of its two editions to both forges. Measured that evening: nine published releases of the three films held about 25 GB on GitHub and five of them 15.7 GB in GitLab's package registry; each film on GitHub had been fetched 1 to 3 times, which the producer's own comparisons and read-backs account for; the GitLab projects are private with 5 to 12 members and record no fetch of a film; one release's 3.2 GB took over 100 minutes to send at about 1 MB/s, with one stall. A rebuild takes an Apple-silicon Mac, one networked supply step and about two hours of machine time for the 25-minute film (four pictures of 14 to 16 minutes, eight films encoded in about 22 minutes, narration about 40), less for the shorter ones. A rebuild from an anonymous clone was proved for one release of one film (every film frame-identical). The two review records of a release are the only assets a rebuild cannot make again.
    - **Considered:** two reasons to put an object on a forge, kept apart. Keeping: the object must outlive this disk; a derived object needs none once its rebuild is proved, so the proof is what is owed. Reaching: someone who will not build must fetch it there; rebuild time does not answer that, an audience at that forge does. ## Risks / Trade-offs

- A film can declare its way past every finding. → Each declaration carries a reason, is listed in the gate record, and is read by the reviewers with the round's changes note; a stale one is refused.
- `preflight` becomes slower as it reads more records. → It reads records, not media; the audits stay in `check-picture` and `check-sound`.
- The breaking parts reach three films at once. → Each already declares these findings in its design; the adoption is moving them into `content/film.toml`, with the lists the films' own records hold.
