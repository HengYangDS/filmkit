## Context

A film is built today by its own task list: 39 lines in each film's `.config/mise/config.toml` call the engine with `python -m` (28 of `filmkit.produce`, 4 of `filmkit.tools`; read 24 November; the derivation counted 34 task lines [S1, §2.2]), and the engine's own configuration builds the sample the same way. The engine knows every dependency and states it once since R2a, in the node table `filmkit.graph.chain`, which `filmkit.graph.nodes` loads and orders with `graphlib.TopologicalSorter`; every command of the chain writes one run record through the seam `durations.run`, and `filmkit plan --json` and `filmkit verify` read those records [S4]. What R2a left standing is the surface: the films still run the commands one by one, `preflight` and `admitted` still judge readiness from a receipt of their own, `filmkit.durations` still writes a second record of every run and sums them, and `filmkit.adoption` still reads films' task lines to police their order [S4, design decision 1 and Non-Goals].

This Change is the first half of R2b. It gives the film the verbs of the derivation's §5.3 and retires the commands they replace. The owner adopted the derivation with its recommendations at 21:56 on 23 November 2026: 「都按照你的建议。还有什么不确定的？」 [S3, D-37]; its decisions (§6) are taken and not reopened here.

Line references are to the R2a tree at `42be201` unless a path says otherwise. The hearing Change (`hear-a-take-as-a-listener-does`, branch `hear` at `346532a`) is released between R2a and this Change. R2a and the hearing Change stand on separate branches today and both change `preflight.py`, `film.py` and `narration/fitting.py`; this Change is built on the engine's release that holds both, and task 1.1 reads every site again there. The section "Files this Change and the hearing Change both touch" lists the overlap.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- One console command, `filmkit`, with the ten verbs of §5.3; every other command a film runs today retires or becomes an internal process of a verb.
- `filmkit build` makes targets fresh from R2a's records, in the derived order, under host rules 2 and 7, so that no film states a build order.
- A heavy node waits for the gates that `preflight` held it behind, stated once in the table.
- `filmkit check`, the film's light gates on its sources, run before any heavy or GPU work and, read-only, by a film gate `film-sources` and the engine's own hosted proof.
- Every question `preflight`, `admitted`, `durations` and `adoption` answered is answered by its owning node, the plan or `check`; nothing a film relies on is lost.
- A film's tests reach the engine through `filmkit.api` and one Node test entry only.
- `release.json` at the film's root; the README one page.

**Non-Goals:**

- **Outputs keyed by their inputs, the set-aside's retirement, the owners' binding keys, their dozen staleness comparisons and the verifier's re-asks**: the Change `key-outputs-by-their-inputs` (decision 1). Here outputs stay at today's places, overwritten as today, R2a's declared writers keep binding the paths several nodes write, and the set-aside keeps guarding a reviewed build (decision 8).
- **Findings and exceptions in the run record, one exception model** (R3) [S1, §5.7 row R3].
- **One picture runtime, fonts once, the font checks in `filmkit check`, `filmkit supply --runtime`** (R3) [S1, §5.7 row R3, decision 6].
- **The narration lock, `edit` carrying readings and the lock, the merge of `judge`, `audition` and `narrate`, the request without the segment's fingerprint** (R4).
- **The entry page rendered from the declaration, the static player check, the review mechanics behind one module** (R6).
- **Parallel runs of nodes.** `build` runs one node at a time, as the films' task lists do; a parallel run changes what host rule 3 measures and waits for a measurement on whole films [S5, rule 3; S1, §5.6].
- **Changing a film's or the sample's delivered bytes.** Every delivered byte stays as the base's build makes it, under the same FFmpeg.

## Decisions

### 1. R2b is two Changes, in the derivation's order, and the later releases depend on both

- **Rests on:** the R2b row holds, besides the verbs, "outputs keyed by their inputs" and the retirement of the set-aside and the verifier's re-asks [S1, §5.7]. Keying outputs moves every output path: 243 lines in 28 engine modules and 545 lines in 62 test files read an edition's output place, and 69 lines of 33 source files name `build/` literally (section "Counts and their patterns"). The verbs change the surface and a small set of modules (decision 20). The two share no mechanism: `build` reads R2a's records whatever the paths.
- **Options:** (a) one Change; (b) the verbs with the retirements they replace first, then keyed outputs with the set-aside, the owners' bindings and the re-asks; (c) the verbs and keyed outputs first, the retirements second.
- **Chosen because:** (b). A verb and the command it replaces should not stand side by side for a release (one way to run a stage), which rules out (c); and under (a) one review must hold a surface change and a body change of about a hundred files. Under (b) the first Change is what a film adopts to delete its build chain and what the kit's deletion waits for [S1, §5.7: "the condition for deleting the kit"]; the second removes the hand-bound records that §3.1 names "the core contradiction of the engine's body".
- **What each leaves working:** after this Change, films build by verbs; outputs stand where they stand today and are overwritten as today; the set-aside still guards a reviewed build (decision 8); owners still bind their inputs in their own records beside the run records. After `key-outputs-by-their-inputs`, no output is overwritten, the set-aside and the owners' bindings are gone, and the verifier asks only what the records do not state.
- **Order:** this Change, then `key-outputs-by-their-inputs`, then R3, as §5.7 orders R2b before R3.
- **What the later releases depend on** (§5.7 gives each "Depends on R2b"; the split assigns it):
  - **R3** (one picture runtime) depends on both: it writes its audit nodes and its one runtime into the table this Change makes buildable, and puts findings and exceptions into the run record of schema 2, which `key-outputs-by-their-inputs` introduces, with outputs that are never overwritten.
  - **R4** (the narration lock) depends on both: `filmkit narrate` and `filmkit edit` are this Change's verbs, and the lock's takes and the selection's verdict replace bindings that `key-outputs-by-their-inputs` removes from the selection and the assembly.
  - **R6** (thin release, delivery and review seams) depends on both: `filmkit deliver`, `release` and `review` are this Change's verbs, and R6's review mechanics presume the set-aside gone and the film-wide node `entry`, both from `key-outputs-by-their-inputs`.

### 2. The table's command nodes stay the unit of record; the derivation's nodes are groups

- **Rests on:** R2a decision 1 chose today's commands as nodes, "the table a bridge that R2b replaces with the target's nodes", because "a record is written by one run" [S4]. The derivation's nodes (`check`, `takes`, `sound`, `audit`, `picture`, `package`, `entry`, `verify`) each span several of those commands [S1, §5.1].
- **Options:** (a) merge each group of commands into one node with one record; (b) keep R2a's command nodes as the units of record and freshness, and name the derivation's nodes as groups that targets may name.
- **Chosen because:** (b). Merging changes what a record states and when a node reruns (a caption-only change would rerun the assembly), re-opens R2a's accepted rules for the paths several nodes write, and serves no goal of R2b; the groups give the surface the derivation names. R2a's records, plan and rules stand unchanged.
- **The groups:** `sound` = `select`, `assemble`, `refresh-captions`, `check-sound`; `audit` = `stage-picture`, `stage-picture-uncaptioned`, `geometry-check`, `player-check-cover`, `check-picture`, `render-draft`; `picture` = `render-picture`, `render-picture-uncaptioned`, `validate-picture`, `validate-picture-uncaptioned`, `qualify-renderer`, `compare-pictures`; `package` = `package-metadata`, `package-film`, `scan-film`. `narrate` is a node and its own group. `check` is a film-wide node (decision 6). `all` is every node but `narrate`. `entry` is no node in this Change: the entry page stays an output of `package-metadata` until `key-outputs-by-their-inputs` makes it the film-wide node `entry`; R6 then renders it from the declaration. `verify` is no node: it judges the whole build (R2a decision 1).
- **Targets:** `<node or group>:<code>`, `<node or group>:all`, `check`, `all`. `filmkit plan` takes the same targets.
- **Reopened when:** a measured whole-film build shows a group's separate records cost more than they save; or R4 replaces `narrate` with the lock's `takes`.

### 3. `filmkit build` runs each stale node's owner in one process, in the derived order

- **Rests on:** each node's owner call is already one line of an adapter: `produce.EDITION_ACTIONS` (`produce.py:107-146`) and the two runs of `tools.BUILD_RUNS` (`tools.py:73-76`), each inside the seam `durations.run` (`produce.py:214-227`, `tools.py:208-215`). The seam measures a run by `getrusage` deltas and a sampler of the process tree (R2a decision 7), which hold for runs one after another in one process. A redo already runs nested in one process (`durations.node`, R2a decision 5).
- **Options:** (a) each node in a subprocess of an internal entry; (b) each node's owner called in the build's own process, inside the seam; (c) doit, the named alternative of decision 1 of §6 [S1, §4.1].
- **Chosen because:** (b). (a) adds an entry point the derivation counts and a Python start for each of about 40 nodes; (c) is the alternative not taken. Each node of the table gains `run`, its owner call, moved from `produce.EDITION_ACTIONS` (with `after_set_aside` where the adapter held it, decision 8) and `tools.BUILD_RUNS`; the geometry check and the cover check call `tools.run` with the arguments the films' task lines passed, derived from the edition and the declaration (decision 14).
- **The run:** `build` loads the table and takes the closure of the targets: each target's nodes and every node upstream of them, by a path or by a wait (decision 4). It visits the closure's nodes in the derived order and, at each node's turn, judges it again against the files as they stand (R2a's `records.judge`); it runs the node, inside the seam, only where the node is stale. A node that was behind only because a node upstream was stale is fresh again once that node has run without changing its outputs, and is not run; a node behind a gate that ran again and passed is fresh by its own inputs, and is not run. A node whose owner refuses ends its run as `refuse` with its one line (R2a decision 2); no node downstream of it, by a path or by a wait, is run; nodes not downstream of it go on. `build` ends with one line per refusal and failure and exits 2 where a node failed, 1 where one refused, 0 where every target is fresh and passed (decision 19).
- **A refusal stands until what its node reads changes:** a node stale only because its last run refused, its record's own reason and no other, would refuse the same if run again, since nothing it reads has changed; `build` does not run it, gives that run's line as the node's refusal, and holds what follows it. The first form ran it again, so a refused `check-sound` was asked again by every build that reached it, and the scenario "A refused sound gate holds the render" did not hold (the independent review of `b0a7bde..653a9f8`, finding 7). The narration's refusal is excepted: no build runs it, and `filmkit narrate` answers it. A run that failed (`error`) is run again, since a failure to finish says nothing of its inputs. An interrupt ends the running node as `child_processes.ending` ends a command today and runs no further node.
- **What `build` never does:** run `narrate` (decisions 5 and 9), write a tracked file, or take a host ticket (the engine takes none; F5 [S1, §1]).
- **Uses of R2a:** `nodes.load` and the order; `plan.plan` for the closure's states; `records.judge` at each turn; `runs.open_node` and the seam; `host` for the class; `records.write`.

### 4. A heavy node waits for the gates on its inputs

- **Rests on:** R2a's table holds no render behind the sound gate, the geometry check, the cover check or the draft. Edges are derived from paths alone (`graph/nodes.py:187-215`, `edges`): a node comes after the writers of what it reads. The only reader of `sound_gates`, `picture_geometry`, the painted cover and `draft_stills` is the node `preflight` (`graph/chain.py:227-239`, `readiness`); `render-picture` reads its stage and `picture_gates` alone (`:376-382`); packaging reads none of them (`delivery/packaging.py:276-288`, `film_inputs`). The plan calls a node ready when every node upstream is fresh (`graph/plan.py:388-394`: `ready = all(states[up] == FRESH for up in upstream) and …`), and a record is fresh only with outcome `pass` (R2a decision 6). So in R2a the hold was the films' task order and `admitted`, not the plan; with `preflight` out of the table, `filmkit plan render-picture:zh` would call a render ready while `check-sound` refused (the independent review of these drafts, finding 1 [S7]). `render-preflight`'s purpose was to stop exactly that: "Report, before an expensive render, every input of the render and of the packaging after it that is stale or missing".
- **Options:** (a) make the gates' records inputs of the heavy nodes; (b) an ordering edge, a wait, that requires the gate's run fresh and passed and binds none of its bytes; (c) a readiness rule in the plan beside the edges.
- **Chosen because:** (b). Under (a) a gate run again with a changed record (a hearing recorded, a cue's rate re-measured) makes every render and package stale although no input of theirs changed, which decision 12 exists to prevent. (c) states the dependency outside the table, a second statement of order. Under (b) `plan.judged` compares the node's own inputs only, as today.
- **The waits, stated once in `chain.py`:**
  - `render-picture`, `render-picture-uncaptioned` and `package-film` of an edition wait for that edition's `check-sound` (the soundtrack's floor, the thought line with the owner's hearing, the reading rate) and `player-check-cover`;
  - `render-picture` and `render-picture-uncaptioned` of every edition wait for every edition's `check-picture` and `geometry-check`, and, where the film declares drafts, every edition's `render-draft`;
  - every heavy or GPU node waits for the film-wide `check`.
- **Waits are their own edges, and a node that waits is `waiting`, not `behind`.** The first revision merged the waits into R2a's path edges, so that "behind", which R2a spreads to every node downstream of one that is not fresh (`graph/plan.py:391`), spread down every path from a wait as well: since `narrate` waits for `check` and every node is downstream of `narrate`, any edit of a source or a table, a recorded hearing included, made every node of both editions behind, and `build` counted heavy nodes that would not change as work that will run (the second reading, S1 [S7]). The table therefore keeps each node's waits beside its path edges, and the plan states four states:
  - `stale`: the node's own record judges stale (R2a decision 6);
  - `behind`: fresh by its own record, and a node upstream of it by a path is stale or behind, so it will likely run when that node has run;
  - `waiting`: fresh by its own record, nothing upstream of it by a path stale or behind, and a gate it waits for, directly or through a node upstream of it by a path, not fresh and passed;
  - `fresh` otherwise.
    `behind` spreads only from stale nodes through paths; `waiting` spreads down paths too, but a waiting node will run only if its own inputs change. `ready` needs every node upstream by a path fresh, not waiting, and every gate the node waits for, directly or through those nodes, fresh and passed; `filmkit verify` refuses a waiting node as it refuses a behind one. The four states are computed in one pass over the derived order taken over paths and waits together, so every gate is judged before every node that waits for it, as R2a's plan judges in one pass (`graph/plan.py:387-406`). The plan's JSON names, for a waiting node, each gate it waits for with that gate's state, so that ETHOS reads why it is held and asks no ticket for it.
- **A wait on a node that does not exist** (a film without drafts has no `render-draft`, `chain.py:364`; `qualify-renderer` exists for one edition, though no node waits for it) is dropped when the table loads, as R2a drops a rewriter's `after` that names no key (`nodes.py:213`), so `Table.upstream` never meets a key the table lacks.
- **The cross-edition waits** carry `admitted`'s rule that "every other edition's receipt passed on the composition, stylesheet, fonts, declaration and entry page as they stand, since every edition renders one composition and a finding in any changes it for all" (`preflight.py:40-43`), narrowed to the gates that judge the shared composition: another edition's sound finding changes no input of this edition's render, so it holds no render here, where `admitted` held it.
- **What it changes for the hearing:** a hearing recorded in `[[narration.heard]]` makes `check` stale (it reads the whole declaration through the loader) and `check-sound` stale (it reads the `narration` table, decision 12), and every render, package and narration waiting; `build all` then runs `check`, `check-sound` and any other node that reads the `narration` table, all light, in one light invocation, and the renders and packages are fresh again once the gates pass, since none of their inputs changed. One exception stands until `key-outputs-by-their-inputs`: the picture gate's own record binds the declaration whole (`picture/gates.py:403-412`), and a render is refused while that record binds another declaration (`rendering.require_passing_gates`), so the node `check-picture` binds the whole declaration through its owner's list and is made stale by any edit of it, a hearing included. Each edition's renders, after its picture gate by a path, are then behind it, not waiting; once the gate has run again its rewritten record makes them stale, and both pictures of each edition render again (heavy, 190 to 220 s each by the derivation's figure [S1, §5.6]), with every node after them whose inputs then change. `key-outputs-by-their-inputs` retires the picture gate's own binding and takes it out of the render's inputs (its decision 4), after which a hearing renders nothing. Stated, not narrowed here, by the design's owner, the films session, 25 November 2026, about 02:31: narrowing that binding in this Change would change the code that Change rewrites. The owner's choice of 13:03 on 24 November, 「1 交给你的耳朵」 (D-46 [S3]), and the soundtrack's and segments' 18 dB floor are held where they were: a render waits until `check-sound` passes.

### 5. Light work that can run now runs first, outside any heavy invocation

- **Rests on:** host rule 2: a ticket is queued only when "the repository's checks that take seconds … have passed" and "the files that its command reads exist"; rule 7: consecutive heavy commands of one build may share a ticket when every command is heavy or "a step of seconds that the next of them needs" and "the sum of their last run times … is at most 30 minutes; a command that has not been measured so enters alone"; rule 3: a first run treated as light is measured, and one that measures over two cores is heavy from then on [S5]. The derivation states two sentences: `build` "refuses a heavy or GPU node while a light node upstream of it is stale or failing, naming the light targets to run first, outside any ticket", and "One invocation runs the nodes of one heavy kind …, plus light steps of seconds that they need" [S1, §5.1].
- **How the two sentences are read together:** the first speaks of the light nodes upstream that can run now: those with no stale heavy or GPU node upstream of them. The second speaks of the light nodes that come after a heavy node of the same invocation (`validate-picture` between `render-picture` and `compare-pictures`), which cannot run before it and run inside the invocation as rule 7's steps of seconds. The first draft read the first sentence as the source check alone, on the ground that `build all` from an empty build would otherwise refuse at once; that holds only of one invocation from nothing, since after `filmkit narrate` the sound chain, the stages, the cover check and the light gates can all run before any heavy node. Under the first draft's reading a `check-sound` refusal would first be met inside a ticket that rule 2 should not have admitted (review finding 5 [S7]). This reading keeps both sentences.
- **The rule:** a node's class is its measured class, or its expected class where it has none. Before running anything, `build` refuses, in one line each and naming what to run:
  - a closure that holds a stale `narrate`, naming `filmkit narrate --edition <code>` (GPU work is the narration verb's, decision 9);
  - a closure that holds a heavy node that will run while a light node of the closure with no stale heavy or GPU node upstream of it is stale or did not pass, naming `filmkit build` with those light nodes as targets; an invocation whose closure holds no heavy node that will run is such a light invocation and runs outside any ticket;
  - a closure whose heavy nodes that will run have more than one name, unless every one of them has a measured class and their last seconds sum to at most 1,800 s; it then prints the invocations to run in order, one heavy name each, each with its class and last seconds, so that each is one ticket.
- **"Will run":** a heavy node that is stale or behind; a waiting node is never counted, since a gate that passes leaves it fresh (decision 4), nor a node whose refusal stands (decision 3) or one downstream of it. Heavy nodes of several names share an invocation only once each is measured: `picture:zh` after a picture module changes holds `render-picture`, `compare-pictures` and, where the film qualifies Chinese, `qualify-renderer`, the last two behind the render and heavy by their expected class, so it runs as one invocation only once all three are measured within 1,800 s, as rule 7 asks of a command never measured; the scenario "Light steps after a heavy node" states that condition (finding 7 of the same review). Measuring the waits' classes is not needed for it: the wait on `check` is an edge of the table, given to each node by its expected class where the table is stated (`chain.CHECKED`), and does not move with the records, as the order derived over it must not. An English retake that makes `check-picture:en` stale leaves `render-picture:zh`, its validation, the qualification and the comparison fresh by their own records and waiting, not behind, and asks no Chinese render. The Chinese packaging is behind it by a path and will run: the viewing package and the entry page list both editions and read the English timeline (`chain.metadata_inputs`), so `package-metadata:zh` (expected light), `package-film:zh` and `scan-film:zh` (expected heavy) run again, and the retake asks a ticket for the Chinese package. Hiding that read from their inputs would make a false graph; the dependency is accepted by the design's owner, the films session, 25 November 2026, about 02:31.
- **The sample's build** is then `filmkit check`, `filmkit narrate --edition all`, one light `filmkit build` outside any ticket, and `filmkit build all` in one ticket once its heavy nodes are measured within 30 minutes, which meets §5.7's "admitted as one ticket"; from nothing, one ticket for each heavy name until each is measured.
- **The 30 minutes and the classes** are read from R2a's records (`plan.Entry.host_class`, `seconds`), the classes as host rule 3 measured them; the limit is a named constant beside R2a's (`graph.host`), citing rule 7. The engine does not read ETHOS's declaration (`host.toml`); it states, and ETHOS admits.
- **Reopened when:** ETHOS declares the 30 minutes or a heavy kind in its own declaration; or the host rules are reviewed (next review 2026-12-05 [S5]); or packaging the other edition after a retake is measured as a heavy ticket; then the bilingual listing becomes a light node of its own.

### 6. `filmkit check` is a film-wide node of the table, with a read-only mode for gates

- **Rests on:** narration's gate already runs the concept contract, the register, the edition's readings and what each picture reads over its derived timeline before any model loads (`narration/narrate.py:126-150`); `preflight` runs the concept and register checks again (`preflight.py:358-359`); `filmkit.adoption` asks eight questions of a film's files, four of which hold after this Change (`adoption.py:20-55`). The derivation's verb: "the declaration, then every light gate on sources: concepts, register, readings, anchors and the picture's reads over a derived timeline" [S1, §5.3]. R2a records no film-wide node; its decision 9 names their place, `build/evidence/records/film/<node>.json` [S4].
- **Options:** (a) a verb outside the table; (b) a film-wide node whose record the plan and `build` read.
- **Chosen because:** (b): host rule 2's "checks that take seconds have passed" is then a fact the plan can state, and the run record binds what the verdict was made from. A node gains `per` (`edition` or `film`), as the derivation's interface names it (`Node(name, per, inputs, run, host_class)`) [S1, §4.2].
- **What it asks, at once, every refusal of a load together:** the declaration as the loader reads it (`film.problems`, the loader's refusals, `declared_sources`); for every edition, the concept contract, the register (Vale, the engine's pinned version, naming `mise use github:vale-cli/vale@<version>` for another), the readings, the anchors and what the picture reads over the derived timeline, which reports an unloadable picture as "not judged" (D-37's reading, item 7 [S3]); the film's npm lock and Python lock against the engine's record (`adoption`'s `npm_packages` and `python_lock`); the picture template (`adoption`'s `template`, until R3 retires the template); and every review prompt line that cites the runtime without "This edition" (`adoption`'s `runtime`). `adoption`'s `tasks` and `editions` questions retire with the build chains and R1's bridge [S1, §5.5].
- **No `--edition`:** §5.3 gives `filmkit check [--edition X]`. The node is film-wide: the register and the concepts read every edition's words, and a check of one edition would be a partial run whose record could not make the node fresh (R2a decision 8). The verb takes no edition; it reports each finding with its edition.
- **Its outputs:** `build/evidence/check/verdict.json` (the outcome and each finding, no digest and no time, so a passing run after an edit elsewhere writes the same bytes) and each edition's occurrence plan (`build/evidence/readings/<code>-plan.json`), which R2a left to no node (R2a decision 8, reopened here).
- **`--read-only`:** the same questions, findings to standard output, exit 1 on a finding, and no file written: no record, no verdict, no occurrence plan. Each film's gate `film-sources` and the engine's own gate run it so, since every gate of a proof set declares `writes_files = false` (`ethos-film/system/gates.toml`) and `film-checks` runs its suite with `-B` and `-p no:cacheprovider` to keep the tree unchanged; decision 11 chose a read-only `verify` for the same reason.
- **Where it runs:** on the host as a node, and in hosted CI as a gate. It needs the film's Mise tools (Node, Vale at the engine's version) and the film's npm packages, which `mise run bootstrap` installs on a runner, and no model, no browser, no FFmpeg and no dictionary: the pinned CMUdict is loaded only by `narration/judgment.py:198-218` (`pronunciations`), called from the judgment, the convergence and the audition (read in the hearing tree). The engine declares a gate `sample-sources` in its own `system/gates.toml`, which runs `filmkit check --read-only` on the sample, as §5.4 asks ("plus `filmkit check` on the sample film"). The same rule holds for the engine as for a film: a gate joins `default` and `full` only after its first hosted run passes, the engine's in this Change's lane before its tag (task 10.6), a film's in its own lane; the release note says so.
- **Where it binds the build:** every heavy or GPU node waits for `check` (decision 4); `filmkit narrate` refuses while the plan calls `narrate:<code>` not ready. Narration's own gate is removed from `narrate.py`.
- **Its key:** a film-wide node is keyed by its name alone, `check`, in the plan, its JSON and the targets, where an edition's node is `<node>:<code>`; `Table.node` gives it no edition, since `Edition` refuses a code the film does not declare (`production.py:189-190`).
- **Its class:** expected `light`; the acceptance measures it on the sample and on a throwaway clone of each film (tasks 10.2, 10.5).
- **Readers of the occurrence plan:** the review prompts cite it by digest (`review/prompts.py:319`); unchanged. Its one other writer, `python -m filmkit.narration.readings` without `--propose` (`narration/readings.py:466,481-484`), would change `check`'s output outside any run, which `filmkit verify` refuses; that checking mode retires with the other `main`s, and `--propose` stays (decision 17).

### 13. Encoders' versions: bound by the nodes that encode, and reproduction across versions compares before encoding

- **Chosen, following the finding:**
  - A node that runs FFmpeg, ffprobe or ExifTool (R2a's `tools` field of each node) states each tool's version among its pins (the first line of `ffmpeg -version`; `exiftool -ver`), so an upgrade makes it stale and the plan names both versions.   - `release.json` records, beside each film, the FFmpeg version that encoded it, and, beside each edition, the drawn-frame digest of each of its UHD pictures that the renderer drew.
  - `filmkit verify --release`, where the running FFmpeg is the recorded one, compares as today.
  - Where it differs, it compares what FFmpeg does not encode as today (each soundtrack by its bytes, frame counts, streams, chapters and tags), and compares the layers FFmpeg encodes before encoding: each film's encoded audio through its edition's soundtrack, already held to its recorded bytes, and each film's picture, UHD and 1080p, through the drawn-frame digest of the UHD picture it carries or is encoded from, against the digest the record states. A release recorded before this one states no drawn-frame digest either; its pictures are compared as today, by decoded frames against each film's floor, and its audio through the soundtrack's bytes, which every release record states (`soundtrack_sha256`), the report saying that the recorded encoder is unknown.
  - This comparison by the floor is what today's standard does in every case; it holds across encoders where the floor is set, and a film without a floor does not pass today either.
- **Rejected:** a pinned FFmpeg supplied by the engine, a native tool beside Homebrew's that the shared "Brew-first" rule asks to justify by a verified gap; and the first draft's failure of every film across versions, which would leave a film's reproduction failing for a cause that is neither the engine's nor the film's, and which Homebrew offers no older FFmpeg to undo.

### 19. A command's exit status tells a refusal from a failure to finish

- **Rests on:** the derivation's finding 3 [S1, §8]: every verb exited 1 for a judged refusal, for the build's scheduling refusal and for a traceback alike, so a caller could not tell the film saying no from a command that broke; `build.run_node` folded any exception of an owner into a refusal line, although the node's record stated `error` (the independent review of `b0a7bde..653a9f8`, finding 3).
- **Chosen:** three statuses, named once beside the exit of a command that a request ends (`child_processes.FINISHED`, `REFUSED`, `UNFINISHED`) and applied once in `cli.main`: 0 where the command finished; 1 where the film or a gate said no, in one line; 2 where it could not finish, an owner's exception printed with its traceback, or a tool the host lacks (`child_processes.MissingToolError`: no `node`, FFmpeg or Vale on the path, no rsync 3) in one line; a program the engine starts by name that is not on the path (`ffmpeg`, `ffprobe`, `exiftool`, `rsync`) is named the same way, from the error that starting it raises (`child_processes.missing`), where the first form showed its traceback (the independent review of `653a9f8..68e0cc6`, finding 6), and the renderer, which starts FFmpeg from Node, asks for it before it starts (`child_processes.required`), where its missing FFmpeg was read as a render that did not complete (the review of `68e0cc6..b1467a2`, finding 3). The host's programs are stated once, with how Homebrew installs each and, for an encoder, the name a record pins it under (`child_processes.HOST_TOOLS`), from which the seam's encoders derive (`runs.ENCODERS`). A command that a request ended keeps the status that names it (130, 143 or 129), as the `build-time` specification states; a command line the parser cannot read exits 2, as `argparse` does. `filmkit build` exits 2 where a node failed, else 1 where one refused or was held.
- **Moved with it:** `tools.ToolError` is now only a tool's refusal, which the chain's tool nodes raise (`chain.ran`, `geometry_check`, `cover_check`); the catches that held a missing Node as a refusal (the delivery's player check, the anchor reads, the review round, the repair) catch it no more.
- **Not chosen:** a status for each kind of refusal: a caller acts on three outcomes, and the line names the rest.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [In one process, a node's module state leaks into the next] → the owners read their inputs from files at each call; the build's acceptance (task 10.2) compares every delivered file with a baseline built on the base by the old tasks, under the same FFmpeg, and the recorder (R2a decision 4) holds every node to its declaration.
- [A waiting node is taken for work that will run] → `waiting` is its own state, named with its gates in the plan's JSON, and never counted by `build` (decision 4); task 4.3 holds an English retake asking no Chinese ticket.
- [A wait missing from the table lets a heavy node run past a refused gate] → the waits are stated once (decision 4), and a test holds that every node `preflight` read is waited for by every render and package it held (task 4.3).
- [Light build steps between heavy nodes run inside a ticket] → they are rule 7's "steps of seconds"; each node's class is measured, and one that measures heavy is heavy from then on.
- [A table the recorder does not see is read by a node] → the per-table recorder logs each top-level attribute a run reads of the loaded declaration, and names a read of the declaration outside a top-level attribute (such as a whole `model_dump`) as a read of every table, which binds more, not less; the acceptance runs every node with the recorder set (task 10.2).
- [A film adopting meets 0.23 to 0.34 at once] → stated in the release note and in each film's adoption; the fused DDWG film, written to R1's layout, adopts first (decision 7 of §6 [S1]).
- [`filmkit check` is slower than "seconds" on a whole film] → measured in tasks 10.2 and 10.5; if it measures heavy, it is heavy, and the release note says so.
