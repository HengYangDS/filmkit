## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- `check-picture` measures the centre of the lit picture's mass beside its box, as the fused film's rule 3 and its prototype read lit pixels, and refuses a stage whose mass leans beyond the rule's limit for 1.5 s or more.
- It refuses a still state, its whole box still, whose box stands more than 50 px above or below y 510 for 2 s or more.
- A composition declares a frame stroke where it draws it; both the box and the mass leave declared frame strokes out, as the recommendation the owner approved says; a film that declares none keeps every lit scene pixel in both.
- No picture, film or delivered byte changes but the sample's; the release says which verdicts change and how a film adopts it.

**Non-Goals:**

- **The fused film's composition rules.** Rule 1 (no camera move) and rule 2 (two rails only for a set of ranks or rules) are the composition's [S1, §14]; the engine gates neither.
- **Recomposing a film but the sample.** The fused film is recomposed by the films session under D-69 and D-70; the three released films are frozen until their December editions [S6].
- **A way for a film to keep a mass or vertical finding.** No `[[picture.detail.exceptions]]` kind is added (decision 9).
- **The other measures of the frame audit.** They keep their measures and their gates (decision 7).
- **The `[960, 500]` centre of `off_balance`.** It differs from the `(960, 510)` of `pictureOffset` and of rule 3; moving it would change `off_balance`'s verdicts by 10 px, which this Change does not set out to do. It is recorded for a later Change.

## Decisions

### 1. The mass is measured before the render, on the uncaptioned stage, by the renderer's canvas

- **Rests on:** rule 3 measures lit pixels, the caption excluded [S1, §14]. The renderer draws a staged picture on a native canvas of `@napi-rs/canvas`, running the composition unchanged in the scope the audits use (`native-canvas.mjs`, header; `compositionScope`, `headless-composition.mjs:345`). A UHD picture takes 190 to 220 s to render on the films' lengths (CHANGELOG, 0.34.0, `Changed`). The frame audit draws on a canvas stand-in that records each paint's geometry and no pixel (`headless-composition.mjs`, header). Every edition is staged without captions as well (`stage-picture-uncaptioned`, light, `graph/chain.py:275`), whatever way its uncaptioned picture is then made.
- **Options:** (a) a new audit, `picture/mass-audit.mjs`, that opens the edition's uncaptioned stage as the renderer opens a stage, draws it every 0.25 s and encodes nothing; (b) an estimate in the frame audit from its paint stream: a stroke's length times its width, a fill's area, a text's box times an ink share, as the prototype's `balance-stages.mjs` does; (c) a measure of the rendered picture after the render, from its decoded frames, as `balance79.py` does.
- **Chosen because:** (a). (b) needs a text's ink, which the stand-in does not have: a glyph's share of its box depends on the script, the face and the size, and every stage carries names, so the estimate's error would sit inside a 90 px limit that rule 3 measures in pixels. (c) measures the right pixels too late: a refusal comes after a render of 190 to 220 s and sends the picture back through it. (a) measures the pixels the renderer draws, with its fonts, text renderer and canvas build, before any render. The uncaptioned stage holds no caption, so the caption is left out by construction.
- **Applies to:**
  - `native-canvas.mjs` exports `openPicture` and gives it one parameter, the width to draw at, the height following by the record's ratio, defaulting to the record's width, so the render is unchanged. Every stage is recorded at the UHD frame (`rendering.PICTURE_SIZE`, `rendering.py:51`), and `mass-audit.mjs` opens it at width 1920: a canvas of 1920 × 1080 and the scope's width 1920, as the headless audits draw, so each 480 × 270 cell is 4 × 4 px and every coordinate is the composition's own 1920 × 1080 frame. It opens the stage with the renderer's text renderer, `renderer.TEXT_RENDERER` (`"system"`, `renderer.py:75`), which `gates.py` passes to it as `renderer.command` passes it to the render; the package's default draws labels lighter (`renderer.py:73-74`) and would light other cells.
  - Through a wrapper of the context's paint methods that only the audit uses, it draws what decision 4's one rule weighs and the ground: `fillRect` always, so each frame starts on the composition's own ground and never on the canvas's black, and `stroke`, `fill` and `fillText` only while `window.__sceneId` names a scene and the role is not `"frame"` (decisions 2 to 4). The headless audits never see a `fillRect` as a mark (Context), so a composition that drew a mark with `fillRect` would be weighed by the mass alone; none of the sample, DDWG or the prototype does.
  - `tools.TOOLS` gains `"mass-audit": Tool("picture/mass-audit.mjs")`, with `measures` and `browses` False: `measures` means a tool that runs the composition on the stand-in, measuring text by the declared faces' table (`tools.py:62-69`), and `tests/test_tools.py` holds the field to whether the module reads `readTextMetrics`, which the mass audit does not; it draws text with the stage's own faces. `check-picture` runs it through `run_audits` beside the frame, motion and detail audits (`picture/gates.py:423`), each in `seam.phase(tool)` (`gates.py:438`), so the run record states each audit's seconds as a phase; `tools.run` refuses another canvas build before it starts the audit, so `check-picture` calls no check of its own.
  - The node `check-picture` gains `stage(e, captions=False)` among its inputs (`graph/chain.py:302`; `graph/bindings.py:196-198`), so the plan orders it after `stage-picture-uncaptioned` and judges it stale when that stage changes; the renders' existing wait on `check-picture` of every edition (`RENDERED`, `graph/chain.py:170`) holds a render while the check is not fresh. The gate record also binds the stage (decision 6).
  - The cost in reruns: through the stage's record (`rendering.stage_places`, `picture/rendering.py:87`) `check-picture`, a heavy node, now runs again when the script, the assembly record, the soundtrack, the source manifest or the template changes, beside the composition, timeline, declaration and faces it read before, and both renders follow it.
- **The budget:** the audits run at once (`run_audits`), so what the mass audit costs the node is the seconds by which it outlasts the others. The budget is read from the run record's own phases of one `check-picture` run on one stage: the `mass-audit` phase is at most the `frame-audit` phase of the same run, the frame audit writing the trace files `check-picture` asks of it. Within it the mass audit ends no later than the frame audit, and `check-picture`'s wall time does not grow; the two still share the host's CPU. It is judged on a film of a real edition's length, not on the sample, whose audits each take under half a second while the mass audit's start-up alone, Node, the canvas library and the faces, takes 0.4 to 0.6 s. The films session took this budget and the step below under delegation on 26 November at about 14:22 [S11], replacing the fifth of [S9], on this measurement: on 26 November between 14:06 and 14:17, each audit alone at nice 15 on the fused film's preview copy of chapters 1 to 5 in Chinese (`preview-034-ch1-5`, `film.js` `5d74589c…`, 713 s), its stage copied outside the copy and its 194 captions removed, the traced frame audit took 20.8 to 29.0 s over four runs for 21,391 frames at 1/30 s (13.2 s without its traces), at 1.08 to 1.10 CPU over wall; the mass audit took 38.3 and 39.1 s at 0.1 s for 7,130 frames, and 14.3 and 15.3 s at 0.25 s for 2,852 frames, at 1.13 to 1.15 CPU over wall. At 0.1 s it is over the budget and at 0.25 s within it. **Not measured:** the audits running together, as `run_audits` runs them, where they compete for the CPU.
- **The step, and what it does to the limits:** the step is 0.25 s. A run of k samples at step s spans k × s seconds and counts when that is at least its limit (`rules/balance.mjs:107`). Rule 3's limits are 1.5 s and 2 s, and at 0.25 s six samples make exactly 1.5 s and eight make 2.0 s: a lean of 1.5 s or more holds at least six samples and always counts, so the rule is never looser than rule 3; a lean of a little over 1.25 s may count by where the samples fall, so it is at most one step stricter. At 0.1 s the same holds with one step of 0.1 s, at two and a half times the frames; at 0.2 s, 1.5 s is no whole number of steps, 7 samples making 1.4 s and 8 making 1.6 s, so a lean of 1.5 to 1.6 s could pass, which loosens rule 3. The cost at 0.25 s stays inside the frame audit's time on a real film (the budget above). **What the step changes at the 1.5 s edge,** on the same preview, 0.25 s against 0.1 s, where the 0.1 s audit found 18 runs in 11 scenes: one run fewer and three more. Scene 11's run at 564.80–566.30 s (−332.5 px) is gone: the 0.25 s samples see the lean from 565.00 to 566.00 s, and both samplings place its length between 1.4 and 1.5 s. Three leans of 1.2 to 1.4 s by the 0.1 s samples now count as six samples: scene 11 at 535.25–536.75 s (−118.0 px), scene 07 at 370.50–372.00 s (−182.4 px) and scene 13 at 669.00–670.50 s (−97.4 px). Every other run stands at both steps, its span read to the nearer step; scene 08's run at 427.40–429.10 s reads its largest offset as −163.9 px, where at 0.1 s one frame read −401.9 px between two 0.25 s samples. If the budget is over at 0.25 s on another film, the audit draws in contiguous spans in parallel processes, as the renderer does (`planSpans`, `native-canvas.mjs:72`), and this decision is reopened with the measure. The step is one constant, `MASS.step`, so the limits' fit is checked where it is set: `massRuns` refuses a step that does not divide 1.5 s and 2 s into whole steps, judged within 1e-9 s of a whole number, since steps such as 0.1 s are not exact in floating point.

### 2. Both the box and the mass leave declared frame strokes out

- **Rests on:** the recommendation the owner approved at 19:41, "lit pixels with declared frame strokes excluded, mass and box" [S4, D-69], and rule 3 as corrected to follow it [S1, §14].
- **Options:**
  - (a) Count every lit pixel in both. Failure modes: a frame across the stage centres the box, so a framed stage's box rules decide nothing; its own pixels pull the mass toward its centre, by 8 to 36 px at the median on the fused previews of chapters 7 to 9 (`balance79.json`, `all` against `no_rails`, six editions [S3]); and it departs from the approved recommendation.
  - (b) Leave declared frame strokes out of the box and count them in the mass. Failure modes: an honestly declared frame still pulls the mass toward its centre by its share, inside a 90 px limit, so it is more lenient than the approved recommendation for every film that declares its frames; and it departs from it.
  - (c) Leave declared frame strokes out of both. Failure modes: both measures trust the declaration. A frame left undeclared counts in both: it centres the box as today, and pulls the mass by its share only, 8 to 36 px on the fused previews, so a lean of more than about 90 px plus that share still shows in the mass. A stroke of content declared a frame leaves both measures, and no gate refuses it; `declared_frames` names each such stroke for the reader. A composition keeps one more declaration, set once in the helper that draws its frames.
  - (d) Leave out long full-height strokes by their geometry, as `balance79.py`'s `no_rails` removes every column lit in 60 % or more of the band's rows. Failure modes: it also removes what such strokes mean, rule 2's two open rails for a set of ranks or rules, a vertical axis or a divider; it misses a frame shorter than the threshold, dashed, or drawn as horizontal rules; removing one such column on one side moves the measure toward the other, which can make a leaning stage pass; and its 60 % is a constant no rule states.
- **Chosen because:** (c). It is the measure the owner approved, in both its parts; a frame is named where it is drawn, by the one who draws it, so no stroke is guessed at from its shape; and the safeguard against a frame left undeclared is the mass, which such a frame moves only by its share.
- **When a film declares none:** every lit scene pixel counts in both measures, every box verdict stays as it is today, and the mass still refuses a large lean.

### 3. How a composition declares a frame stroke

- **Rests on:** rule 3: "a stroke drawn only to frame a stage is declared so where it is drawn" [S1, §14]; the scene id, which film.js publishes around each scene and the audits attach to each paint (Context).
- **Options:** (i) a role in effect while the stroke is painted: film.js sets `window.__paintRole = "frame"` before it strokes a frame and `null` after, and the audits attach it to each paint where they attach the scene; (ii) a role on the stroke's entry in the layout's `draws`; (iii) a list of the frames' boxes published with the layout.
- **Chosen because:** (i). The `draws` list only strokes drawn on by their tip, so a frame that appears whole has no entry; an entry carries no geometry and is read by the detail audit, not the frame audit (`detail-audit/quiets.mjs:8`), so (ii) could tie a role to its painted stroke only by matching shapes or drawing lines afterwards, and (iii) only by matching shapes. (i) is declared at the stroke itself, through the mechanism the scene id already uses, and the fused film's `rails()` helper, or any film's frame helper, sets it once for every frame it draws.
- **Applies to:**
  - The value is `"frame"` or absent (`null`), held as `DECLARED_FRAME_ROLE` in `headless-composition.mjs`, a name apart from seat-marks' `FRAME` container (`frame-audit/composition.mjs:13`).
  - One exported function of `headless-composition.mjs` records a paint's scene and role, and both paint deliveries call it (`headless-composition.mjs:420`, `testing.mjs:62`); it judges nothing and never throws, so the motion and detail audits, which share the delivery, run on as before. The reads of the scene for an anchor (`:408`) and for `scene()` (`:453`) stay as they are.
  - The stand-in's `fillRect` still hands over no paint, so no audit's `onPaint` sees a new kind. The composition's handle (`openComposition`, `headless-composition.mjs:433-480`) instead keeps a list, `roleErrors()`, of the role errors of the frame last rendered: each `fillRect` call made while `window.__paintRole` is set, and each paint whose recorded role is neither `"frame"` nor `null`, or is `"frame"` on a fill or a text; the handle finds them among the roles the record function wrote, and the record function itself judges none. The frame audit reads that list after each frame it renders, as it reads `declaredCuts()`; no other audit reads it.
  - The frame audit alone judges the role, from that list, and reports as one of its errors, which `check-picture` gates with its errors: a role of another value; a role `"frame"` on a fill, a text or a `fillRect` call. Only a stroke can be a declared frame stroke, so a rail drawn as a fill and declared a frame cannot pass unseen.
  - The renderer ignores the role and draws a declared frame stroke as any other, so no picture changes; `mass-audit.mjs` does not draw it.
  - The frame audit leaves each declared frame stroke out of the box of `reviewedBalance` (`frame-audit/composition.mjs:181`), which its build-up, held, settled, finished and far runs read, and records per scene the declared frame strokes it left out, their number and the box they span (`declared_frames`), refusing nothing for them.

### 4. The limits, and the runs they make

- **Rests on:** rule 3: a settled state of 2 s or more within 50 px of x 960 by the box and 90 px by the mass, and within 50 px of y 510 by the box; a build-up of 1.5 s or more within 90 px by both; lit pixels "of the stage" [S1, §14]. The prototype weighs the paints of the stage's scene alone and calls a state a run whose box and mass centres stay within 4 px (Context).
- **Chosen because:**
  - **What is weighed, one rule for both:** the mass and the vertical runs weigh, in each frame, the paints of the scene being drawn: every paint drawn while `window.__sceneId` names a scene, declared frame strokes left out; a paint of no scene (titles, chapter cards and credits drawn outside every scene) is not weighed. Their runs are keyed by that scene, so a run ends where the scene drawn changes. This is the films session's decision under delegation of 26 November, recorded here: the eye sees what is painted. The frame's timeline segment is not the scene drawn: the fused composition and DDWG go on drawing the outgoing scene for about 0.7 to 1.3 s into each continuing segment, until the incoming scene's cut (`preview-034-ch1-5/picture/film.js:8016-8024, 8057-8060`; DDWG's `picture/film.js:16824-16828, 16860-16862`; read by the second reader [S7]), and the sample draws scene i−1 until 0.6 s before scene i's voice (`picture/film.js:300-301`); keyed by the segment, those frames would weigh nothing. A frame in which two scenes are drawn at once weighs both, as the eye sees both; the sample, DDWG and the prototype draw one scene at a time. In the mass audit the wrapper draws a paint while `window.__sceneId` names a scene and records which; the vertical runs take the box of the scene paints in `reviewedBalance`'s frame and the scene they belong to. The reviewers' box keeps its paints outside scenes and its runs keyed by the segment, as today: its rules are the reviewers', and changing what they count is not this Change's.
  - **Titles drawn inside a scene are weighed.** DDWG and the fused prototype draw their opening title inside scene 01 (Context). The prototype leaves out texts of role `title`, `chapter` and `caption` by the role a composition writes in its layout (`balance-stages.mjs:135`). The engine reads the roles `title` and `caption` by convention elsewhere (`rules/frame.mjs:118-119`, `frame-audit/composition.mjs:224`, `motion-audit.mjs:149`), but the native canvas's `fillText` carries no role at the moment it draws, and the layout that names a text's role is published only after the frame; so the mass audit cannot leave a text out by its role, and weighs by the scene a paint is drawn in. That follows rule 3, which leaves out only the head band, the caption and the chip: a title drawn inside a stage is lit pixels of that stage. A title drawn inside a scene is therefore weighed; a film that does not want it weighed draws it outside the scene. Task 8.1 names this as a difference from the prototype, not an error; D-70's 01.1 is where it is likely to show.
  - **The mass:** the mass rule for a settled state (90 px for 2 s) lies inside the build-up rule (90 px for 1.5 s), so one run kind holds both: `off_centre_mass`, each run of 1.5 s or more within one scene through which the mass's centre stands more than 90 px off x 960, moving or not, with its span and its largest signed offset; at the step of 0.25 s that is six samples or more. A run is ended by a frame in which another scene is drawn, by a gap in the samples, and by a frame with fewer than 40 lit cells, which has no mass (the floor `balance79.py` uses). This is stricter than the prototype, which judges only still states and no moving stretch; rule 3's build-up limit names no stillness, and the engine's build-up rule for the box already reads it so (`reviewedRuns`, `rules/balance.mjs:151`).
  - **The vertical:** a still-state rule, as rule 3 limits only a settled state by y and sets no vertical limit on a build-up. `off_centre_vertical` is each run of 2 s or more within one scene through which the scene box's four edges stay within 4 px of where they stood when it began, so the whole box is still, and its centre stands more than 50 px off y 510. A row of names that grows sideways is no still state and makes no run. A run is ended by a stage cut the composition declares, another scene, a gap or a frame without a lit scene box, and is timed from its first still frame. The prototype's state also holds the mass still; the engine holds the box, the measure the vertical limit is set on, and nothing tighter.
  - **Why the vertical runs differ from the horizontal still runs:** those are timed from the glide that settles into them and split into held, settled and finished (`rules/balance.mjs:69-94`) because DDWG's reviews of x counted each (ZH6-1 [S5]); rule 3 sets one settled limit for y, and since every still class is gated, one class gates the same states. A vertical glide into a still state starts its run at its first still frame, which is more lenient than x by the glide's length.
  - Exactly 90 px and exactly 50 px pass, as rule 3's "within" says. Both count every frame, spoken or not, as the reviewers' runs do.
- **Applies to:** `rules/balance.mjs`: `massRuns(step)` and `verticalRuns(step)` beside `reviewedRuns`, their limits as `MASS = { px: 90, seconds: 1.5, cells: 40, step: 0.25 }` and `VERTICAL = { px: 50, seconds: 2, still: 4 }`, each citing rule 3.

### 5. The grey the mass is read on

- **Rests on:** the engine holds two definitions of a lit mark, and neither is the prototype's. `litAtFullSize` tests a paint's colour at full size, brighter than grey 60 by BT.601 luma (`rules/ink.mjs:64-79`); `litLevel` lights a 480 × 270 cell above grey 48 by BT.709 weights and a share model (`rules/ink.mjs:39-59`). The prototype, whose way the engine is asked to measure (D-69, rule 4), thresholds at 60 the grey that FFmpeg's `format=gray` gives of the decoded picture at 480 × 270 (`balance79.py`). The second reader measured that filter on a picture encoded by the engine's own conversion (`native-canvas.mjs:58-59`, tagged limited range): RGB (60, 60, 60) gives grey 59 where the Y plane holds 68, and white gives 253 where Y holds 235 [S7]. The prototype's grey is therefore the full-range luma, and the reviewers' measure since round 29 thresholds the same full-range grey at 60 (`rules/ink.mjs:64-66`).
- **Options:** (a) reuse `FULL_SIZE_LIT_GRAY` on the cells under a second meaning; (b) the limited-range Y the encoder writes, above 60, which lights cells about nine levels fainter than the prototype ((60 − 16) × 255 / 219 = 51.2); (c) a definition of its own that reproduces the prototype's full-range grey.
- **Chosen because:** (c), one constant of its own, `MASS_LIT_GRAY = 60` with `massLit` in `rules/ink.mjs`, citing `balance79.py` and the reviewers' grey: each cell is the mean colour of the 4 × 4 pixels it covers, its grey that colour's full-range BT.709 luma (0 to 255), and it is lit above 60. FFmpeg's `format=gray` gives that grey within about 2 levels [S7]; on the sample's palette BT.709 and BT.601 luma differ by at most 6.6 levels and by under 1 level for its ground, line, ink and muted colours. Each frame is drawn on the composition's ground (decision 1), so a cell a mark half covers reads as it does in the rendered picture: the half-covered 2 px line of the Context reads 67.4, lit, over the ground, where over the canvas's starting black it would read 57.4, not lit. The content band is rows 30 to 229 of the grey, the composition's rows 120 to 919 (`REVIEWED_BAND`), which leaves out the head band and the chip. The mass's centre is the mean of the lit cells' centres in the composition's 1920 × 1080 frame, given as offsets from `(960, 510)`. The prototype reduced with FFmpeg's default bicubic scaling after compression, not an area mean of drawn pixels; task 7.1 bounds the difference.

### 6. What it gates, what it records and what it binds

### 7. The other measures of the frame audit keep what they count

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A frame is left undeclared] → it counts in both measures; it centres the box as today, and pulls the mass by its share only, 8 to 36 px on the fused previews, so a large lean still refuses (decision 2).
- [A stroke of content is declared a frame] → it leaves both measures and no gate refuses it; `declared_frames` names every stroke a declaration took out, for the reader and the reviewers.
- [The mass audit sets `check-picture`'s length] → its phase is held to the frame audit's phase of the same run, read by task 6.5 from the run record on a film of a real edition's length; it samples every 0.25 s, with spans in parallel processes as the fallback (decision 1).
- [`check-picture` runs more often] → decision 1 states the reruns the uncaptioned stage adds.
- [The engine's mass differs from the prototype's or the reviewers'] → task 7.1 compares it with the decoded uncaptioned picture's mass on the sample, threshold and floor included; task 8.1 sets the vertical runs beside D-70's stages on the base they were counted on, and task 8.2 runs the whole check on the recomposed copies, with the differences named.
- [Stages pass by one sample] → the rules sample at fixed steps (0.25 s and 1/30 s), as every balance rule does; the lessons ask for margins (film-picture-lessons [S5]).
