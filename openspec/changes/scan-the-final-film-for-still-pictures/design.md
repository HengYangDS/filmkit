## Context

See [proposal.md](proposal.md). What the engine holds, read at `99d36fe` [S1]:

- `filmkit.picture.still_scan` decodes a film at 10 fps to 480x270 grey, twice on a film of 60 fps (two phases 0.05 s apart), calls a frame still by two rules, and lists each still run with the speech it carries. `record_scan` writes a record that names the film, the timeline and the composition with their SHA-256 and the runs with 3.5 s of speech or more. `record_draft` writes an edition's draft record.
- The scan is reached through a draft alone: `produce render-draft`, and the scan's own command with `--language`. `preflight` asks for the draft record only of a film that declares drafts. The scan's general form, `<movie> <timeline> --out <record>`, writes a record that no step reads.
- A record's owner requires it. `equivalence.require_equivalence` and `rendering.require_passing_gates` each refuse a missing record, a record that binds another input and a record that did not pass, name the command to run, and are called by the steps that depend on the record. The verifier lists the owners' refusals among its own.
- The verifier (`filmkit.delivery.verification`) runs its bindings, then decodes the films, then reads the review unless `--technical-only` is given. A film's proof gate and its build both run it with `--technical-only` [S5].

What happened is in the proposal; its records are [S2] and [S4].

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- No edition is verified or delivered, and no review round is prepared, whose film the engine's scan has not passed.
- The question "is this record the film's, and does it pass" has one owner.
- A draft's scan is described as what it is.

**Non-Goals:**

- Changing the scan: its rules, its phases, its limit and its sample of the film stay as they are. What a record states of a run's speech changes by at most a hundredth (decision 14).
- Changing the draft: its size, its frame rate and its record stay as they are.
- Rebuilding a released edition, or correcting one.
- Reconciling the reviewers' measure with the engine's. The two differ by 1.66 s on one run [S2] [S4]; why was not looked into.
- A declaration of runs a film keeps (decision 7).

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, where it applies, what would reopen it, and its sources.

1. **The step is an action of `filmkit.produce`, `scan-film`, and not a mode of the scan's own command.**
   - **Rests on:** `produce` gives each of its seventeen actions the edition by `--language`, a duration record named after the action, the refusal line `<action>: <reason>`, and the end of its children on a request to end. The scan's own command has two forms, exits with status 1 and no reason, and writes no duration record [S1].
   - **Considered:** (a) a third form of the scan's command, `--language <code> --final`, whose film argument would have to become optional and which would repeat the duration record and the refusal line; (b) an action of `produce`; (c) the scan as the last phase of `package-film`, with no new command.
   - **Chosen because:** (b) adds one name to the action table and no option. (a) grows a parser by a mode to gain what `produce` already gives. (c) puts a second verdict into packaging: a film refused for a still picture would read as a packaging that did not complete, after its films are in place, and a packaged film could not be scanned without being packaged again.
   - **Applies to:** every film's build, once for each edition after `package-film`.
   - **Reopened when:** the scan needs an input that only packaging holds, or the two are shown never to run apart.
   - **Sources:** [S1].
2. **The record is `build/evidence/editions/<code>/film-stills.json`, of the 1080p captioned film, and it names the engine's version.**
   - **Rests on:** an edition's records stand under its evidence folder, the draft's as `draft-stills.json` (`production.Edition`). The gate records and the equivalence record bind the engine's version beside their inputs. The 1080p captioned film is the film a review names and a viewer watches, and it is the film on which the run was found [S1] [S2].
   - **Considered:** (a) the draft record's place, one record for both, which a draft and a film would overwrite in turn and which `preflight` reads for what drew a draft; (b) a field of `film-package.json`, a record that packaging owns and a set-aside moves; (c) a record of its own. For the film: the UHD master, four times the pixels to decode for a scan that reads 480x270; all four films.
   - **Chosen because:** (c) gives the scan's verdict one place and one writer. The 1080p captioned film is the one the measure was found to matter on; the uncaptioned films are held to the captioned ones by the equivalence record. The engine's version is bound because the rules and the limit are the engine's: a record of another engine was judged by rules this one may not hold.
   - **Applies to:** `Edition.film_stills`, `still_scan.SCANNED_FILM`.
   - **Reopened when:** a still run is found in a UHD or uncaptioned film that its 1080p captioned film does not hold.
   - **Sources:** [S1] [S2].
3. **`still_scan.require_film_scan` is the one owner of whether an edition passes, and `still_scan.film_record` the one reader of its record.**
   - **Rests on:** the two precedents named in the Context; `verification.py` holds 493 non-blank lines of the 500 a source file may hold; `preflight` imports the verifier, so the verifier cannot import `preflight`; `preflight.draft` held its own comparison of a record's bindings [S1]. In this Change's first form three places read the record's fields, each with its own assumptions about them [S9].
   - **Considered:** (a) the rules in the verifier; (b) the rules in `preflight`, beside the draft's; (c) the rules with the record's writer.
   - **Chosen because:** (c) is where the record's shape is known, it is importable by every caller, and the verifier gains one call. `film_record` returns a record only when it answers (decision 10), so the requirement, the scan's answer without a decode and any later reader ask one function. The reader itself is `filmkit.picture.still_record.answer`, which holds no rule of the scan and reads the draft's record too (decision 16), so no second statement of a record's shape or of its bindings stands.
   - **Applies to:** the verifier, the delivery (decision 13), a review round's preparation and `preflight.draft`. Four paths do not ask the requirement, each because it gives another verdict or none: `package-film`, where a still picture would be a second verdict in packaging (decision 1); a set-aside, which asks no reader of the record (decision 15); `review.round record`, whose round asked when it was prepared (decision 6); and the reproduction (`delivery.reproduction`), which compares a rebuild with the published release and is no step that every release passes, as the verifier is (decision 6).
   - **Reopened when:** not applicable.
   - **Sources:** [S1] [S9].
4. **A film record answers only under the engine's own rules, its limit among them.**
   - **Rests on:** the scan's general form takes `--limit` and `--out`, so a record at the film record's place may have been written with any limit; between two releases the scan's constants can change under one version string, and an editable install keeps one version across commits [S1]. The review's demonstrations: a record that states a limit of 99 s and lists no run of 3.5 s passed, and a record written under other rules was answered from without a decode [S9].
   - **Considered:** (a) applying the engine's limit to the runs a record lists and naming the record's limit beside it, this Change's first form; (b) refusing a record whose stated rules are not the engine's, the limit being one of them; (c) binding a digest of the scan's source.
   - **Chosen because:** (b). The listed runs depend on every rule but the limit, so (a) reads a record of other pixels, levels, band or phases as if it were the engine's. With (b) one comparison covers every rule a record states, a laxer and a stricter limit are refused alike, and the refusal names the record's limit beside the engine's where it is another number, with `scan-film`. (c) would refuse a record for a changed comment. The version stays bound beside the rules: a release may change the scan's code under unchanged constants.
   - **Applies to:** `still_scan.rules`, which is what a record states: the rate, the size, the band, the phases, the filters of the decode as FFmpeg is given them (the scaler and the grey conversion), both rules' texts and the coarse rule's share as a number, the least speech a run is reported with, how a run's speech is recorded, and the limit. What the scan's code does beyond these (which frames the coarse measure keys on, where a run begins and ends) no rule states: the engine's version, which a film record binds, stands for it. A draft record is read by the same comparison (decision 16).
   - **Reopened when:** a reading is found to depend on something that neither a stated rule nor the engine's version moves. The second review found three such things that no rule stated (the scaler, the grey conversion and the cut of speech), and they are stated now [S11].
   - **Sources:** [S1] [S9] [S11].
5. **A second scan of an unchanged film decodes nothing.**
   - **Rests on:** a scan costs 96 to 126 s an edition at the loads measured [S3]; a build's package task is run again after a refusal elsewhere; `admitted` already answers from a receipt without repeating its checks [S1].
   - **Considered:** (a) decoding on every run; (b) answering from a record that binds the film, the timeline, the composition and the engine as they stand; (c) (b) with an option to force a scan.
   - **Chosen because:** (b). A record that answers (decision 10) binds or states what a scan's reading depends on, as decision 4 bounds it, so the answer does not differ. The printed line and the duration record say `scanned: false`, so a build's durations do not state a scan that did not run. Removing the record forces one; an option for that is one more thing to document for no case in hand.
   - **Applies to:** `scan_film`. A refused film is refused again from its record, with the same runs. A record that does not answer, a damaged one included, is scanned over.
   - **Reopened when:** two builds of FFmpeg are shown to read one film otherwise at the scan's thresholds. The record does not bind the FFmpeg that decoded.
   - **Sources:** [S1] [S3].
6. **The delivery's verification, in both scopes, and a review round's preparation require the record.**
   - **Rests on:** each film's proof gate `film-media` and its build task `film:verify` run the verifier with `--technical-only` [S5]; the acceptance scope reads the review's record, so by itself it runs after a round; a round's prompts bind it to exact films [S1], and since the Change `prepare-a-review-round` a round's preparation, `review.round prepare`, writes them last, after every check that only reads.
   - **Considered:** (a) the verifier alone; (b) a round's preparation alone, which a release without a new round would not pass; (c) both; (d) `package-film` refusing, which is decision 1's (c).
   - **Chosen because:** (c). The verifier is the step every release passes, by a film's own gate. A round's preparation is the last step before other people's and reviewers' time is spent on a film; the requirement is one of its checks that only read, so it refuses before anything is derived or written and does not depend on a task list having run the verifier first. A preparation asks those checks twice, before it derives anything and again when the prompts are written from the round's own copies of the note and the dispositions; each edition's requirement is so asked twice, and each asking reads the edition's record and hashes the three files it binds.
   - **Applies to:** `verification.verify_bindings` (the bound inputs that the verifier's command and the release record's writer ask, after the package's) and `prompts.read_round` (the checks a round's preparation asks before it derives or writes anything, for both editions; the scan's refusal is raised as its own and reported as the round's in one line).
   - **Reopened when:** a review is wanted of a film known to hold a run, for a second opinion on it; or the reviewers' prompt is to cite the engine's record beside their own measure, which is a field of the films' templates and a Change of its own.
   - **Sources:** [S1] [S5].
7. **No film declares a run it keeps.**
   - **Rests on:** one run at the limit in four editions measured [S3], which its film's own record reads as a scene to change [S4]; the draft `decide-before-heavy-work`, decision 1, which gives a recorded finding the form of a declaration by identity with a reason, stale ones refused [S6].
   - **Considered:** (a) no declaration; (b) declared runs by their seconds; (c) declared runs by the thought they stand under.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). One case is not a class, and that case is a defect by its film's own reading. A run named by its seconds goes stale with every narration; one named by its thought needs a rule for a run that spans two, which no case yet informs.
   - **Applies to:** every film.
   - **Reopened when:** a film holds a run at the limit that its review accepts as designed, such as a quotation held on the screen while it is read. The declaration then takes the form of [S6], and its identity is decided on that case.
   - **Sources:** [S3] [S4] [S6].
8. **The draft's scan stays as it is and is described as an early warning.**
   - **Rests on:** one label's place read every 0.2 s on four pictures of one scene: both drafts at 1920x1080 set its text one whole pixel higher before 845.6 s than after, and the film at both its sizes holds it still; the drafts then end at 845.5 s a still run that the film holds to 847.4 s. Read as text set on whole pixels of the size it is drawn at; the scene's code was not read. Over four editions the two drafts differ from each other in 16 groups of runs; the film's runs equal the browser draft's in 8 of them, the native draft's in 5 and neither's in 3 [S2].
   - **Considered:** (a) removing the draft's scan; (b) drawing the draft at the film's size; (c) keeping it and saying what it settles.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). A draft still shows a long still run an hour before a render does. (b) is an open question of the draft `faster-films-less-by-hand`, with its cost unmeasured [S7]; this Change does not decide it.
   - **Applies to:** the module texts of the scan, the draft and the pre-render check, one requirement added to `render-preflight`, and the README.
   - **Reopened when:** that open question is measured.
   - **Sources:** [S2] [S7].
9. **The sample film's package task runs the step, after its two final films read at least 0.5 s under the limit.**
   - **Rests on:** the sample film is what the engine's tests load and its acceptance builds; its Chinese draft's longest still run read 2.5 s by a browser and 3.32 s by the native canvas, against the limit of 3.5 s [S8]. Decision 8 says what a draft's reading is worth for the film: in the one case measured a film read 1.54 s more than its drafts [S2]. The final films of two earlier builds of the sample, neither drawn by this engine's head, read under the limit by the scan on 19 November 2026: the Chinese film 3.28 s by the browser's build and 3.32 s by the native canvas's (2.9 to 7.1 s and to 7.2 s), the English film 2.98 s by both (4.0 to 7.9 s) [S10]. The Chinese film stands 0.18 s under the limit, and neither is a reading of the build that `tasks.md` 2.1 asks for. The scan's thresholds stand close to what renderers vary by (Risks), and the two builds already differ by one sample at that run's end, so a film this close is not one to hang every later sample build on [S11].
   - **Considered:** a task of its own between the package and the verification; two lines in the package task. For the order: landing the task and proving it by the next whole build; reading the two films first. For the scene that reads too close: its three cards arriving one after another from the left, which the films' session reports the picture gate to refuse as a build that stands off the centre (`off_centre_builds`; not rerun for this Change); the middle card standing with the scene and the two beside it arriving together on a word of the scene's first sentence.
   - **Chosen because:** the package task is where a film's task list holds `package-film` for each edition [S5], so an adopting film adds its two lines in the same place, and no stage is added to a build's list. The reading comes first because a film at the limit would leave every commit from the task's on with a sample build that stops at its package task, and reading two films of 60 s costs seconds where a build costs a host ticket. The reading must stand at least 0.5 s under the limit, the band inside which `draw-pictures-natively` lists a released edition's runs (its task 5.4). By the readings so far the Chinese film does not, so its first scene changes in a step of this Change before the reading (`tasks.md` 2.3), by the parent session's decision of 19 November 2026: the three cards stood from the scene's start and left the picture still for the whole of its first sentence. With the middle card standing and the two beside it arriving on a word of that sentence, the row opens from its centre and stays centred, and the drafts' longest still runs of speech read 2.5 s and 2.07 s where they read 3.32 s and 2.98 s [S12]. A draft's reading settles nothing about the film (decision 8): the reading of 2.1 does.
   - **Applies to:** `.config/mise/config.toml`, `sample:package`; `tasks.md` 2.1 before 2.2. One test holds the task's two lines (`tests/test_sample_tasks.py`), which nothing but a whole build ran.
   - **Reopened when:** two builds of one scene read further apart than the band of 0.5 s.
   - **Sources:** [S5] [S8] [S10] [S11].
10. **One reader of a still scan's record, and the command a refusal names repairs any record.**
    - **Rests on:** the review's demonstrations [S9]: a record cut to half its bytes, an empty file, a list, a bound name that is no object, a missing field or a speech stated as text ended the verifier, the prompts and `scan-film` with a traceback, and `scan-film`, which every other refusal names, read the record first and died on it. `repository.read_entry` is the engine's reader of an entry that may be damaged [S1]. The second review found what still ended a reader with an error: a body nested deeper than the JSON reader follows, a run whose figure lies past a float's range, and inputs other than the record (an answer of ffprobe that is no frame rate, a timeline whose duration is no finite positive number or whose words cannot be read); and it found fields of the scan's that the reader did not ask for [S11].
    - **Considered:** (a) catching the errors at each caller; (b) one reader that returns a record only when it has the scan's fields and their types, and says why otherwise; (c) a schema file for the record.
    - **Chosen because:** (b). The shape is known where the record is written, three callers cannot then differ, and `scan-film` reads "does not answer" as "scan", so no damaged record needs a hand. (c) puts a second statement of the shape beside the writer, for one record.
    - **Applies to:** `filmkit.picture.still_record.answer` and its `NoAnswerError`, which `still_scan.film_record` and `still_scan.draft_record` call: every field the scan writes, of the scan's type, and each listed run inside the film with no more speech than its length. `repository.read_entry` reads a body it cannot follow as one that cannot be read. `still_scan.frame_rate`, `decode`, `aligned`, `film_frames` and `judged` refuse what they cannot read by the file's name. The scan counts a run's speech at no more than the run lasts, so a timeline whose words overlap cannot make it write a record that its own reader refuses; in the eight timelines read (six released editions and the sample's two) no two words overlap [S11]. The scan's own command (`still_scan.main`) turns what the scan cannot read into its refusal in one line, as `scan-film` does. `require_film_scan` turns it into the refusal: the film "has no still scan record", or its record "cannot be read", "binds another" film, timeline, composition or engine, "states other rules than the engine's" or "cannot be trusted", each with `scan-film`. `scan_film` turns it into a scan.
    - **Reopened when:** another record of the engine is found whose readers disagree on its shape; the pattern then belongs with `repository`.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S9] [S11].
11. **A record must agree with itself, and is not sealed.**
    - **Rests on:** a record states its verdict three times (the runs it lists, its runs at the limit, its longest speech), its limit twice (its rules', its own) and the frames it read. It also states its rate, both rules' wording and the number of its phases beside its `rules`, for readers that take one field: the draft's refusal reads a record's limit, and the tables of 18 November read a general-form record's frames and limit [S3] [S11]. The review emptied the listed runs of a refused film's record, and the verifier passed it while its other statements still named the run [S9]. No record of the engine carries a seal [S1].
    - **Considered:** (a) reading one statement and ignoring the others, the first form; (b) requiring the statements to agree; (c) (b) and a digest of the record's content that a reader recomputes; (d) decoding the film at each reading; (e) stating each rule once, which removes fields that every form of the scan reports and that records already written hold.
    - **Chosen because:** (b), for every pair: two statements of one thing must be equal. It is the smaller change, where (e) alters what every form of the scan reports for no reader's gain. An edit that leaves a contradiction is the one a slip or a half-written tool makes, and refusing it costs nothing. (c) shows an edit only to someone who does not recompute the digest, whom (b) already stops, and adds a field every writer must keep. (d) costs a decode at every verification. A record guards against a scan that was forgotten or is stale, as every record of the engine does, and not against someone who rewrites it consistently. The review's guarantee named a record edited by hand: it is met for an edit that leaves a contradiction and struck for a consistent rewriting, by the parent session's decision of 18 November 2026.
    - **Applies to:** `still_record.contradictions`, read by `still_record.answer`: the limit, the rate, both rules' wording, the phases, the runs at the limit, the longest speech and the frames read.
    - **Reopened when:** a record is found edited.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S3] [S9] [S11].
12. **A record binds the film that was decoded, and only a film that was read whole.**
    - **Rests on:** the record's hashes were taken after the decodes, about 100 s an edition [S3], so a film replaced meanwhile got a passing record of bytes that no scan read; and the scan asked nothing of the frames it read, so a record of one frame passed [S9]. A timeline states its film's seconds (`duration`) [S1].
    - **Considered:** for the binding: hashing after the decode; hashing before it and after it; reading from a private copy of the film. For the frames: no check; the film's declared seconds; the seconds of the timeline the record binds.
    - **Chosen because:** hashing before and after refuses exactly the case, either file changed meanwhile, for two more reads of files the scan reads anyway; a copy costs the film's size on disk for every scan. The timeline is bound by the record, so writer and reader compare the frames with what they bind and need no second source.
    - **Applies to:** `still_scan.judged`, for every form of the scan; `still_scan.whole` for the frames on writing, of a film (`scan_film`) and of a draft (`record_draft`), whose timeline is the edition's, and `still_record.answer` on reading. A decode that ends early is so refused for a draft as for a film, where the first repair left a draft reported as a shorter film [S11]. The general form asks nothing of the frames: its timeline may be any.
    - **Reopened when:** a packaged film is found whose frames differ by design from its timeline's seconds at the scan's rate.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S3] [S9] [S11].
13. **The delivery asks the same owner.**
    - **Rests on:** `filmkit.delivery.folder` fills the delivery folder after a player check and asks nothing of the verifier, whose run is left to a film's task order, as its module text says [S1]; `package-film` followed by the delivery command planned the delivery of a film that was never scanned [S9].
    - **Considered:** (a) leaving it to the task order and saying so; (b) one call to `require_film_scan` for each edition before anything is copied; (c) having the delivery require a record of the whole verification.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) costs one asking an edition (decision 6 states what an asking reads and hashes), holds in a dry run too, and makes "no order of commands delivers an unscanned film" true of this gate. (c) concerns every binding the verifier checks, predates this Change, and stands under Open Questions.
    - **Applies to:** `folder.deliver`, after the destination and the members are checked and before the checksum list, the player check and rsync. The requirement reads the records of the film the command runs in, so `deliver` refuses a root that is another folder, whose members would be delivered on that film's scans [S11].
    - **Reopened when:** that open question is decided.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S9] [S11].
14. **Speech is recorded cut to whole hundredths, and the limit has one wording.**
    - **Rests on:** a run of 3.496 s was recorded as 3.5 s and refused, the comparison being made on the rounded figure, and the messages said "over 3.5 s" of a rule that is "3.5 s or more" [S9]. Word times are floats: 3.5 s can stand as 3.4999999999999996.
    - **Considered:** (a) comparing the unrounded speech and recording it rounded, which leaves a record whose reader cannot repeat the writer's decision at the boundary; (b) recording more decimals; (c) cutting to hundredths after rounding off what lies under a microsecond.
    - **Chosen because:** (c). The limit is stated in hundredths, so the cut figure stands at the limit when the measured one does, to the microsecond that is rounded off first, and writer and reader decide alike from the record.
    - **Applies to:** `still_scan.cut`, in every form of the scan, so a film's recorded speech can read a hundredth lower than by the earlier form. The scan's, the draft's and the pre-render check's messages and both capabilities say "3.5 s of speech or more" or "at the limit". The scan's command refuses a `--limit` that is not in whole hundredths, which a cut figure would be judged wrongly against [S11].
    - **Reopened when:** a limit finer than hundredths is wanted.
    - **Sources:** [S9] [S11].
15. **A set-aside moves the film's record with its films.**
    - **Rests on:** `set-aside` moved an edition's films and package record and left `film-stills.json`, which then bound a film that had moved until the next scan overwrote it [S9].
    - **Considered:** leaving the record in place; moving it as a member when it stands.
    - **Chosen because:** the record is those films' scan record, whatever it says. A set-aside asks no reader of it and calls it no evidence of a pass: an unreviewed set-aside may move a failing or a stale one [S11]. As a member it gets the manifest's hash and its readback, like the package record. A build of an engine before the scan holds none and is set aside as before.
    - **Applies to:** `set_aside.edition_members`.
    - **Reopened when:** not applicable.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S9] [S11].
16. **A draft's record is read by the reader of the film's record.**
    - **Rests on:** the first repair kept a reading of the draft's own, the engine's limit on the runs a record lists. The second review showed what that left: a draft record whose list of runs was emptied while its runs at the limit still named a run of 3.9 s admitted a render; a draft record cut short, empty or holding a list ended the pre-render check with an error; and a draft whose decode ended early was recorded as a shorter film and passed [S11]. Of the three films and the sample, only the sample declares drafts [S1].
    - **Considered:** (a) the draft's own reading, repaired case by case; (b) the film record's reader for both, a draft binding its timeline and the composition and no engine.
    - **Chosen because:** (b), by the parent session's decision of 19 November 2026, which overrules the first repair's choice. One reader cannot disagree with itself, and a draft's record then has the scan's whole shape, states the engine's rules and the frames of its timeline's seconds, and agrees with itself, as a film's does. The cost: a draft record that an engine before this Change wrote states no rules and no frames, so it cannot be read, and `render-draft` draws the draft again. Only the sample's build meets that.
    - **Applies to:** `still_scan.draft_record`, which `preflight.draft` calls, and `still_scan.record_draft`, which refuses a draft that does not decode to its timeline's seconds and leaves no record. A draft binds no engine version: its record admits a render and nothing after it (decision 8). What drew the draft is asked by `preflight` as before (`drawn_by`).
    - **Reopened when:** a film's draft is scanned against another timeline than its edition's.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S11].
17. **One decode gives both phases their frames.**
    - **Rests on:** a film of 60 fps was decoded twice, once a phase, every frame of it each time, though a phase keeps one frame in six; a scan cost 96 to 126 s an edition at the loads measured [S3], and every edition's build now pays the step. A decode yields its frames in the film's order, so one process that keeps the frames of both phases yields one of each in turn [S1].
    - **Considered:** (a) a decode a phase, as before; (b) one FFmpeg process that keeps the frames of both phases, dealt to their phases in order; (c) two processes at once, which halves the wall seconds and not the work, on a host whose cores other builds share; (d) decoding less of each frame, by hardware or by keyframes, which changes which pixels or frames a phase reads.
    - **Chosen because:** (b) decodes the film once and changes nothing a phase reads: the same frames, through the same filter, in the same order. Its gain is not stated before it is measured on a whole film (`tasks.md` 5.2).
    - **Applies to:** `still_scan.decode` and `sampling` (the phases whose frames one decode keeps, as FFmpeg's select expression), `Stills` (both measures taken a frame at a time) and `phase_reports` (the dealing), in every form of the scan. Phases that select the same frames, as on a draft of 10 fps, read one report, as before.
    - **Reopened when:** a phase's report from the one decode differs from that of a decode of its own (the tests of `tests/picture/test_still_decode.py` on a stand-in for the FFmpeg process, `test_one_decode_reports_each_phase_as_a_decode_of_its_own_does` with FFmpeg, and the ticket's comparison on a released film), or the measurement shows no gain.
    - **Sources:** [S1] [S3].

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A film adopts the engine and its build stops at the verification. → The refusal names `scan-film`; the Changelog and the README say where the two lines go.
- The ETHOS film's Chinese edition cannot be verified on this engine as its picture stands. → That is the defect this Change exists to hold. Its film's record already names the scene [S4].
- The scan's thresholds stand close to what encoders and renderers vary by: three of the largest differences between two drafts of one edition stood at 12 counted pixels against 13 and 15, and at 25 levels against 24 [S2]. A film just under the limit on one build may read just over it on the next. → The limit is not moved; a film that reads within a few tenths of it is a film to change. No margin is added without a case.
- The sample film is such a film. By the scan, on 19 November 2026, its Chinese final film read 3.28 s in a browser's build and 3.32 s in a native canvas's (2.9 to 7.1 s and to 7.2 s), 0.18 s under the limit, and its English film 2.98 s in both (4.0 to 7.9 s) [S10]. → `tasks.md` 2.1 asks for 0.5 s under the limit on a build of this engine's head before the sample's package task scans its films (decision 9); the scene changes in this Change (decision 9, `tasks.md` 2.3), and the steps that follow wait for the reading.
- A build paid 96 to 126 s an edition with a decode a phase [S3]; with one decode its seconds are not yet measured (decision 17). → Once for each packaged film; decision 5.
- A change to any constant of the scan makes every film record stale, and so does an engine release. → That is what the two comparisons are for; the cost is one build step for each edition.
- A record of the first form of this Change, or of the scan's general form before it, states no rules and no frames. → At the film record's place it is named as one that cannot be read, and `scan-film` writes a new one. No released engine wrote a film record. A draft record of an engine before this Change is named the same way by the pre-render check, and `render-draft` draws the draft again (decision 16).

- What `scan-film` itself reads on real films. Four released editions are measured by the same scan through its general form [S3]. The DDWG film's two editions are read by the draft tickets of 19 November 2026, outside this Change.
- Whether the sample film's two final films pass on this engine's head. `tasks.md` 2.1; decision 9 states what two earlier builds read.
- Whether the delivery should require the pass of the whole verification, and not of the still scan alone. It asks none of the verifier's other bindings and relies on a film's task order; that predates this Change (decision 13).
- How many cores the decode keeps busy, what the one decode takes against two (`tasks.md` 5.2), and what a scan costs on an edition of 1,500 s. Not measured.
- Whether the verifier's own decode of the 1080p film could feed the scan, which would save a decode. Not looked into: the verifier decodes to count frames and reads no pixels today [S1].
