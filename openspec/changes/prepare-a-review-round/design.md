## Context

The draft Change `decide-before-heavy-work` leaves "the review round's changes note, dispositions and release documents" to a later Change; its decision 17 names `filmkit.review.round prepare` and its decision 20 is about `review.round record`. This Change admits the part of it that prepares a round. Its proposal states why and what changes; its specs state the behavior. This design records how the slice is built, where it departs from the draft's wording and why, and what stays in the draft. A mark such as [S2] names an entry under Sources.

What the engine holds, read at `db7a837` (0.24.1) on 20 November 2026 [S2]:

- `review.round bind` lists the members of a delivery, the viewing members' names taken from the package records, and computes each member's SHA-256 from its file; `review.listening_plan --out` and `review.measurement --out` each write one record; `review.round prompts` fills the film's template and requires only that those three files exist in the round's directory, not that they belong to the build. The prompts cite the directory they are written to.
- A round's prompts bind the edition's films: `assemble` and `refresh-captions` refuse until the round is set aside, by its reviews or, unreviewed, by a consumer folder that holds the films' copies.
- The measurement requires a sync record that states the timeline and composition it was traced on, and only `picture.sync_audit --bind --out` writes one. `check-picture` runs the same trace, derives the same report, and its gate record binds that report by path and SHA-256 beside the composition's and the timeline's SHA-256. The measurement already reads the motion report that way.
- `delivery.folder` reads the players back in a real browser from the build's members before it copies anything; the check writes a receipt and one screenshot a player beside it.
- The film verifier refuses a material finding; `review.round record` refuses one through the level check, for a film that declares levels. Neither names another severity.

What the films' kit holds, read the same day [S3]: a preparation script that refuses an existing round directory, a changes note with a placeholder left, a dispositions file that is not JSON and a film with more than one author; makes a preview delivery for the players' readback and copies its receipt into the round; runs a second trace for the bound sync record; copies the changes note and the dispositions into the round; and then calls the four engine commands in order. After the reviews it refuses a delivery while a review holds a finding of any severity but a note. The script imports `filmkit.delivery.documents_folder`, which 0.23.0 renamed.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- A film prepares a review round with one engine command and no script of its own.
- A round whose prompts exist holds inputs of one build: the one the prompts name.
- A film that declares nothing new keeps its verdicts; no byte of a film depends on this Change.

**Non-Goals:**

- Another review. The dimensions, the floors, the template and the reviewers stay the film's.
- Writing a film's changes note or its dispositions. The author states them; the command keeps them.
- Naming every refusal in one run. The checks that only read take under a second, so a refusal costs a run of seconds.
- Running the reviewers.

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, and where it applies. A decision of the draft is cited by its number there.

1. **One command prepares a round, and the two commands it replaces go.**
   - **Rests on:** the order of a round's steps lives in the kit's script and nowhere in the engine [S3]. `prompts` accepts any three files of the right names, so prompts can be written over another build's measurements [S2]. The trial ran the binding, the listening plan and the measurement by hand on 19 November 2026, each as it is and light, the measurement 36 s on one core; the prompts were refused for want of a previous round and then of dispositions, so no prompt has been written for a film on 0.24 [S1].
   - **Considered:** (a) a task in each film that lists the commands in order, which moves the order into three repositories and leaves `prompts` callable alone; (b) `prepare` beside `prompts` and `bind`, two ways to one result; (c) `prepare` alone.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). A round's inputs are made together, and a caller should not have to know their order.
   - **Applies to:** `filmkit.review.round`: the command `prepare --round <n> [--previous <dir> --dispositions <file>] [--changes <file>]`. Writing the prompts stays a function of the module, tested where it is today.
2. **The checks that read come first, the prompts are written last, and a round is prepared again only while it is nothing but a preparation.**
   - **Rests on:** the kit refuses a round whose directory exists, so a preparation that failed half-way needs a hand before it can run again [S3]. A round's prompts bind its films, so the films of a prepared round cannot be rebuilt until a set-aside, and a set-aside under the round's label keeps that label [S2]. The prompts cite the round's directory, and the player check records the screenshots it writes beside its receipt [S2]. A reviewer writes a record, a report and a working folder into the round; the essentials film's last round also holds a summary the kit wrote after the reviews [S3].
   - **Considered:** (a) deriving in a work folder and moving it into place, which makes the prompts and the receipt name a folder that no longer exists; (b) gathering every refusal before anything is written, a refactoring of each check for a saving of seconds; (c) writing in the round's directory in a fixed order with the prompts last, after the checks that only read, and removing an earlier preparation's files first.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). A round without prompts binds no film and starts no reviewer, so a preparation that stops leaves nothing that can be mistaken for a round.
   - **Applies to:** `prepare`. Before it derives it reads the changes note and the dispositions it was given and runs every check that only reads: that the round's number is not negative; the template's fields, a field the engine does not fill being refused by name; the previous round's review and report for each edition, and its dispositions; the note's text; the soundtracks' program levels; each edition's timeline, for the sentences; and the films and the delivery's members. A dispositions file that the engine's reader cannot decode as JSON, one with a byte order mark or in UTF-16 among them, is refused by name. It refuses a round that holds any entry it does not write, naming the entry, an entry that bears a prepared file's name and is no file and an image the round's receipt does not list among them, and a round whose label holds set-aside films, naming the next number; Finder's `.DS_Store` is no entry, as in a delivery. A preparation's files are the names the round itself gives (the prompts' inputs, the readback's receipt, the copies, the sentences and the prompts), whether a given run writes them or not, and the files that the round's receipt lists under `screenshots`, that lie in the round's directory and that hold the bytes the receipt lists, by their SHA-256, which rests on the owner's rule that each fact has one source and what repeats it is derived from that source [S7]: the receipt is the one statement of what the check wrote beside it, so the round states no screenshot's name; a listed file that holds other bytes is not what the check wrote and is an entry a preparation does not write, so a receipt that lists another file of the round, by hand or by fault, has nothing removed, and a receipt of another shape is a fault, as below. It then removes those files and writes the copies of decision 5 from what it read, the binding, the listening plan, the measurements, the readback, the sentences and the prompts, in that order: the copies may be the round's own and the only ones, so they are back before anything can refuse, and what the measurement refuses is known before the browser starts. A refusal that an owner the command asks raises as its own class is reported as the round's refusal in one line, each class caught by name: the review's `ReviewError`, the delivery module's `DeliveryRefusedError`, the tools' `ToolError` and the renderer's `PictureError`, the measurement's `MediaError` and `LevelError`, the narration flow's `AlignmentError` in the listening plan and the measurement, and the source manifest's `SourceError` in the prompts. A fault is not caught and keeps its traceback: a tool's failed process (`CalledProcessError`), a missing record of the engine's own (`FileNotFoundError`), a record of another shape (`KeyError`). The films of a prepared round are rebuilt only after `set-aside --unreviewed`, by a consumer folder that holds their copies; the README says so.
3. **The measurement reads the sync report through the gate's record.**
   - **Rests on:** the gate record binds the sync report by path and SHA-256 with the composition's and the timeline's SHA-256, and `review/motion.py` reads the motion report through it, refusing a record that is not bound to the current composition and timeline or a report that differs from the one bound [S2]. On the trial build the gate's sync report and the separately bound sync record hold equal values in every shared field, in both editions [S4].
   - **Considered:** (a) `prepare` runs the trace again; (b) `check-picture` writes a second, bound copy of its report, a new file at every gate and nothing for an edition gated before the upgrade; (c) the measurement reads the report the gate record binds, as it does for motion.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). No trace, no new record, no change to the gate, and an edition gated by 0.24 is read as it stands.
   - **Applies to:** `review/measurement.py` and `review/motion.py`: one reader of a report through the gate's record, for motion and for sync. A missing gate record, a record that names no such report and a report missing from the gate's work folder are each a refusal that names `check-picture`. The edition's separate sync record and the option of `picture.sync_audit` that bound it lose their only reader and go.
4. **The round's readback is the player check's receipt for the members the round binds, both from the delivery's own plan.**
   - **Rests on:** the essentials film's prompt calls `player-check-artifacts.json` "the real-browser readback", and that file is the binding, a list of members and digests [S5]. `round.bind` and `delivery.folder.read_players` build the same list of 25 members, in the same order and shape, from two places [S2]. The kit obtains a receipt by delivering to a preview folder [S3]; in the trial a delivery to a throwaway folder took 26 s [S1].
   - **Considered:** (a) keep citing the binding; (b) require a delivery before every round; (c) `prepare` asks the delivery module for its members and their readback and keeps both in the round.
   - **Chosen because:** (c). The players are read from the build's members, as a delivery reads them before it copies, so preparing a round needs no folder outside the build.
   - **Applies to:** `delivery/folder.py`: the players' readback takes the places of its binding and receipt. The round holds `player-check-artifacts.json`, the binding; `player-check.json`, the receipt; and the screenshots the receipt names. The round reads the screenshots' names from the receipt and states none of its own, on the rule of one source [S7]: a screenshot the receipt lists is replaced by the next preparation, and an image it does not list is an entry a preparation does not write. The player check states a screenshot's name once, where it reads a player, removes a file of that name there, so that no receipt lists a file an earlier check left, and lists in its receipt each screenshot it wrote, with its SHA-256, also when a read throws, meets its deadline or is asked to end by SIGTERM, the signal the engine's own `tools.run` sends: the three end a read in one place and close it the same way, the receipt names the signal, and the process then ends by it (`player-read.mjs`, `player-check.mjs`). Such a readback leaves no image its receipt does not list, and the round is prepared again by the command alone. An interrupt at a terminal (SIGINT) and a kill by the host end the check with no receipt, and the image it left, which no receipt lists, is refused by name by the next preparation until its author removes it. The interrupt is left to the browser's driver, which registers a listener of its own at the launch, kills its browser and exits the process with status 130 [S8]: taking it from the driver (`handleSIGINT: false`) would open about a second, between the launch and the arming of the check's own end, in which an interrupt ends the process by default action and can leave the browser running, since the driver starts it detached in a process group of its own; a file to remove by hand is the smaller fault, and it needs no handover of listeners. A signal that comes before the read begins, while the binding is read and the browser launched, leaves no receipt; no player has been read, so the round holds no image and is prepared again (a delivery's evidence folder keeps the image of an earlier check, which the check removes only when it names that player's screenshot). A failing page refuses the round by page and problem. `round.bind` and its own list go. A film's prompt names the receipt as the readback.
5. **The round keeps the changes note and the dispositions it was prepared with.**
   - **Rests on:** the engine reads both from wherever the caller names and keeps neither; the kit copies them into the round [S2] [S3]. The next round's dispositions answer this round's findings, and a reader of a round has otherwise only the prompts' rendering of both.
   - **Considered:** (a) reading them from the round's directory, which mixes what an author writes with what the command derives; (b) copying what the options name into the round as `changes.md` and `dispositions.json`.
   - **Chosen because:** (b).
   - **Applies to:** `prepare`.
6. **The record derives a review's overall from its scores** (the draft's decision 20, as decided there).
   - **Rests on:** the draft's decision 20.
   - **Applies to:** `review.round record`: the record's overall is the mean of its scores to one decimal as `round` gives it, 99.2 for a mean of 99.25, and an overall the reviewer states is neither required nor compared.
7. **A film declares the severities a review may hold.**
   - **Rests on:** the engine names one severity, `material` [S2]. The films' prompts define three, material, minor and note, and the kit holds the films' rule: a delivery waits while a review holds a finding of any severity but a note, so a misspelt or missing severity waits too [S3]. The owner's acceptance of 13 November named the same line for the editions it covered [S6]. The prompts' scoring bands put a dimension with a minor finding under the films' floor of 95, but the bands are prose the engine does not read.
   - **Considered:** (a) leaving it to the floors and the bands, which holds while every reviewer scores by the bands; (b) the engine refusing minor findings for every film, one film's rule made the engine's; (c) a list of the severities that refuse, which passes a severity nobody declared; (d) a list of the severities a review may hold, with today's rule where a film declares none.
   - **Chosen because:** (d). It states the films' rule as they hold it, needs no vocabulary in the engine, and refuses what it does not know.
   - **Applies to:** `film.py`: `[review] accepted_severities`, a list that does not hold `material`, however it is cased or padded, and names no severity twice; an empty list accepts no finding. `review/record.py` names the findings a review may not hold once. The verifier calls it in place of its own test for `material`, for every film: a film that does not declare the list gets that test as it is. `review.round record` calls it only for a film that declares the list, so a film that declares nothing and has no levels still records a review with a material finding, which the verifier then refuses, as today.

## Where each step of the kit's preparation goes

- Refusing an existing round, copying the note and the dispositions, the binding, the receipt, the listening plan, the measurement and the prompts: `prepare` (decisions 1, 2, 4 and 5).
- The second trace: gone (decision 3).
- The preview delivery: gone for the readback (decision 4); a film that wants a preview folder calls `delivery.folder --destination`.
- One author for each film: the film verifier's Spotlight check, which a film's task runs before the round.
- A placeholder left in the note: gone with the kit's filler.
- Filling the note's measured lines, and writing the dispositions: the author.
- The rule on severities after the reviews: `review.round record` and the verifier (decision 7).

## Left in the draft

- **The check of evidence a changes note cites** (the draft's decision 19). The convention it checks, a subject line the kit's scans write, exists in no record the engine writes; a film built by engine commands cites the engine's records, which their own gates bind. The check is admitted with the first scan that stays outside the engine after the still scan has joined it, or not at all.
- **The measured lines of a changes note** (the draft's decision 17, option (c)). With the round's records derived by the command that writes the prompts, a note can state what changed and cite a record for each figure.
- **Dispositions written from reverification lines** (the draft's second open question).
- **A receipt of the film verifier's verdict.** `prepare` cannot know that the build was verified without decoding it again. A film's task orders the verification before the round.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- A film's task or script that calls `prompts` or `bind` stops. → The films' script is already stopped by 0.23.0's rename; the Changelog names the replacement.
- `prepare` starts a browser. → A delivery does the same; a host that cannot run the player check can neither prepare a round nor deliver.
- A preparation that stops after the first files leaves a round without prompts. → Intended: such a round binds nothing, and the next preparation replaces it.
- An author who finds a fault after preparing must set the films aside by a consumer folder before a rebuild. → The engine's rule, unchanged; `delivery.folder --destination` makes such a folder.
- A film whose rule accepts notes only must declare it, or minor findings pass where the kit refused them. → The adopting films' declarations are part of their adoption; the default keeps every other film's verdicts.

## Migration

A film replaces its preparation script by one task, changes the sentence of its prompt that names the readback, and declares `accepted_severities` if its rule accepts fewer findings than the engine's. Nothing a film has released changes.

## Open Questions

- Where a previous round's reviews outlive a checkout. `--previous` names a directory, and a lane's first round names the released edition's last round, which lies in the ignored evidence of the checkout that made it (the trial's finding 15 [S1]). The release publishes each edition's review record and report as assets; whether a previous round can be read from them is not examined here, since each film's last round still lies on the host that builds its next edition.
- Whether the player check writes its receipt after each screenshot is written, and not once when it closes. That would cover an interrupt and a kill with no signal handling at all, since the receipt on disk would always list what stands beside it. It changes how the read hands its screenshots to the check, and is left for a later Change.
