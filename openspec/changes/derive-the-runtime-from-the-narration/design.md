## Context

This draft was read against the engine at 0.27.0 (`dev` `6d59ad6`). It is to be rebased onto the head of the 0.28.0 lane, `work/20261122-hold-a-take-to-its-words`, before it is built: that lane changes 39 source files, among them most of the files this design names (`rendering.py`, `packaging.py`, `verification.py`, `player.py`, `still_scan.py`, `documents.py`, `production.py`, `produce.py`, `draft.py`, `equivalence.py`, `derivation.py`, `preflight.py`, `player-check.mjs`, `native-canvas.mjs`). A search of its diff found no edit to the runtime's arithmetic, but its line numbers move; task 1.1 reads the readers again on that head.

An edition's runtime has one source today, and it is not the narration. `filmkit.specification` reads `duration` from both editions' scripts when it is imported, refuses scripts that declare different values, and exports one `DURATION` for the film (`specification.py:15-22,34`); it reads `credits_duration` the same way (`:37`). The assembly fills that number: `allocate` takes the runtime before the credits, subtracts the clips' speech with their designed thought pauses, the designed gaps and the closing quiet, and shares what is left among the opening, paragraph and chapter gaps by weight within their limits, or refuses a narration that runs over or leaves more than the limits take (`assembly.py:379-392`, `pauses.py:434-480`). The timeline is written as the script with timed segments, so it carries the script's `duration` and `credits_duration` (`assembly.py:611-616`); every film's composition places its credits at `film.duration - film.credits_duration` from it [S4]. Sixteen Python modules read the runtime directly, through `DURATION` or the timeline's `duration`; three more (the dry run, the convergence's count and the convergence) judge a narration against it, and the pre-render check reaches it through the stage and geometry checks it asks. Eleven Node modules read it from the timeline, the stage or the player binding. `research.md` lists each with its lines.

The films session decided on 23 November 2026 that each December edition lasts as long as its settled narration at its designed pauses, rounded up to the whole second, counted by the engine's own count [S1], and then answered and ruled on this draft's questions [S5]. The two editions of one film then differ: by the decision's planning figures the full film's Chinese edition takes about 1636 s and its English edition about 1476 s. The proposal states what changes; this design records why each part takes the form it does.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- One source for an edition's runtime: its narration, counted by the assembly, in whole seconds.
- One place where the runtime and the credits as laid out are recorded, the timeline, and one way every step reads them.
- No thought pause, closing quiet, take or text changed, and no gap outside its range, to reach a runtime.

**Non-Goals:**

- A ceiling or a target for a runtime (decision 4).
- Rebuilding a released edition under this engine.
- Changing the pause design, the pause policies or the films' texts.
- Moving the author, the frame rate and the chapter count out of the scripts (decision 10).

## Decisions

Each decision states what it rests on, what was considered, why the option taken was chosen, where it applies and what would reopen it.

1. **The runtime is counted where the gaps are allocated.**
   - **Rests on:** `assembly.allocate` is the one place that holds, in samples, the clips' speech with their thought pauses, the designed gaps and the closing quiet (`assembly.py:379-392`). `produce fit` and the convergence's count call it (`fit.py:142-155`, `fitting.py:175-188`). The decision names the engine's own count [S1].
   - **Considered:** (a) in `allocate`, from what it already holds; (b) in the convergence, passed to the assembly as a number; (c) a command that writes the runtime into the scripts.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). The dry run, the convergence and the assembly cannot then give different runtimes for the same recordings and policy, as they cannot give different gaps today. (b) makes the assembly depend on an earlier run; (c) is a second source and a step by hand.
   - **Applies to:** `soundtrack/assembly.py`: `allocate`, `Allocation`, `_lay_out`, `_receipt` and the timeline that `assemble` writes.
   - **Reopened when:** the assembly's count changes its steps; the runtime follows, since it is that count.
2. **The runtime is always whole seconds; the fraction goes to the gaps within their ranges, then to the credits.**
   - **Rests on:** the decision: the whole second, rounded up, and never a pause outside its declared range [S1]. The films session's ruling, which replaces its earlier answer that an unrounded count would end on a whole frame: the runtime is always a whole number of seconds, stored as an integer; the fraction goes first to the gaps by their weights, each capped at its range's top, an opening or chapter gap at its declared limit; what the gaps cannot take goes to the credits, which are no pause [S5]. Today `allocate_designed_gaps` caps each gap at `designed_pause_limits` (`pauses.py:434-480`), and a paragraph gap's limit may lie above the top of its `paragraph_pause_range` (`policy.py:190-196`), so a paragraph gap can end above its range; no released edition's paragraph gap does, and their paragraph gaps alone stay at least 2.4 s below their ranges' tops [S3]. Every reader of the runtime multiplies or formats an integer: frames `DURATION * FPS`, samples `DURATION * RATE`, `divmod(DURATION, 60)` with `:02d`, `DURATION // 60`, `range(0, DURATION * FPS, …)`, `--duration type=int` [S2]. An odd number of seconds ends the picture on half a keyframe group, 60 of 120 frames; the renderer's span plan takes a short last group (`native-canvas.mjs:71-79`), and the keyframe checks take a grid that ends early (`rendering.py:491-494`, `packaging.py:403-406`).
   - **Considered:** (a) the fraction to the gaps within `designed_pause_limits`, as free time is shared today; (b) to the gaps capped at their ranges' tops, then the rest to the credits, the runtime whole; (c) to the gaps capped at their ranges' tops, or none, the runtime then unrounded or ending on a whole frame, the films session's earlier answer; (d) to the closing quiet.
   - **Chosen because:** (b), the films session's ruling. (a) can set a paragraph gap above its range. (c) gives a runtime that is not whole seconds, which every reader would have to take in a new form, and an MP4 whose stated length a player check compares exactly. (d) lengthens a pause past its designed value; the credits are not a pause. `allocate_designed_gaps` loses both its refusals: "exceeds the runtime" (`pauses.py:455-457`) cannot occur once the runtime is counted from the narration, and "short by … for the declared gap limits" (`:464-466`) becomes the share that goes to the credits.
   - **Applies to:** `pauses.allocate_designed_gaps` (its caps, its two refusals and the share it returns for the credits) and `assembly.allocate`.
   - **Reopened when:** a film's credits draw something that a fraction of a second added to them would change.
3. **The timeline's `duration` and `credits_duration` carry what was laid out, and one reading serves every step.**
   - **Rests on:** every film's composition reads `film.duration` and `film.credits_duration` from the timeline to place its credits [S4]; the Node audits read `timeline.duration` and `motion-audit.mjs:372` `timeline.credits_duration`; the renderer and the geometry check read the stage's `duration` [S2]. The still scan already reads an edition's runtime from its timeline and refuses one that states none (`still_scan.py:429-438`). The steps that read `DURATION` are called for one edition, but not every function holds it: `produce.arguments()` binds `--duration`'s default before `--language` is parsed (`produce.py:167`), `player.runtime_label()` and `packaging.keyed_as_declared(rendition, keyframes)` take no edition, `prompts.prompt_context` fills fields both editions share, and `packaging.BOUND_FILM` is a module constant.
   - **Considered:** where to record them: (a) the timeline; (b) a record of their own; (c) the assembly receipt alone. How a step reads the runtime: (d) one reading beside the edition's places in `production.Edition`, refusing in one line, naming `produce assemble`, an edition that has no timeline or whose timeline states no whole number of seconds; (e) a function of the assembly.
   - **Chosen because:** (a) and (d). The timeline is what the compositions, the stage and the audits read already, so no film's picture changes. The receipt states the same values because the same step writes both; it is the assembly's record, not a second source. (b) is one more file to bind. Every reader depends on `production` already and none on the assembly, whose imports a delivery step does not need. In the timeline, `credits_duration` is the credits as laid out, the declared length plus any share of the fraction; the declaration's value is only the assembly's input. Every reader of the credits' start, the assembly's last segment and the verifier's caption bound (`verification.py:87`) among them, takes the timeline's. The five functions above change their signatures to take the edition or its runtime; the still scan's own reading moves to the edition.
   - **Applies to:** `production.Edition`, `picture/still_scan.film_frames` and every reader in `research.md`.
   - **Reopened when:** a step needs an edition's runtime before its assembly; none does today, since every picture step binds the assembled timeline (`rendering.py:84-114`).
4. **A script declares no `duration`: neither a copy nor a ceiling.**
   - **Rests on:** the owner's C-2, one source for a fact, and the owner's rule of 20 November 2026 that a check refusing a disagreement after the fact is not a source. The decision records that no film text states a runtime and that the owner has decided none [S1]. Its follow-up called the script's `duration` "a stated check, not the source"; the decision's owner answered on 23 November 2026 that it is removed, and that a script still declaring it is refused with one line that says to remove it [S5].
   - **Considered:** (a) removed, and refused where a script still declares it; (b) kept, and required to equal the derived runtime; (c) kept as a ceiling the edition may not exceed; (d) two modes, a declared runtime fitted as today or none and derived; (e) no engine change, with a person copying `produce fit`'s figure into both scripts at each build. `research.md` compares their costs.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). (b) is a second copy that a person must rewrite after every retake and that only repeats the count. (c) states a limit that nobody has set and brings back the miss, whose one lever, a shorter thought pause, the decision forbids for a runtime. (d) keeps two paths through every reader, with the lever and the reserve, to rebuild released editions that keep their own engine anyway (decision 7). (e) is the step by hand that the follow-up removes. The decision's owner chose (a) [S5].
   - **Applies to:** `specification.py`, the sample film's scripts and a film that adopts.
   - **Reopened when:** the owner sets a limit on a film's length. It is then one added requirement that refuses an edition over the limit and names no pause to shorten.
5. **Each edition keeps its own runtime, also where a page or a check covers both.**
   - **Rests on:** the decision's figures put the full film's two editions about 2 min 40 s apart [S1]. Today the entry page fills both `/*RUNTIME*/` anchors with one label (`packaging.py:160`), the player binding names one `duration` for the film (`packaging.py:102`, `folder.py:339,359`), which the player check applies to both editions (`player-read.mjs:377,441-444`), and a review prompt's `{duration}` comes from fields both prompts share (`prompts.py:316`), although each prompt is filled from its own edition's fields (`prompts.py:579-583`). Each film's entry page holds one `/*RUNTIME*/` inside each language link, and each film's review prompt says "Each edition: exactly {duration} s" and asks the reviewer to compare with the other edition [S4]. On `dev` the three films' tasks assemble both editions before either is packaged (`.config/mise/config.toml:59-65`, `:96-100`) [S6].
   - **Considered:** (a) each edition's own runtime everywhere, the entry page filling the anchor inside each language link with that link's edition, and each prompt stating its own edition's; (b) one runtime for the film, the longer edition's, the shorter one padded with quiet; (c) a prompt field that names both editions' runtimes.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). (b) puts silence into a film to make two numbers agree, which the decision rules out. Filled with its own edition's runtime, "Each edition: exactly {duration} s" would tell the Chinese reviewer that both editions last 1636 s; the sample's template says "This edition: exactly {duration} s", and a film rewords its own when it adopts. (c) adds a field for a fact each reviewer reads from the other edition's films. The entry page needs both editions' timelines, so `package-metadata` refuses, naming the other edition, while that edition has none.
   - **Applies to:** `delivery/packaging.py`, `delivery/player.py`, `delivery/folder.py`, `player-check.mjs`, `player-read.mjs`, `review/prompts.py` and the sample film's `content/review/prompt.md`.
   - **Reopened when:** a film is to be packaged one edition at a time.
6. **The dry run and the convergence report the runtime; the miss, its lever and the reserve go.**
   - **Rests on:** with the runtime derived, no count can exceed it, so the convergence's miss (`fitting.py:183-185`) cannot occur and `thought_pause_to_fit` (`fit.py:218-283`) has nothing to close; its one use would be to shorten thought pauses to meet a number, which the decision forbids [S1]. No cast of the three films declares `runtime_fit` [S3].
   - **Considered:** (a) keep the record's `fit` and the verdict `fits`, add `runtime_seconds` and the credits' share, keep `free_seconds` for the gaps' share, drop `misses`, the lever and the reserve, and refuse a cast that still declares `runtime_fit`; (b) a new record key `runtime` with a verdict `counted`; (c) leave the lever and the reserve in place, unused.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). `fits` stays true: the allocation still fits the narration's gaps and credits into a runtime and accepts them. `produce fit` keeps its name, which the films' tasks call and `filmkit.adoption` checks against the engine's actions; the modules keep theirs with it. (b) renames what the convergence's requirements, the README and the films' records call `fit`, without changing what it states. (c) leaves code without a caller and a declaration that silently does nothing; the engine refuses a declaration it does not read, as it refuses a variant's field that the pause policy does not read.
   - **Applies to:** `soundtrack/fit.py`, `narration/fitting.py`, `narration/converge.py` and `produce.py`.
   - **Reopened when:** a ceiling is added (decision 4); a miss then returns, named without a thought pause.
7. **No mode keeps a declared runtime; released editions keep their own engine.**
   - **Rests on:** the three films pin `v0.22.3` [S4], and the engine's changelog for 0.26.0 says that released editions keep their tag's engine and are not rebuilt [S2]. The repository's entry file says that an engine change keeps every existing film's output bytes unless its release says otherwise. Each released edition's assembly receipt gives the seconds its narration left beyond the designed gaps: the DDWG film 15.10 s in Chinese and 1.18 s in English, the essentials film 0.82 s and 0.76 s, the ETHOS film 0.22 s and 1.00 s [S3].
   - **Considered:** (a) one rule, marked breaking, under which a released edition rebuilt by this engine would take its narration's runtime; (b) a compatibility mode for released editions (decision 4 (d)).
   - **Chosen because:** (a). The decision makes one rule for every edition [S1], and no released edition is to be rebuilt by this engine. Counted by this Change's rule from their receipts, three would change length: the DDWG film's Chinese edition from 1500 to 1485 s, its English edition to 1499 s and the ETHOS film's English edition to 899 s; the other three keep 600 and 900 s. For those three, this Change neither alters the total their allocation receives nor binds a new cap, since none of their paragraph gaps reaches its range's top; whether the engine releases since `v0.22.3` would keep their bytes is not this draft's question [S3]. Every edition's fraction would fit within its gaps' ranges, so none would put a share in its credits.
   - **Applies to:** the release's changelog entry.
   - **Reopened when:** a released edition must be rebuilt by this engine.
8. **Before its narration is assembled an edition has no runtime and no picture.**
   - **Rests on:** staging, the draft and every picture step bind the assembled timeline, receipt and soundtrack (`rendering.py:84-114`), and `produce fit` gives the runtime from the selected recordings before the assembly.
   - **Considered:** (a) no runtime until `assemble`, with `produce fit` telling it in advance; (b) a provisional runtime from the scripts for drafts.
   - **Chosen because:** (a). No picture exists without a timeline today, so (b) would serve no step. The sample film follows the same rule: its scripts lose `"duration": 60`, and it lasts as long as its narration. A test builds the timeline it needs, with its `duration`, where it patched a module constant.
   - **Applies to:** the sample film, the tests and `produce stage-picture`, whose `--duration` defaults to the edition's runtime and may not exceed it.
   - **Reopened when:** a step is found that needs a runtime before the assembly.
9. **The transcript's byline keeps whole minutes, rounded down.**
   - **Rests on:** the byline states `DURATION // 60` minutes (`captions/documents.py:151-153`), exact for 1500, 600 and 900 s; an edition of 1636 s (27:16) reads "27 分钟" and one of 1476 s (24:36) "24 min". The decision's owner answered on 23 November 2026 that the rule stays [S5].
   - **Considered:** (a) whole minutes, rounded down, from the timeline's runtime; (b) `mm:ss`, as the player page shows it.
   - **Chosen because:** (a), the owner's answer; it never states more than the film holds.
   - **Applies to:** `captions/documents.py`, which reads the timeline's `duration` in place of `DURATION`.
   - **Reopened when:** the owner asks for another form.
10. **The film's declaration owns the credits' length; the scripts' other film-wide values are deferred.**
    - **Rests on:** both editions render one composition (`preflight.py:43`), which draws the credits for both. Today each script states `credits_duration` and `specification.declared` holds the two equal by a refusal (`specification.py:15-22,37`): two hand-kept copies of one fact, which C-2 and the rule of 20 November 2026 do not allow, a check that compares them included. The film's declaration (`film.py:561`, `content/film.toml`) is where the engine takes every other film-wide value. The scripts' `author`, `fps` and chapter count are held equal the same way.
    - **Considered:** (a) the declaration states `credits_duration` once, and a script that states it is refused; (b) each script owns its edition's credits, with no equality; (c) the two copies as today.
    - **Chosen because:** (a). The credits are the film's, drawn by one composition for both editions, so one statement serves both; (b) would let two numbers for one design drift apart without a check. The timeline then states the credits as laid out (decision 3), and the declaration's value is the assembly's input. The author, the frame rate and the chapter count are of the same kind and are not moved here, to keep this Change to the runtime: their owner is the engine's maintainer, the next action is a Change that moves them to the declaration as this one moves the credits, and its review date is 30 November 2026. The film-engine delta keeps their existing rule as it stands and writes no new copy held equal.
    - **Applies to:** `film.py`, `specification.py`, `adoption.py`, the sample film's declaration and scripts, and a film that adopts. The declaration's reader refuses a declaration that omits `credits_duration` in one line that says to declare it there, in the scripts' style, and loads no film from it: the credits are never defaulted, and the declaration's other problems keep their words (the films session's ruling of 23 November 2026).
    - **Reopened when:** a film needs its editions' credits to differ in length.
11. **`filmkit.adoption` names the declarations this release removes.**
    - **Rests on:** `filmkit.adoption` is "what this engine release asks of a film's tracked files, listed at once" (`adoption.py:1`), and reads no script, cast or review prompt today (`adoption.py:46-57`). Without a question of its own, a film learns of `duration` and `credits_duration` only when its first engine command refuses at import, of `runtime_fit` only when it runs the convergence, and of a review prompt that says "Each edition: exactly {duration} s" not at all: each prompt then tells its reviewer that both editions last its own edition's runtime (decision 5).
    - **Considered:** (a) a question that lists each script declaring `duration` or `credits_duration`, each cast declaring `runtime_fit` and each line of the review prompt that cites the runtime's field without saying "This edition"; (b) the refusals at load and at the convergence alone; (c) renaming the prompt's field, so that an old template fails to format.
    - **Chosen because:** (a): a film sees every item of its adoption in one run, as the command promises. The question reads the scripts, casts and prompt as text and needs no declaration that loads. A declaration without `credits_duration` is one fact with one finding, which the existing `declaration` question gives (the films session's ruling of 23 November 2026). The prompt's reading fails closed: a line that cites the field and does not say "This edition" is named whatever else it says, so a film may reword a line that was already true, and no line that ties the runtime to both editions passes. (c) would refuse the film's review instead of telling its adoption (the films session's ruling of 23 November 2026).
    - **Applies to:** `adoption.py` and the `engine-distribution` delta.
    - **Reopened when:** the refusals at load change.

The specs remove several requirements and add them under new names, where `RENAMED` and `MODIFIED` would keep their history. Each of them loses a scenario that can no longer occur, and OpenSpec 2.1.1 refuses a `MODIFIED` block that drops one, so a rename with a change to its scenarios is written as a removal and an addition, as this repository's archived Changes do.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [A runtime of an odd number of seconds ends the picture on half a keyframe group, which no film has had] → Read in code only (decision 2). Task 1.5's packaging test takes a runtime of 1477 s.
- [The credits take what the gaps cannot] → They lengthen by less than one second, and only where every weighted gap stands at its range's top; no released edition would reach that (decision 7).
- [Capping paragraph gaps at their range's top changes how free time is shared] → It binds only where a paragraph gap would pass its range's top, which no released edition's does; only the derived runtime's fraction is shared now.
- [`package-metadata` needs the other edition's timeline] → It refuses in one line naming the edition to assemble first.
- [A retake of one edition re-packages the other] → The entry page is shared and states both runtimes, so one edition's retake rewrites it, and the other edition's viewing package no longer matches it (`verification.py:275`) until that edition's `package-metadata` runs again. The check exists already, and `ddwg-film`'s package task packages both editions in one run (`.config/mise/config.toml:94-100`); the other two films' tasks were read only by the reader [S6].
- [The runtime moves with every retake] → Every step reads it from the timeline, whose hash already binds the stage, the package and the review, so a retake makes them stale as it does today.
- [A film keeps `duration` or `credits_duration` in a script after adopting] → `filmkit.adoption` lists it, and the engine refuses at load with one line that says to remove it.
- [The timeline's `credits_duration` becomes seconds with a fraction, where it held the script's integer] → Accepted by the films session; the changelog states it among the breaking changes, with what a reader of the timeline does.
- [The transcript's byline understates by up to 59 s] → Kept by the owner's answer (decision 9).
- [A released edition rebuilt by this engine changes length] → Decision 7; the release says so.

## Migration Plan

- The base: the draft was read at 0.27.0; before it is built it is rebased onto the 0.28.0 lane's head, and task 1.1 reads `research.md`'s readers again there.
- The engine: one release after 0.28.0. Its changelog names each breaking change and what a film does about it.
- A film that adopts, in its own December lane (the films session's): removes `duration` and `credits_duration` from both scripts and declares `credits_duration` once in `content/film.toml`; removes `runtime_fit` from a cast that declares it; rewords "Each edition: exactly {duration} s" in its review prompt to "This edition"; runs `filmkit.adoption`, then `select`, `assemble` and the rest of its build. Every later record is made again, since the scripts' hashes change.
- The specs: the Purpose of `runtime-fit` ("how much of the film's runtime its narration leaves for the designed gaps") is reworded when the Change is archived, because a delta carries requirements and not a Purpose.
- Rollback: before a film adopts, the engine's release is reverted. A film that adopted pins its former tag again and restores its scripts and declaration from Git.
- Two drafts lose their purpose, and this Change edits neither [S5]:
  - `let-narration-converge`, decision 3, "The selection is made over the whole edition and fits the runtime": no fixed runtime remains for a selection to fit.
  - `faster-films-less-by-hand`, decision 8, "The narration's fit to the runtime is solved by a narration that converges by itself": the runtime now follows the narration, so there is no fit to solve.
